An allograph is a variation of a letter in another context . Uppercase and lowercase letters are allographs . Before the letter U was added to the Latin alphabet , the shape V was an allograph and stood for both the vowel U and the consonant V. It was n’t until printing standardized letter shapes in the 1600s that the letter U became regularly used , but I could n't say why the museum used the shape V in their text . Maybe the architect was a history buff . Interesting Factoid : Many older cities do n't have a J Street because , back then , type had yet to be standardized , and I and J were also allographs .
