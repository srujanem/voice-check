Well it 's no surprise because we tend to be pretty mixed . Our origin comes from Christianity , but a lot of our traditions and practices are based on whatever new members bring to our community . Within Christianity there 's been a * very * old debate about what it takes to get into heaven / be right with God / be saved from sin : do you have to perform correct deeds , or is it enough to believe correct things?^1 From time to time some Christians would think that believing is a form of doing , and that a loving God would not damn any person eternally . Those views were suppressed until the protestant reformation made it possible for some scholars to teach them openly in some countries . And as they believed there was no reason to convert the infidels to save their souls , many also found inspiration in the commonalities between some of their traditions and those of other religions . From there it snowballed into a modern religion with no official doctrines , that is about a community dedicated to “ a free and responsible search for truth and meaning ” . As for myself I was raised Christian , became atheist / agnostic / it 's complicated , then adopted the teachings of Epicurus as a systematic philosophy of life , then since he emphasized having a community I found UU to be the one compatible with my beliefs / views / whatever . In our day - to - day activities we coordinate most frequently with our local Reform Jewish synagogue and United Church of Christ church .
