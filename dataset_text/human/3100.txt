Source : IT at a bank It is all about expense versus convenience . It is FAR more expensive to purchase , and moreso to maintain , an ATM that dispenses multiple denominations of money . So banks have to decide whether they want to spend more to convenience the customer , or spend less . Most national banks opt for the latter , while hometown banks opt for the former . An additional factor banks have to consider , is that having multiple denominations in an ATM also increases the risk factor for malfunctions . Our ATMs distribute 10s and 20s , and we often have to shutdown ATMs for hours because one or the other jams . ATMs that distribute multiple denominations have more moving parts , as there are multiple " tapes " ( plastic containers that hold the money ) that the ATM has to pull from . Another humorous ( read : annoying ) malfunction , is that sometimes the company that refills the money ( most of the time this is n't the bank ) will put the tapes in backwards , which causes more problems . Most ATMs have a failsafe so it wo n't distribute the wrong denominations , but sometimes the ATM does n't catch it , and someone withdraws $ 20 , and gets a $ 10 instead . tl;dr ATMs can distribute any number of different denominations , but banks have to decide if they want to spend more money to provide this convenience .
