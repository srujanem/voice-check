3D glasses work by presenting each of your eyes with a different image . Modern 3D glasses do this by being polarized . Specific , they use [ circular polarization ] ( URL_0 ) , which has only two directions , clockwise and counterclockwise . A polarized lens will only let through light that is polarized in the same direction . So to make the 3D effect work , one lens is clockwise - polarized , and one is counterclockwise - polarized . That lets them project two overlapping imaged on the screen at once , with the light for each image polarized to match the lens that its intended to be visible through . This means that the light for the image intended for your right eye will pass through the right lens , but be blocked by the left lens , and vice versa for the other eye . Ambient light is not polarized , but rather consists of lots of different photons that are all vibrating in many different ways . Some of this light will be blocked by the lenses in your glasses , and some will pass through them . The light that passes through a polarized lens will be only the light that matches the lens 's polarization , meaning that , when light passes through a polarized lens , the light coming out the other side only consists of light vibrating in the same direction : i.e. * it 's polarized * . So when you look in the mirror and close one eye , the light bouncing off your face on the closed - eye side passes through the lens on that side and becomes polarized . It then bounces off the mirror and toward your open eye , which is behind a lens that is polarized opposite from the opposite lens , and opposite from the light coming through that lens . And thus the light passing through the opposite lens is blocked , and it looks black .
