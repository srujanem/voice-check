There 's quite a lot of writing in the old format -- several millenia of classic literature , history , and philosophy . As long as that writing system is still in common use , all of those books are relatively accessible to the population at large . If you change writing systems , suddenly you have a cultural divide between the present and the past . China does not want a culture divide between the present and the past . Its ability to function as a country is dependent on people perceiving themselves as part of the gigantic country of China . Part of that identity is continuity with the thousands of years of culture . The Chinese government implicitly takes legitimacy by association with the traditions that preceded it ( notwithstanding actual resemblance to those traditions ) . Breaking off from history may lead to a new cultural identity , and potentially one that is fragmented or locally - based rather than nationally unified . That is something the Chinese government wants to avoid .
