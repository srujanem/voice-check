That is not characteristic of the Matroska file format . The mkv format is just a bowl for the oatmeal . What the oatmeal contains ( the quality of it , how filling it is , etc ) is completely up to what is put inside of it . There is a certain way to prepare the oatmeal that makes it very tastey and filling , but without requiring very much of it . That is the " codec " - or the " language " that the video file speaks . h.264 does an excellent job of storing high quality video into tiny amounts of space . Abandoning the oatmeal analogy , the codec does this by taking a ' picture ' every so many frames , and then tracking which pixels move / change from that picture . ( instead of storing a completely new picture for each frame )
