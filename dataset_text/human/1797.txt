We 're not really sure why . Human languages like to classify nouns : a lot of languages end up with some combination of masculine / feminine / neuter gender , but others have animate / inanimate gender , and there are a few languages that have over a dozen genders , loosely based on the " shape " of the object being referred to . We do n't know what the first languages look like , and we do n't know how gender first appeared . Our best methods for finding out what languages were like in the past only lets us go back 6,000 - 10,000 years at most . There have been examples of gender disappearing from a language ( eg , English , Persian ) , but I know of no languages that did not have gender and then obtained it , so there 's really nothing to go on for how it might enter a language .
