For Curiosity and the other Mars robots , they use two different types of antennas . A low - gain one . This is like the antenna on your phone or in your laptop for wifi -- it broadcasts and listens for radio from everywhere , but that means it 's signal does n't go very far . It looks like a stick , not too different from the antenna on a car . It can receive data straight from a powerful transmitter on earth with a dish to focus it , but it usually just talks to an orbiting satellite around Mars , and that relays the messages back to Earth with a high - powered , directional , high - gain antenna . Here is a picture of an earth - based high - gain dish antenna : URL_2 The dish focuses the radio waves coming from a very narrow beam onto a receiver . It points directly at mars to pick up the weak signals from the Rover or the relay satellite . It also has a steerable dish high - gain antenna , but that takes more power and requires the rover to be stable and have a clear line of sight to earth .
