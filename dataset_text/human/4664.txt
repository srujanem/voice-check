Short selling works like this : 1 . The person shorting a stock / commodity borrows it from a lender for a fixed time period at a nominal fee . 2 . The seller then sells off the stock / commodity 3 . Later , the seller repurchases the stock / commodity and returns the borrowed amount to the lender . If the stock / commodity drops in price in between steps 2 and 3 , and the drop is greater than the fee paid for borrowing , the shorter makes money . For example : Bob owns 100 units of Chemical X , worth $ 100 each . John borrows those 100 shares for 1 month , paying Bob $ 10 per share as a lending fee , and sells them for $ 10,000 . Over the next month , the price of Chemical X drops to $ 50 per unit . John buys back the 100 units at $ 5,000 and returns them to Bob . John has made $ 4,000 by selling for $ 10,000 , buying for $ 5,000 , and paying $ 1,000 in fees . Bob has made $ 1,000 in fees , but his Chemical X is not worth as much . The reason this works is typically stocks / commodities rise in value , not fall , so Bob sees this is a safe investment to make as he expects to make the $ 1,000 while still having $ 10,000 in Chemical X. John is betting Chemical X will fall ; if it does n't fall , then he is out the $ 1,000 plus whatever change in cost there is . It can get much more complicated , but that 's the simple form .
