The areas between each rib are known as intercostal spaces and within these spaces are both interior and external intercostal muscles . Others have explained how they are connected to the vertebrae in the back and through hyaline cartilage at the sternum . Our ribs have a natural downward slope to them . The reason for this is so that when our external intercostal muscles contract with inspiration ( breathing in ) , the muscles physically raise the ribs to increase the size of the thoracic cavity by essentially just repositioning the ribs . This is possible because the connective tissue on the sternal end of the rib is hyaline cartilage which possesses elastic tendencies . The rib is not physically expanding , it is just being repositioned with help from the intercostal muscles , connective cartilage , and diaphragm .
