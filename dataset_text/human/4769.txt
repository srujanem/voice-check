A lot of these responses are missing one of the big factors . Companies often own their own mail servers , which means they can do stuff like use any string as an account name , regardless of if anybody is actually registered to that account . For example , let 's say Bob owns his own mail server , at URL_0 . His email address is bob@ URL_0 . If you send that address an email , he sees it in his inbox . Today , Bob decides to send an email from Alice@ URL_0 . Nobody is connected to that email address . If someone were to send an email there , it would n't go to anybody . A lot of times , email servers will send an error email to the server admin if an email is received to a " fake " account - people can do this with malicious intent , like to fill up the mail server 's storage . By specifying not to reply to those addresses , admins can cut down on the number of error emails , and focus more on the ones likely to be malicious .
