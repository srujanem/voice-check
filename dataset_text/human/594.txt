Strip down flashy graphics and animations , and most sports games are relatively simple exercises . The computer has almost instantaneous reflexes and can think and act quicker than your input can be translated into the game world . What 's more , it can " cheat " if it has to since it knows your inputs as well as the exact limits of what 's possible in the game environment . Always losing a game would be no fun , so there needs to be some way to introduce mistakes into the computer 's decision making . This is done by limiting the range of decisions , using a preset pattern that does n't take user behavior into account or can be easily deduced by the user , slowing down the computer reaction time , or increasing the chance that the computer does not take the recommended course of action . The frequency and severity of these computer " mistakes " can be varied , creating different difficulty settings . Strategy games like chess where the number of outcomes is much harder to calculate operate differently .
