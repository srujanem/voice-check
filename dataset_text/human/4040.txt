CRT TVs have a device called a flyback transformer , which used a moving magnetic field to trade current for voltage in order to have a high enough voltage to drag the electron beam back to the left - hand side of the screen . The process invariably caused some components to vibrate in response to the magnetic field . In standard - definition TVs the flyback transformer operated at around 15.5 kHz ( no exactly that frequency , and somewhat dependent on whether you were on the US standard ( NTSC ) or one of the European standards ( PAL or SECAM ) ) . In high - definition CRT TVs , as well as in CRT computer monitors , the flyback trasnsformer 's frequency is well out of human hearing range , typically somewhere north of 30 kHz , with higher resolutions calling for higher frequencies . In plasma and LCD TVs , there is no flyback transformer . I can hear this 15 kHz frequency even now that I am in my 40s , and I will tell you that it was a godsend to get a high - definition TV around 2001 or so ; even though it was still a CRT , the whistle was inaudible . Othere electronic devices may have other things going on involving oscillators in the same frequency range , and driving either magnetic or capacitive components , the result still being that something vibrates physically in response to it .
