Think of it this way : technically , hydrophobics do n't repel water . Rather , water prefers sticking to itself so much that it avoids non - polar chemicals . A single molecule of water in oil does n't avoid the oil at all . But add two molecules of water , and put them near each other , and they 'll want to find each other and stick together . Put enough water there , and they 'll bunch together so well that they 'll push the oil away . So , no . You would not be covered by a layer of air , or anything else of the sort ( maybe a few bubbles , but certainly not a layer ) . When you got back out , the water would continue it 's plan to stick to itself . Mostly , it would be in drops big enough to roll off of you . Some smaller droplets would remain .
