I 'm assuming you know what the Monty Hall problem is already , but for those who do n't know and do n't want to look it up , it 's essentially Deal or No Deal . Howie Mendel shows you 3 cases . 2 of the cases are for $ 1 , but the third is worth $ 1 000 000 . He asks you to pick a case . He then opens one of the cases that contains $ 1 . Now he asks you if you want to switch cases . You know that between the 2 remaining cases , one of them contains $ 1 , and the other $ 1000000 . Do you switch ? As you say , the advantage is that now you have a 50 % chance of getting it right if you switch , as oppose to 33 % if you keep the case , so you should switch cases . At the beginning , you had a 1/3 chance of picking the right case right ? Imagine you had an imaginary 4th case that you are switching out for one of the 3 real cases . No matter which case you pick , there is now a 33 % chance that the case you picked contains $ 1000000 , and a 67 % it contains $ 1 . After Howie removes one of the bad cases , the case you own * * still * * has only a 33 % chance of containing $ 1000000 . But the other case now has a 50 % chance of containing $ 1000000 . So you should switch . If you already knew this explanation , and wanted a non - statistics reason , I 'm afraid there really is n't one , that i 've heard at least . It 's why this method is used so much in game shows . It sounds exciting when really it is n't .
