In the scientific world , a law describes * * what * * happens , a theory attempts to formalise * * how * * or * * why * * it happens . So for example we have a Law of Gravity . Simple observation shows that objects always fall directly downwards . Newton 's Theory of Gravity attempts to put that on a mathematical basis , by saying that the objects are actually falling towards the centre of the earth ; furthermore he ( in a stroke of genius ) determines that the same force which pulls apples downwards from trees also governs the orbit of celestial objects , such as the earth around the sun . Einstein 's Theory of General Relativity goes even further and tries to explain the observed Law of Gravity in terms of the bending of space - time . So to answer the second part of your question , " When does a theory become a law ? " -- it does n't work like that in science . The law usually comes first , and then theories are later developed to try to explain the law .
