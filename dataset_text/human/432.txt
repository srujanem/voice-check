It actually depends on how the law was changed . If changed by legislation ( government decides to change the law ) the issue of people already convicted will likely be addressed in the legislation . If the legislation is silent ( this is a very rare ) the black letter is that you were convicted and are serving your time BUT there could well be a constitutional challenge of that depending on the constitution applicable in your jurisdiction . If the law was changed by a judge things are different . For example if your country 's highest court said that the law you were convicted under is unconstitutional it is going to depend on how your country 's legal system interprets those kind of judicial decisions . I understand that there are some countries where this kind of decision is treated as changing the law from the day the order is made . However a lot of countries take the position that unconstitutional laws were always unconstitutional and it has just taken a while for their invalidity to be discovered . Thus you could make a quick court application and expect your conviction to be overturned . edit for grammar
