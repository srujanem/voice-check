As a previous comment mentioned , quoting the DSMV : depression becomes classified as a mental illness when you identify with 5 of the criteria for a consistent and extended period of time . However , that 's not really an ELI5 answer , but in this TED talk ( URL_0 ) Andrew Solomon discusses depression in a way that I think anybody can relate to . In specific relation to this question , he says that Depression can not simply be described as " sadness " , but rather , " much too much sadness , at far too slight a cause " . He also brings up situations that sometimes get incorrectly labelled as " depression " . For instance , if a loved one passes away , and you feel deep sadness for several weeks , it may be hard to separate that feeling from the experience of depression , but if you can manage to function normally after a few weeks ( and by that I mean , you can get out of bed , you can take care of yourself , attend to your daily activities , not that you suddenly feel perfectly contented ) , then it 's likely a period of grief that you 're experiencing , and it will ultimately resolve itself . However , if you experience a loss and find yourself utterly in despair for months and can not manage to function at all then it 's more likely that the event triggered a depression and that you should seek treatment to resolve it , because it may not resolve itself normatively . I know this is n't exactly the most crystal clear difference , but the point is that the difference between sadness and depression is not necessarily the depth of the feeling . Some people find themselves very sad at times , but as a reaction to equally dismal experiences , but that is not mental illness . The label , " Mental illness " belongs to the inability for the mind to resolve the sadness , not the experience of the sadness itself .
