Whitening strips are actual small pieces of polyethylene , a flexible plastic that molds to the top surfaces , around the edges and into crevices between teeth . Each strip is coated with a gel treatment made from hydrogen peroxide or carbamide peroxide that reacts with moisture in the mouth and breaks down , or oxidizes , getting into the enamel of teeth where stains settle . Part of the oxidization is the peroxide , which acts to whiten , while urea , an acid that comes out during the reaction , acts to break apart some of the stain bonds , allowing the whitening to disperse .
