First , it clears out a lot of dead and about to be dead trees , bushes , etc . that are n't doing anything but sitting there , waiting to become fuel for a summer wildfire . If you burn some of that stuff out when the weather is such that you can control it , it 's much less of a problem than if it contributes to an uncontrolled fire in hot weather , when the ashes could float away and set more fires , possibly miles away.(it would surprise you just how hot a piece of ash carried off in the wind can be , and how long it could stay hot ) Second , when that burns down , it leaves ash and charcoal behind . That stuff is carbon , and precipitation will cause that carbon to leech into the soil . This is very good for the soil , it makes the new vegetation grow well . So , not only does it clear away wildfire fuel , it leaves the soil fertile for new plants to grow .
