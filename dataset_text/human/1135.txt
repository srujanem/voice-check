The comments already here are correct , though probably not quite as helpful as you were hoping . Yes , bacteria , molds , and fungus are no inherently bad for you . In fact the majority are completely benign ( meaning they have no discernible effect on you ) while a minority are considered beneficial ( like penicillin as an antibiotic ) or harmful ( like e.coli ) . There are three types of cheeses that use mold ( the benign kind ) . Soft ripe cheeses ( like brie and camembert ) , washed rind cheeses ( like munster and limberger ) and blue cheeses ( which you already know ) . Molds are used because * a ) they have no noticeable effect on the body and * b ) by using a * specific kind of mold * - the " cheese maturation " process can be controlled , meaning the " bad mold " does n't have a chance to grow on the cheese . The cheese molds basically tell the bad molds " fuck off , we already live here - there 's no more room " . The type of mold chosen for the job is * very specific * as it has a big impact on how the cheese will look , feel , and taste at the end of the maturation process . Check out these mold strains ( say on Wikipedia ) for examples : * Penicillium camemberti * Brevibacterium linens * Penicillium roqueforti * Penicillium glaucum There are many more types used , but the above four are quite popular .
