Probably , but there 's no guarantee . Vitamin deficiencies and electrolyte imbalances can be deadly , and depending on other health variables , the fit person may be healthier to start with . Muscle can be used as a last - resort energy source , too , though it does n't provide as much energy as fat and has the obvious problem of reducing strength and damaging organs if it gets to the point of using organ muscle . So in a situation with absolutely no food , electrolyte problems ( not enough sodium / potassium / etc . ) can set in before sheer lack of calories and body wasting is a serious threat . It 's possible to " starve " to death and still be at a high body weight if you lack the vitamins and minerals to stay alive and poor health will speed that along . In a long - term situation where food is scarce but there 's still a little to eat , the fat person would be very likely to outlive the fit one . Humans are pretty well adapted to having little food for awhile and store fat easily to get through those times , but even then it would have been rare that there was * absolutely nothing * to eat at all for extended periods of time , and we 're not as good at dealing with that . Either way , my money is on the fat person living longer , but I would n't bet as much in a completely foodless situation vs. one where they find a little bit here and there .
