Well , you can look at the EU for a smaller scale example of that . Basically you end up with strong economies ( like Germany ) subsidizing weak economies ( like Greece ) via the shared currency . EDIT : thinking about this again , I 'm not sure how it would work with a single world currency . Currently , all countries in the EU gain the same benefit from a relatively weak or strong euro ( e.g. weak euro = competitive exports & economic growth , strong euro = cheap imports , increased purchasing power , etc . ) , but with a single currency there would be no such thing as weak or strong because there is no point of comparison . It would effectively " lock in " countries to the currency system regardless of their economic condition . For example today , if a country is suffering weak growth , they can devalue their currency in order to make their exports cheaper and therefore more competitive globally . This stimulates growth in the economy through increased demands for lower priced goods . With only a single currency , this would not be possible . As mentioned in another comment , there really is no ELI5 , because it is an incredibly complex subject and it has never been tried . You can make general statements about how it would affect certain market dynamics , but no conclusive statements on what it would do on a macro level .
