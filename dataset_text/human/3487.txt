In this context , " china " actually refers to the material it 's made from , and not the place it comes from . China tableware like plates and bowls and cups and such are not necessarily made in China -- they 're made * from * China , which is a fancier name for what we often call porcelain . It 's usually a clean white color , but may be semi - translucent . It also often comes glazed with elaborate designs on it , and these designs may increase the value . Chinaware in general is highly respected because of its traditional association with " The Orient . " Time was , only the very wealthy could afford to own complete sets of china , because it was made almost exclusively in China . Indeed , even long after china ( porcelain ) tableware and vases and decorative pieces began making their way out of China , the exact processes of making fine china were not well - understood outside of the region , so it maintained much of its value . Even today , complete sets of high - quality china can still be quite expensive , despite the fact that other , more easily - made , and less delicate replacements have made their way into common use .
