We live on a planet that is encased in a soupy mix of air . This air is all around us and pressing down on the surface , and against our bodies , with about 14 pounds of force per square inch . In other words , draw a square inch on your arm and put 14 pounds on it and it 's basically like that . So ... this 14 " PSI " ( pounds per square inch ) is an average pressure . However , when you take this soupy mix of air that is all around us , and you start pushing and pulling and stirring it , it create pockets that are sometimes low , and sometimes high . That is , lower than 14 or higher than 14 . So " low pressure " and " high pressure " can only exist when you have a baseline . That said , there are a host of interesting things that come with high and low pressure systems . Stability , instability , cold , heat , wind ... etc . So ... typically , a lot of attention is paid to low and high pressure fronts and patterns when it comes to understanding and predicting weather .
