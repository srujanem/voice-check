AnteChronos has the best reply , but I would like to add that the use of the word " Lucifer " is itself not part of Biblical Christian Theology . Lucifer was undeniably a mistranslation in the KJV that somehow weasled it 's way into mainstream christianity . The word in Hebrew is " helel " , and it appears in only one verse , where it is described as " falling from the sky " . The meaning is literally " light bringer " and it is used to describe the planet Venus , and was also one of the titles claimed by the King of Bablylon . If you interpret it as a divine being , it 's not clear if it 's a name or a title . Even so , " Lucifer " is not the correct translation . " Lucifer " is the Latin word for " light - bringer " . When you 're translating a book to English , if you think a particular word is a name , you do n't translate it at all , much less into a third language . Daniel is n't called " Amadeus " , Emmanuel is n't called " Deuscumnos " , so " Helel " should n't be " Lucifer " .
