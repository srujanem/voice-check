The download is fast due to CDNs . A CDN is a " content delivery network " , where the distributor ( in this case , JetBrains or Google ) put their files on servers near your location . When your browser hits ` URL_0 ` , it does n't go across the Atlantic , but instead goes to a server most likely in or near your country ( or to be more specific , to a place that 's very few hops away ) . Another thing to add - when your browser is downloading that file , it is simply grabbing a stream and writing it to a buffer or to disk . It 's a single task . Now an unzip , it does a bit more . Android Studio for example , contains hundreds or thousands of files . If you go to the folder that it created , you will see not just Android Studio but an Android SDK and hundreds of other files . That means , when the file is being unzipped , it is actually writing many files to disk . In other words , it is doing more than a single task of ' downloading ' , it is uncompressing and writing . That is why it appears relatively ' slower ' . This is also affected by whether you have a mechanical disk or a solid state disk , and how busy your computer is , whether other applications are also writing to disk at the time , but you get the gist of it .
