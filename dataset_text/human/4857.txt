That 's a great question , actually . It is incredibly viable but unfortunately politically unrealistic for now . To understand nuclear waste , you need to understand how nuclear power works . The tl;dr version is that nuclear waste is the left over radioactive material after it is ' burned up ' as fuel in a nuclear reactor . We ca n't get rid of it because these elements have EXTREMELY long half lives , or in other words they wo n't decay into less harmful materials for thousands of years . So we bury it in mountains , or chain it to the bottom of the ocean , etc . The matter of reprocessing it is actually a pretty active field right now . Thorium reactors have fluid constantly running through them to keep them cool and help to keep them self - regulating which makes it magnitudes safer than traditional reactors . The spent up nuclear waste from other reactors can basically be dumped into these and burned up with the rest of it . The only problem with thorium reactors is they do n't exist yet ... pretty much as a result of lack of funding / interest from the large purses of the world . Bill Gates in particular has done a lot to donate towards research .
