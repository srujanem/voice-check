1 . Complexity 1 . Efficient pricing Scalping tickets to the superbowl is easy . There is only one superbowl , and the " official " ticket prices are well below what people are willing to pay . Even for regular football games , there 's just one game in a city in a given week , and since the " official " price does n't vary much over a season , it 's relatively predictable which tickets can be re - sold for profit . Ditto concerns : Known in advance , small number , re - selling is predictable . Airplane tickets vary wildly . From St Louis , there are like 132098401 different destinations you could want to fly to , at any date , at any time . You can buy " 100 tickets to the Superbowl " and sell them , but you ca n't just buy " 100 tickets from St Louis " and sell them . You would have to pick ahead of time which tickets are worth buying based on which ones you think will re - sell for more in the future . But here 's the other stumbling block : The airlines are * also * calculating which tickets are going to be more popular , and adjusting the prices . I 'm sure you 're aware that airline ticket pricing is some of the most complex - ass pricing models that consumers are regularly exposed to ; this is because airlines invest millions of dollars in statisticians and big computers in order to squeeze the price on each individual ticket as high as it can reasonably go . Because the airlines already optimize all the risk / rewards for the prices they offer , there 's no opportunity to buy tickets and re - sell them for more later on . Most attempts to do so will lose money on average , and any profit - making strategies will quickly become obsoleted as the airlines adapt their pricing to recoup the money that the reseller is making instead of themselves .
