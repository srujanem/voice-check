Firstly , December 21st would be the " shortest day " in the Northern Hemisphere , not the longest . And it 's not . The dates of the longest and shortest days of the year change depending on your latitude . December 21st is the winter solstice -- which has to do with the position of the sun in the sky not the length of the day . Also , the winter solstice sometimes falls on the 22nd . Secondly , and to answer the question : it DOES get warmer immediately following the shortest day . However the air temperature that you experience in January and February is artificially cooler if you have land to the west of you . Mountains with snow pack , frozen lakes , and snow piles in parking lots all combine to steal heat from the air as they melt -- lowering the temperature in January and February . As evidence of this , look at the 100 - year median temperatures of Seattle and Los Angeles -- which upturn immediately after their respective " shortest days " . Because the Pacific does n't have frozen patches , it absorbs heat from the air uniformly . Thus the air temperature in those coastal cities are protected from the extremes and you can see on the graph that the coldest days are the shortest days . BTW , there are places on the Earth where for a few weeks a year there is no night at all . It 's called Midnight Sun .
