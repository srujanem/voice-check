The stuff about proving a negative is true , but I think the term is used specifically in IP and patent law cases where the owner of patent would normally be required to prove that her patent is the only way to make some product . For example , Anna invents a machine that makes AnnaSnacks and she patents it . A few months later , Bob starts selling AnnaSnacks . As far as Anna knows , her machine is the only way to make AnnaSnacks so she sues Bob for violating her patent . Since it 's basically impossible for Anna to prove that there 's no other way to make AnnaSnacks , the Court will either shift the burden of proof on Bob so that Bob has to prove he can make AnnaSnacks in a different way or it will give Anna legal authority to go inspect Bob 's operation so she can see if he 's using her patent without permission .
