When a foreign body enters your blood system ( a virus , a bacterium etc . ) it has antigens on it surface . Think if these antigens as an ID for that body . Your immune system can read these antigens , and if it recognizes them as friendly , it lets them be , if it does not , it attacks them , and if it remembers them as a pathogen ( disease causing body ) it uses specific attacks . Now your own blood cells have antigens too , and your blood type corresponds to the presence of that antigen . A means antigen type A , B means type B , AB means presence of both , and O is presence of neither . If you 're type A and you 're given type B blood , your immune system will attack the type B blood cells because it wo n't recognize them , it will however , leave your type A cells alone . Your immune cells and antibodies ( proteins that help fight disease ) will attach to the foreign blood cells and destroy them . This can cause blood clots , and result in internal hemorrhage ( bleeding ) , which can lead to death . This is why blood typing is very important . There is also another aspect of blood typing to consider , this is Rh factoring . This is what is meant by being A positive , or B negative . Positive Rh means that you have the Rh antigen on your blood cells , and Rh negative means you do not . If you have are negative for Rh , and you receive Rh positive blood , regardless of whether the A or B was correct , the immune system will still attack the Rh positive blood . This is why AB positive is the universal recipient , as it has both AB , and Rh antigens , so the immune system has been exposed to everything , and it will not attack any different blood type . It is also why O negative is the universal donor , it has neither A , B , nor Rh antigens , so the immune system does not attack . ( AB and Rh are not the only antigens on your blood cells , there are many more , but these are the three most important ) Source : 5 years in EMS and various physiology classes
