The structure varies even in the U.S. Army depending on the weapons and type of troops involved but the basic structure using infantry is : * Fireteam of 4 men * Squad of 2 fireteams + an NCO ( 9 men total ) * Platoon of 4 squads and a platoon headquarters group to make up 42 men , includes an junior officer . * Company of 4 - 6 platoons plus some HQ * Battalion of 3 companies + extras * Regiment of 3 battalions + extras * Brigade of 3 regiments + extras * Division of 3 brigades + extras ( 10,000 - 30,000 men ) . * Corps a variable number of divisions + extras * Army a variable number of corps + extras * Army group a variable number of armies . After you get to the company level additional smaller units are usually attached for various purposes the number above are just the direct combat troops . Intelligence , Artillery , Recon , and Logistics can all be attached . The size of those units depends on the size of the parent unit and what is required but its usually 1 or 2 steps lower . So an Infantry Division like the U.S. [ 1st Infantry Division ] ( URL_0 ) during WWI had 2 infantry brigades , an artillery brigade , a regiment of engineers , a battalion each of machine gunners , signal troops , ammunition , supply , engineer , and sanitary trains , and a headquarters company . For most the 20th century the Division has been the basic unit of the army capable of combined arms independent operation with the Army above it as really just organizational unit . Divisions moved in and out of Armies it as needed . That is changing in the U.S. Army as capabilities increase brigades are becoming that unit and divisions having brigades moved in and out as needed . When you get back to the 19th century the regiment was the basic building block but it was n't a combined arms force . There are other terms used specially for certain types of troops : a company of Artillery is a Battery , a company of cavalry ( horses originally , tanks and helicopters in the modern day ) is a Troop . A battalion of cavalry is a Squadron .
