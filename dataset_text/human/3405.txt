It all comes down to heating days and cooling days . This is a massive oversimplification , but imagine the temperature goes up 1 degree every hour during daylight , and goes down 1 degree every hour of night . At the equinox , 12 hours of day and 12 hours of night , it stays the same . By mid autumn , you have 11 hours of day and 13 hours of night , so you lose 2 degrees a day . On winter solstice , you have 10 hours of day and 14 night , so lose 4 degrees a day . Here is where it gets interesting . Midwinter , the days are longer , but still shorter than the nights . You are back to 11 hours of daylight and 13 of night , but you are still losing 2 degrees a day . Days are longer , and the rate of cooling is shrinking , but you are still cooling . Obviously , there is a * lot * more going on here , but that is basic mechanism of seasonal lag .
