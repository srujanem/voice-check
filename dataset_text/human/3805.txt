Normally , the top layer of your skin is made up of what are basically the husks of dead skin cells ( stratum corneum ) . Under this thin layer are tightly connected skin cells for several layers making up the majority of the epidermis . Under the epidermis is the dermis , which is mostly made of connective tissue ( regularly organized strands of protein like collagen ) and some immune cells , fat cells , and connective tissue cells . When the skin is injured down to the dermis or even deep into the epidermis , all of the cells in the area try to heal the wound . The skin cells that were damage / killed are cleared away , and the healthy skin cells try to proliferate into the wound to regenerate the damaged tissue . The problem is that the cells that lay down the connective tissue proteins ( collagen , fibrin , and others ) produce the proteins rapidly and in a disorganized fashion . So the tissue ' heals ' with a scar faster than it can ' regenerate ' well organized skin tissue . Once the wound is healed , even if it is with a collagen scar , the cells in the area responsible for wound healing ( macrophages and fibroblasts ) are all like : " well , nothing 's getting in here " and go about their business maintaining the dermal layer . So , while normal skin renews itself from the bottom of the skin , up , a scar is n't really proper skin to begin with and you are pretty much stuck with it without cosmetic surgery .
