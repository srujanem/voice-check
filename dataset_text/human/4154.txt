Russia has never really seen Ukraine as a really independent country . A few reasons for this - Ukraine has a long history of being associated with Russia , and was a province of the USSR until the late 80s ( so that 's only 20 years of independence ) . Unlike other USSR provinces ( such as the Baltic states ) , Ukraine was a much more natural fit inside the union , as their language , culture , and history are much closer to Russian language , culture , and history than those other states . Many parts of Ukraine ( notably Crimea ) were part of Russia before they become part of Ukraine , and the populations there are still predominantly Russian speaking . Then there are are the geopolitical reasons - Russia does not want to give up their naval base in the Crimea , their only " warm water " port . To lose this would be a huge loss from a military strategy point of view . And Russia does not want to see NATO troops and missiles sitting in Ukraine along their border . It would be like Russia taking over Canada and sticking missiles along the US Canadian border pointed at US cities . The US would n't come within a thousand light years of that coming to pass , so it 's strange that we think Russia should accept it . So Russia really sees the moves in Ukraine for what they really are : nothing to do with Ukraine and everything to do with weakening Russia , which is unfriendly and threatening to them .
