It is n't allowed per se . The players still get penalized . The severity of the penalty varies league by league ; in minor leagues it is a game misconduct and the player is ejected , in the NHL it is a 5 - minute penalty and the player is ineligible to play for the next 5 minutes . Fighting was banned in both basketball and baseball because they are both ( mostly ) non - contact sports . Football is a contact sport however there is just as much downtime as there is playing time therefor the tension and player temper is a lot lower . With the fast pace of hockey , tension and tempers can boil over . A lot of minor slashes , hits and bumps that would otherwise not be judge to be worth a penalty annoy the opposing player and sometimes they retaliate and a fight ensues . In the past , fighting was used as a deterrent to protect more skilled players . Dave Semenko is probably the most famous for this as his main roll was to protect Wayne Gretzky ; if any opposing player dare touch Gretzky , he 'd have to answer to Semenko . This led to other teams employing fighters as a strategy to take care of other teams tough guys . This was the era of the enforcer . Bob Probert , Tie Domi and many other players had very little role other than to fight the other teams tough guy . One common justification is that winning a fight could " swing momentum " in a game and your team would play better . This has not been proven despite study . In the mid 90s the NHL tried to curtail fighting by adding the " enforcer rule " . If a player is judged to be the initiator of a fight that player may be penalized an extra 2 minutes and/or receive a 10 - minute misconduct . That cut down on fights as retaliation . In short , the NHL allows fighting because they 've never banned it . The keepers of the game view it as an important part of the sport .
