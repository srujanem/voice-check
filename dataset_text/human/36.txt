Indian Councils Act of 1909 is also called as 'Minto-Morley Reforms Act'. During the formulation and implementation of this act, Lord Minto was the viceroy of India and Lord Morley was the Secretary of State for India. This Act was used to divide and rule India. The total number of central legislature members was increased to 60 from 16. The number of council members was also increased in the provinces. The members for the legislature was allowed through election for the first time. In order to provide separate representation for Muslims, 'Separate Electorate College' was created.
