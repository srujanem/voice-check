It can be lost . Generally speaking , speech is one of the first things we learn and last things we lose . In fact , loss of speech is one of the defining characteristics of Stage 7 Alzheimer 's Disease ( there are only 7 stages ) . My mother had a stroke , and her memory was severely changed . She has no short term memory , and lost many years of memories . What stayed is unreliable and spotty , but she 's grateful she remembers her childhood , having kids , getting married , family vacations , etc . She could not speak after it happened , she could make happy noises and sad noises , but it was nothing like a word . We brought her favorite cookies , and she quite literally could not pick them up and put them in her mouth , she had forgotten how and we had to help . She understood our words , but could n't make a response come out of her own mouth . Strangely enough , she could still write a response , and her beautiful handwriting was exactly the same . It was the only comfort I had that my mom was still in there somewhere . Thankfully , she 's regained speech , but her memory has not improved and her motor functions are still lacking . TL;DR You can literally lose everything , speech included .
