Water clarity is mainly affected by two factors : ( 1 ) sediment floating around in the water and ( 2 ) plankton and other organic material . Like /u / jesonnier said , the Gulf is generally muddy from sediment from the Mississippi river . For the West Coast , it 's a combination of the two . Ocean surface water generally flows west to east due to the earth 's rotation . When deeper water flows in to replace the surface water that flowed out , it often brings more sediment with it that it picked up from the ocean floor . Thus , water near the surface on the West Coast has a fair amount of sediment in it that it loses as it moves further out east . The sediment also tends to be smaller than that in the water in the Caribbean , so it stays suspended near the surface more easily . The water near the West Coast is also more nutrient rich so there 's more plankton and other organic material . The Caribbean ( and other places , such as Hawaii ) do n't experience the influx same amount of deep , sediment - rich ocean water that the West Coast does . It also has more barrier reefs , which help keep sediment - rich water away from the coast . There are also fewer nutrients in the water and less plankton as a result .
