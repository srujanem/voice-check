German also evolved , but not quite as much , so modern German still has a case and gender system : but it has already dropped the instrumental case , the accusative case differs from the nominative case only in the masculine singular , and the genitive case is being used less than it used to be . The Dutch language has also evolved , further than German but not quite as far as English : the case system has virtually disappeared , and the Dutch for " the " is " de " except for the neuter singular , which is " het " . Why are genders and cases necessary ? Well , in one sense , they 're not ; but they are n't there because somebody thought they were necessary . They 're there because the language evolved that way . Cases , of course , are useful so that you know what the relationship between different parts of the sentence is . If , if German , you say " Den Mann beißt der Hund " , the " den " makes it clear that it is the dog that bites the man , not the other way around . In English ( and increasingly in German ) , we use word order instead of case : " The man bites the dog " means something quite different from " The dog bites the man " . Nobody knows for certain why noun genders exist , but the best guess is that humans just like to categorize things . In that sense , calling them " genders " is unhelpful ; although people an animals may be put into different categories based on their biological sex , that 's obviously not so with inanimate nouns like " der Tisch " , " die Lampe " or " das Haus " . Just call them " categories " . Why nouns should be categorized in this way is n't known , but different cultures have different ways of categorizing things : one culture , for example , might have one category for men and gods , and a different category for women and inanimate objects ; others have a large number of categories according to shape and size , so one category might be " long and thin " , another might be " round and flat " , and so on . European languages just call these categories " masculine " , " feminine " and sometimes " neuter " , but those really are just names for the categories . That 's about all we know for sure .
