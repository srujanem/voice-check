British imposed a heavy tax on sale of Indian goods in England. Because of the zamindari system, the landlords, acting as commission agents between the Government and the farmers, exploited the farmers. The right given to the taluks to collect tax was withdrawn. An ‘Inam Commission’ was appointed and all the gifted lands (‘Inam lands’) were taken back. Due to this, the farmers felt insulted and experienced many financial problems.
