Have you ever noticed how hairy most flies are ? Many of those hairs are sensory hairs that can detect even the slightest movement of air . If you look at these hairs up close under a microscope , you 'll notice that they 're each in a little socket in the fly 's exoskeleton , which means that they can move back and forth . When a hand ( or something else ) is moving towards the fly , the air in front of the hand also moves ... and causes those hairs to bend in their sockets . Underneath the exoskeleton , there are nerve endings tied tightly around the bases of these hairs . When the hairs bend , those nerve endings stretch , and that causes a signal to be sent to the central nervous system at a very rapid pace . This alerts the fly to the danger approaching it , giving it enough time to high - tail it out of there . It 's a beautifully intricate system . Also , like others have said , many flies have 360 degree ( or near-360 ) vision , and can see things approaching them even if they ca n't feel them with their sensory hairs . Insects are amazing little machines .
