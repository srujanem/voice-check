I only know a little bit about SSL so i can try to explain this ( hopefully someone else will append this and make it much better ) . SSL stands for secure sockets layer . What happens is that when you send data across the internet , it 's sent in little packets or chunks . Theoretically , someone could read these packets and figure out what you 're sending . With SSL , your web browser ( internet explorer , mozilla firefox , or chrome to name a few ) will encrypt the packets before sending them . Once sent , they will be decrypted on the other end by the receiving person . This way , even if someone saw the packet in between , the contents would be total garbage and they would n't be able to understand it . And this is good because it ensures secrecy of your information between you and your recipient . To encrypt means , more or less , to scramble . And to decrypt means to unscramble . It 's like if you and your friend wanted to talk to each other but did n't want anyone else to know what you were saying , you might come up with a code that only you two know about . Lets say , for instance , that when you wrote to your friend you 'd replace every ' a ' with a ' b ' , every ' b ' with a ' c ' , etc . If someone picked up the letter you wrote to your friend it would look like complete gibberish . However your friend would understand how to read it ( replace every ' b ' with an ' a ' , every ' c ' with a ' b ' , etc . ) and therefore the contents would be secret between you and your friend . This is the major form of security used by a lot of banking websites , email websites , etc .
