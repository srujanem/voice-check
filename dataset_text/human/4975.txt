Yikes , without opening myself up for a world of hurt : The word " Bible " comes from the ancient Greek for " the books " and is used ( in this context ) to describe the religious texts of either Judaism or Christianity . I 'll use the Christian Bible as an example as it 's what I 'm most familiar with . The Christian Bible is split into two parts , the Old and the New Testament , each of these contain many separate books . The * * Old Testament * * contains books describing the formation of the universe and the creation of mankind ( The Book of Genesis ) and the history of the Israelites . The Old Testament contains passages you 're probably familiar with ; the stories of Adam and Eve , Noah , Moses etc are told within , these all take place before the birth of Jesus Christ The * * New Testament * * tells the tale of Jesus Christ and the formation of Christianity , it contains the narrations of Jesus ' birth , life , death and resurrection as told by his followers . Where it gets complicated is that depending on which denomination you belong to dictates how many books , and which books are contained within the Testaments , this is the major difference between the denominations . Many of the books in the Old Testament are also seen as sacred by the followers of Judaism , though they do not regard Jesus Christ as the son of God .
