You mean " differentiated , " not " derived . " For any function f(x ) , let 's differentiate ( f(x))^2 . By chain rule you get d / dx ( ( f(x))^2 ) = 2 f(x ) f'(x ) When f(x ) = x , you get f'(x ) = 1 so the derivative is just 2x . When f(x ) = sin(x ) , you get derivative of ( f(x))^2 = sin^2(x ) is 2 sin(x ) cos(x ) . So the answer is that when you differentiate the square of something there 's an extra term in the product due to chain rule , it 's just that when you 're trying to differentiate x^2 the extra term is 1 and ends up not mattering .
