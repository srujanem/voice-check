A mirage is an optical illusion that occurs when light is bent, or refracted, in a way that makes an object appear to be in a different location than it actually is. This can happen when light travels through layers of air with different temperatures, such as when hot air rises from the ground on a sunny day. The light from the sky or from distant objects is bent as it passes through the hot air, causing the objects to appear distorted or displaced. Mirages are most common in desert or other arid regions, where the ground can heat up quickly and create layers of hot air. They can also occur at sea, where the air above the water may be cooler than the air above the land, causing light to be refracted in a way that makes distant objects appear to be hovering above the water. Mirages can take many different forms, including the appearance of a pool of water on the ground, or the appearance of a distant mountain or city on the horizon. They can be very convincing, but they are not real and are caused by the bending of light.
