The ancient Greeks were among the first people to propose that the Earth is round. They came to this conclusion by observing the way that the sun, moon, and stars move across the sky. They noticed that, during the day, the sun appears to move across the sky in a predictable pattern, and that at night, the stars also seem to move in a circular pattern around a fixed point in the sky. They also noticed that during a solar eclipse, the moon appears to be the same size as the sun, even though it is much farther away. All of these observations suggested to the ancient Greeks that the Earth must be a sphere, rather than a flat surface.\n\nLater, as humans began to explore more of the world, they found additional evidence that the Earth is round. For example, when sailors sailed far out to sea, they noticed that the horizon appeared to curve away from them, which would not be the case if the Earth were flat. They also observed that as they sailed toward the equator, the stars in the sky appeared to shift position, which is something that would not happen if the Earth were flat.\n\nIn modern times, we have even more evidence that the Earth is round, thanks to satellite imagery and other advanced technologies. However, the basic idea that the Earth is a sphere has been known for thousands of years.
