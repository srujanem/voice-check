If a syringe of water were injected into your veins, it would not be harmful in small quantities. However, injecting a large amount of water into your veins can be dangerous because it can cause your blood cells to become diluted. This can lead to a condition called hyponatremia, which occurs when the sodium levels in your blood become too low. Symptoms of hyponatremia include headache, nausea, vomiting, seizures, and even coma or death in severe cases. It is important to always follow your doctor's instructions and use medications as directed to avoid any potential harm.
