Lightning strikes are detected using special instruments called lightning detectors. These detectors are typically placed high up on towers or on top of buildings, and they use sensors to detect the electromagnetic waves that are emitted when lightning strikes.\n\nWhen a lightning strike occurs, it creates a sudden burst of electromagnetic energy that travels through the air. This energy can be detected by the sensors on the lightning detector, which sends a signal to a computer or other device to let it know that a lightning strike has occurred.\n\nThe computer or device then uses this information to calculate the location of the lightning strike and can display it on a map or other display. This allows people to see where the lightning is striking in real-time, even if they are far away from the storm.\n\nThere are also satellite-based lightning detectors that can detect lightning strikes from space. These satellites are equipped with sensors that can detect the electromagnetic waves emitted by lightning, and they can send this information back to Earth to be displayed on a map or other display. This allows people to see lightning strikes from anywhere in the world, as long as they have access to the satellite data.
