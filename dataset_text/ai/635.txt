There are a few factors that can make one country's passport more valuable than another's. One factor is the number of countries that the passport holder can visit without needing to get a visa in advance. Passports from countries that have strong diplomatic relationships with many other countries, or that are part of international organizations like the European Union, tend to have more visa-free travel options. This can make it easier and more convenient for the passport holder to travel internationally, which is why these passports are sometimes considered more valuable. 
Another factor that can make a passport more valuable is the overall reputation and stability of the country. Passports from countries that are seen as safe and well-governed are often more highly valued because they can give the passport holder access to more opportunities and privileges when traveling or living abroad. 
Finally, some passports are considered more valuable because they can provide their holders with certain privileges or benefits, such as access to exclusive clubs or discounts on certain products or services. This is often the case for passports from countries with a high standard of living or a strong economy. 
So, in general, a "valuable" passport is one that allows the holder to travel easily and conveniently, is issued by a stable and well-regarded country, and may offer other privileges or benefits.
