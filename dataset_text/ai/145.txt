TV shows are generally a certain length because it's a convenient amount of time for the people who make and watch the show. 30-minute sitcoms (which is short for "situation comedies") are usually shorter because they are meant to be funny and lighthearted. 60-minute dramas are usually longer because they often have more complex storylines and deal with heavier subject matter. 
There have been a few 60-minute comedies, but they are not very common. One example is the show "Arrested Development," which aired on FOX from 2003 to 2006 and then on Netflix from 2013 to 2018. This show was a bit longer than most sitcoms, but it still followed the same basic format of telling funny stories with characters who have wacky problems and conflicts. 
In general, though, most TV shows are either 30 minutes or 60 minutes long because those lengths work well for the kind of stories that the show is trying to tell.
