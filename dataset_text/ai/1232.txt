Rigor mortis, or the stiffening of muscles after death, occurs because the muscles in the body are no longer able to move or contract. This happens because the chemical reactions in the body that allow the muscles to move have stopped. 
When meat is processed at a factory, the animal has already been slaughtered and the muscles are no longer able to move or contract. The meat is then cut into smaller pieces and may be processed in various ways, such as being ground into ground beef or cured to make ham. 
There are no chemicals or processes that are used to prevent rigor mortis from occurring in processed meat. Instead, the meat has already gone through rigor mortis and the muscles are no longer able to move or contract.
