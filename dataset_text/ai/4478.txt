In radio frequencies, the lower the frequency, the longer the wavelength. Longer wavelengths can travel further distances because they are less affected by obstacles in the environment, such as walls and trees. However, lower frequencies also have less bandwidth, which means they can carry less data at a time.\n\nImagine a highway with cars as a way to understand bandwidth. If the highway is wider, more cars can travel on it at the same time, just like if a radio frequency has more bandwidth, more data can be transmitted at once.\n\nOn the other hand, higher frequencies have shorter wavelengths and can be more easily absorbed or blocked by obstacles. This is why higher frequencies have a shorter range and are more affected by things like walls and trees. However, higher frequencies also have more bandwidth, which means they can carry more data at a time.\n\nSo, in summary, lower frequencies have longer wavelengths and can travel further distances, but have less bandwidth. Higher frequencies have shorter wavelengths and a shorter range, but have more bandwidth.
