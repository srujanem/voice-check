Quantitative easing is a monetary policy that central banks use to stimulate the economy. It works by increasing the money supply in the economy, which can help increase economic activity and reduce unemployment. Here's how it works: The central bank creates new money by buying government bonds or other financial assets from banks. This new money is then used by the banks to make new loans to businesses and individuals, which can help increase spending and investment in the economy. The central bank can also reduce the interest rates it charges to banks, which can encourage them to lend more and stimulate economic activity. So, to summarize, quantitative easing is a way for central banks to try to boost the economy by adding more money to the financial system and encouraging banks to lend more.
