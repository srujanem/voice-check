Sweating and feeling hot when it's 90 degrees outside is a normal and natural response to the body trying to cool itself down. Our bodies are naturally set to maintain a temperature of around 98.6 degrees Fahrenheit, which is considered to be the "normal" body temperature. When the temperature outside is warmer than our body temperature, our bodies will naturally try to cool themselves down to maintain this normal temperature. One way our bodies cool themselves down is by sweating. When we sweat, the moisture on our skin evaporates, which helps to cool our bodies down. So, if it's hot and humid outside, it can be more difficult for the sweat on our skin to evaporate and cool us down, which is why we may feel even hotter and more uncomfortable. Overall, sweating and feeling hot when it's hot outside is just the body's way of trying to keep itself at a healthy temperature.
