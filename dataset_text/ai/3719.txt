Private prisons are facilities that are run by private companies, rather than by the government. They began in the United States in the 1980s as a way to help reduce overcrowding in public prisons and to save money on the cost of running prisons. Private prisons are not very prevalent in the United States, as they make up a small percentage of the total number of prisons. However, some states have a higher percentage of prisoners in private facilities than others. Private companies make money from private prisons by being paid a certain amount of money by the government for each prisoner that they hold. Private prisons are not like people owning other people, because prisoners are not owned by the private companies that run the facilities. However, some people do criticize private prisons because they believe that the companies running them are more concerned with making a profit than with the well-being of the prisoners. Prisoners in private facilities may be required to work, just like prisoners in public facilities. However, they are not paid very much for their work, if they are paid at all. There is some debate about whether there is any good in having a privatized prison system. Some people believe that private prisons can be more cost-effective and efficient than public prisons, while others believe that they may be less accountable and may not provide as good of conditions for prisoners.
