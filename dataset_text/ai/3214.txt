In biology, the term "family" usually refers to a group of animals that are closely related and have similar characteristics. Some animals, like humans, have strict rules about who they can have babies with because their babies might have health problems if they have parents who are too closely related. This is because close relatives have many of the same genes, and if a baby gets two copies of a gene that isn't working properly, it can cause problems. Other animals, like many species of birds and some species of fish, don't have this problem because they lay eggs and the eggs hatch into babies. The parents don't have to be closely related to each other for the babies to be healthy. In general, animals can have babies with other animals that are similar to them, but it's usually healthier for babies to have parents who aren't too closely related. This is because babies are more likely to have a mix of good genes from both parents, which can help them grow and be healthy.
