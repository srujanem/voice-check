The price of gasoline is typically listed in terms of the price per gallon, but the pumps at gas stations often display the price for a fraction of a gallon. The reason for this is that the pumps are measuring the volume of gas in terms of hundredths of a gallon, rather than full gallons. Imagine you are filling up a gas can at a gas station. The gas can might hold one gallon of gas, but the pump is measuring the volume of gas in smaller increments, like a hundredth of a gallon at a time. When you see the price listed as "9/10" of a gallon, it means that you are paying for nine-tenths of a gallon of gas. So, if you see a price listed as "$3.00/9/10 gal," it means that you will pay $3.00 for nine-tenths of a gallon of gas, which is just a little bit less than a full gallon. This way, the gas station can charge you a little bit more accurately for the exact amount of gas that you are pumping, rather than just rounding up to the nearest full gallon.
