During sleep, your body goes into a state of muscle paralysis called "sleep paralysis." This is a normal part of the sleep cycle and it helps to prevent you from acting out your dreams and potentially harming yourself. When you are in the state of sleep paralysis, you may feel like you cannot move or speak, but this is only temporary and will pass as you continue to sleep. It is not uncommon for people to experience vivid dreams and sensations while in the state of sleep paralysis, and some people may even feel like they are rolling around in bed or moving in some other way, even though their body is still paralyzed. 
It is also possible that you were not actually in a state of sleep paralysis when you woke up hanging off your bed. It could be that you simply moved around in your sleep and ended up in that position. Some people are more active in their sleep and may move around more than others. If you are concerned about your sleep habits or have experienced episodes of sleep paralysis, it may be a good idea to speak with a healthcare professional for further evaluation and guidance.
