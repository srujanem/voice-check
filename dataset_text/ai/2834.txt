Radiation poisoning, also known as radiation sickness, occurs when a person is exposed to a large amount of ionizing radiation, which is a type of energy that can damage the cells in the body. When a person is exposed to radiation, it can damage the DNA in their cells, which can lead to the cells becoming mutated or dying. This can cause a variety of symptoms, including nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and a weakened immune system, which can make it more difficult for the body to fight off infections. When a person drinks contaminated water or inhales contaminated air, they may be exposed to radiation that has been released from a nuclear power plant, a nuclear weapon, or some other source of radiation. The amount of radiation a person is exposed to, and how it affects their health, depends on the type of radiation, the amount of radiation, and the duration of the exposure. In severe cases of radiation poisoning, the damage to the cells in the body can be so extensive that it can lead to organ failure and death. For example, if the radiation damages the cells in the bone marrow (where new blood cells are made), it can lead to a lack of white blood cells, which can make it more difficult for the body to fight off infections. If the radiation damages the cells in the digestive system, it can lead to severe diarrhea, dehydration, and malnutrition. If the radiation damages the cells in the central nervous system, it can lead to seizures, coma, and death.
