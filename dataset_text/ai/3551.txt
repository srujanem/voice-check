Black holes are objects that are so massive and dense that they have a gravitational pull so strong that nothing can escape from them, not even light. When two black holes collide, they create a massive amount of energy in the form of gravitational waves. These waves can be detected by special instruments, and scientists have actually observed the collision of two black holes using these instruments. The collision of two black holes would also create a new, larger black hole. This black hole would be formed from the combined mass of the two original black holes. The black holes would "swirl" around each other and eventually merge into one. It's important to note that black hole collisions are extremely rare and only happen in very specific circumstances. They are also extremely far away from Earth, so there's no danger of them affecting us directly. However, the collision of two black holes can help us learn more about the universe and the forces at work within it.
