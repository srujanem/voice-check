Tidal locking occurs when the period of rotation of a celestial body is the same as the period of revolution around its parent body. For example, the Moon is tidally locked to Earth, meaning that it rotates on its axis at the same rate that it orbits Earth. The reason that tidal locking occurs is because of the gravitational forces between the two bodies. When one body is much more massive than the other, it can exert a strong gravitational pull on the smaller body. This can cause the smaller body to become "locked" in place, with one side always facing the larger body. As for whether a tidally locked body can start spinning again, it is possible in theory, but it would require a very large change in the gravitational forces acting on the body. For example, if the Moon were to move much farther away from Earth, the gravitational forces between the two would be weaker, and the Moon might be able to start spinning again. However, this is extremely unlikely to happen, since the Moon is already very far from Earth and the gravitational forces between the two are already very weak.
