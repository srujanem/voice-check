Money laundering is a way for people to make money that they have gotten through illegal or dishonest means seem legal. Imagine that a person has stolen some money, but they can't just go and spend it because it's not legal for them to have it. They need to find a way to make it look like they got the money through legal means, so they don't get caught. One way they might do this is by "washing" the money, or making it seem like it came from a legal source. For example, they might take the stolen money and use it to buy something like a car or a piece of jewelry. Then, they might sell the car or jewelry for a slightly higher price to someone else. Now, the person who bought the car or jewelry has the money, and the person who stole it has a little bit more money that seems like it came from the sale of a car or jewelry. Another way they might do this is by moving the money through different accounts or countries, so it's harder to trace where it came from. For example, they might take the stolen money and deposit it into a bank account in one country. Then, they might transfer that money to another bank account in a different country, and then transfer it again to another account. Each time they do this, it becomes harder to track where the money came from, and it starts to look like it came from a legal source. Money laundering is illegal because it helps people get away with breaking the law and keeps them from being punished for their crimes. It also takes money out of the economy and can hurt people who depend on the money for things like jobs, taxes, and government services.
