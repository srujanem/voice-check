In the United States, sweepstakes and contests are regulated by the Federal Trade Commission (FTC), which is a government agency that protects consumers from deceptive or misleading practices. According to the FTC, a sweepstakes is a game of chance in which a prize is awarded to a winner or winners selected by chance. A contest is a game of skill in which a prize is awarded to a winner or winners based on their performance in a particular activity. 
In order to comply with FTC regulations, companies that offer sweepstakes or contests must clearly disclose the rules of the game, including how the winner will be selected and how to enter without making a purchase. This means that if a company offers a sweepstakes or contest that requires a purchase to enter, it must also offer a way for consumers to enter without making a purchase, commonly known as a "no purchase necessary" option. 
For example, if a restaurant like Subway or McDonald's offers a sticker peel game in which customers can win a prize by collecting a certain number of stickers with their purchases, the restaurant must also provide a way for customers to enter the game without making a purchase. This could include, for example, mailing in a request to enter or visiting the restaurant's website and filling out an online entry form. 
In summary, "no purchase necessary" means that a consumer can enter a sweepstakes or contest without making a purchase, as long as they follow the instructions provided by the company for entering without making a purchase.
