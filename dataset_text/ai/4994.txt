\nRainbows are formed when light is refracted, or bent, as it passes through water droplets in the air. The size of a rainbow depends on the size of the water droplets and the angle at which the light is refracted. Rainbows can appear in different sizes, but they are usually circular and can be quite large.\n\nThe curve of the Earth does not affect the size of a rainbow. However, the shape of the Earth can affect the way that rainbows are seen. For example, if you are standing on a mountain and looking at a rainbow, it might appear to be closer to you than it actually is because of the way the Earth curves.\n\nTo explain it like you're five: Rainbows are colorful circles that are made when light passes through water in the air.
