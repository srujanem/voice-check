The European Space Agency (ESA) is actually a major player in the world of space exploration, and has made many important contributions to the field. However, the reason that Europe has not yet launched a human into space on its own is primarily because of the very high cost and technical challenges involved in building and operating a manned space program. Developing the technology and infrastructure needed to send humans into space requires significant resources, including funding, skilled engineers and scientists, and specialized equipment. It also requires a lot of time and effort to research, design, and test new systems to ensure that they are safe and reliable. In the past, European countries have chosen to collaborate with other space agencies, such as NASA, in order to access the resources and expertise needed to send humans into space. For example, ESA has worked closely with NASA on a number of manned space missions, including the International Space Station (ISS), which has been continuously occupied by humans since 2000. Overall, while Europe may not yet have launched a human into space on its own, it has made many important contributions to the field of space exploration and will continue to do so in the future.
