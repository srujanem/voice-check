Apple and Microsoft have both bundled their web browsers with their operating systems in the past, which means that the web browser was included automatically with the operating system when a user purchased a device or computer. The difference is that Microsoft was accused of using its dominant market position to unfairly advantage its Internet Explorer browser over competing browsers. This is known as monopolistic behavior and is illegal under antitrust laws. As a result, Microsoft was sued and ultimately settled the case by agreeing to offer a choice of different web browsers to users during the installation process of its operating system. Apple, on the other hand, has not been accused of using its market position to unfairly advantage its Safari browser over competing browsers in the same way. As a result, Apple has not faced the same legal challenges or criticism for bundling Safari with its operating system. It's important to note that antitrust laws are designed to protect competition and ensure that consumers have a choice of products and services, rather than allowing a single company or product to dominate the market.
