Tor (short for The Onion Router) is a free, open-source network of servers that allows users to browse the internet anonymously. When you use Tor, your internet traffic is routed through a series of servers, or "nodes," that are run by volunteers all around the world. This makes it difficult for anyone to track your online activity or see your real IP address. Many people use Tor to protect their privacy and security online, especially when they are communicating sensitive information or accessing websites that might be blocked in their country. For example, journalists, activists, and whistle-blowers might use Tor to communicate with each other and share information without fear of being monitored or censored by their governments. However, it is possible for the Tor network to be compromised, either by hackers who are able to gain access to one of the nodes in the network, or by government agencies who are able to monitor traffic on the network. If this happens, it could potentially allow someone to see the activity of users on the Tor network and potentially even identify them.
