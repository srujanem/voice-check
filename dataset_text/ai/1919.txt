Non-Euclidean geometry is a type of geometry that is based on different postulates (assumptions) than the ones that Euclid used in his geometry. In Euclidean geometry, which is the type of geometry that we use in everyday life, the postulate states that the sum of the angles in a triangle is always 180 degrees. However, in non-Euclidean geometry, this postulate is not true. There are two main types of non-Euclidean geometry: hyperbolic geometry and elliptic geometry. In hyperbolic geometry, the sum of the angles in a triangle is less than 180 degrees. This means that the lines in a hyperbolic geometry can curve and bend in strange ways, and they can even intersect each other multiple times. In elliptic geometry, the sum of the angles in a triangle is more than 180 degrees. This means that the lines in an elliptic geometry also curve and bend, but in a different way than in hyperbolic geometry. Non-Euclidean geometry can be difficult to understand because it goes against our everyday experience of the world. However, it has important applications in mathematics and physics, and it helps us understand the shape of the universe.
