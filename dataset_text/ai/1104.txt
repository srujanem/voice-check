In German, as in many other languages, the articles (the words for "the" in English) change depending on the noun they are referring to. This is called declension, and it allows speakers of the language to convey information about the noun's gender, case, and number. 
For example, the German word "der" is used for masculine singular nouns, while "die" is used for feminine singular nouns and "das" is used for neuter singular nouns. The articles "dem" and "den" are used for the dative case (a grammatical case used to indicate the recipient of an action), and "des" is used for the genitive case (a grammatical case used to indicate possession or origin). 
In English, we do not have to worry about these different forms of the article because English does not have grammatical cases or gender for nouns. Instead, we use the same article "the" for all nouns, regardless of their gender or case.
