There are several reasons why prescription drug prices can fluctuate. One reason is that the cost of ingredients and manufacturing a drug can change over time. For example, if the price of a certain ingredient used to make a drug goes up, the drug manufacturer may need to charge more for the drug to make up for the added cost. Another reason is that different pharmacies may charge different prices for the same drug. This can be because they are in different locations, or because they have different agreements with drug manufacturers or insurance companies. Additionally, the price of a prescription drug can also depend on whether or not a person has insurance and what kind of insurance they have. Some insurance plans may cover certain drugs at a lower cost, while other plans may not cover the drug at all or may require the person to pay a higher copayment. Finally, it's important to note that the price of a prescription drug can also vary depending on the specific strength or quantity of the drug being purchased. For example, a larger quantity of a drug may cost more than a smaller quantity, even if it is the same type of drug.
