A military is a group of people trained to fight in wars and protect their country. The military is usually divided into smaller units, or groups, to make it easier to manage and control. Here are some common units in a military and what they do: 
Platoon: A platoon is a small unit of soldiers, usually made up of about 30 to 50 people. Platoons are led by a platoon leader, who is usually a lieutenant or a captain. Platoons are usually part of a larger unit called a company.
Company: A company is a unit of soldiers, usually made up of a few platoons. A company is usually led by a captain or a major.
Battalion: A battalion is a larger unit of soldiers, usually made up of several companies. A battalion is usually led by a lieutenant colonel.
Brigade: A brigade is an even larger unit of soldiers, made up of several battalions. A brigade is usually led by a colonel.
Division: A division is a very large unit of soldiers, made up of several brigades. A division is usually led by a general.
Corps: A corps is an even larger unit of soldiers, made up of several divisions. A corps is usually led by a lieutenant general or a general.
Army: An army is the largest unit in a military, made up of several corps. An army is usually led by a general. 
The numbers, like "5th" or "7th," often indicate the order in which a unit was formed or the region it is from. For example, the "5th Army" could be the fifth army that was formed in a country's military.
