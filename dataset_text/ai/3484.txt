When a plane flies at high altitudes, the air is much colder and the humidity is much lower. When the plane's engines burn fuel, they produce hot exhaust gases. When these gases mix with the cold, dry air at high altitudes, it causes the water vapor in the exhaust to condense into tiny droplets, forming what looks like a white trail or "smoke" behind the plane. This is called a contrail. Contrails are similar to the clouds that you see in the sky on a humid day, except that they are formed by the plane's exhaust instead of by the natural process of water vapor condensing in the air. Contrails are usually only visible at high altitudes because the air is colder and there is less moisture to condense. Some planes are designed to leave a more visible contrail on purpose, for example for advertising or special events. These planes may have special equipment that releases smoke or other materials to create the contrail. However, most planes that fly at high altitudes will leave a contrail, even if it is not very visible.
