HIV, or human immunodeficiency virus, is a virus that attacks the immune system. It is spread through certain bodily fluids, such as blood, semen, vaginal fluids, and breast milk. HIV is not spread through casual contact, such as shaking hands, hugging, or sharing food or drinks. The exact origins of HIV are not fully understood, but it is believed to have originated in non-human primates in West-central Africa and was transmitted to humans through contact with infected blood. It is thought that HIV first emerged in humans in the early 20th century and spread slowly at first. It is not possible for a person to get infected with HIV by sleeping with someone who already has the virus, unless there is exchange of bodily fluids during sexual activity or if HIV-infected blood or body fluids come into contact with an open wound or mucous membranes (such as the lining of the vagina, rectum, or mouth). It is not possible for HIV to "re-occur" in a virgin or in any other person who has not been exposed to the virus. HIV is not a new virus and it is not something that can spontaneously occur in people who have not been exposed to it. It is important to practice safe sex and to use protection, such as condoms, to reduce the risk of HIV transmission.
