The common logarithm base is 10 because it is a convenient choice that simplifies many calculations.\n\nImagine you have a set of numbers that are all very different from each other, like 1, 10, 100, 1000, and 10000. If you want to compare these numbers, it can be difficult to do so because they are so different from each other. This is where logarithms come in.\n\nA logarithm is a way to show how many times you need to multiply a number by itself to get another number. For example, the logarithm of 100 to base 10 is 2, because 10 x 10 = 100. The logarithm of 1000 to base 10 is 3, because 10 x 10 x 10 = 1000.\n\nUsing logarithms, we can compare these very different numbers by expressing them as powers of 10. For example, instead of saying "1000 is 1000 times bigger than 1," we can say "1000 is 3 powers of 10 bigger than 1," because the logarithm of 1000 to base 10 is 3, and the logarithm of 1 to base 10 is 0.\n\nSo, the common logarithm base is 10 because it allows us to easily compare and work with numbers that are very different from each other, and it is a convenient choice that has been widely adopted in mathematics and engineering.
