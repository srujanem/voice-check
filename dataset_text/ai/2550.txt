The Korean Demilitarized Zone (DMZ) is a strip of land that runs across the Korean Peninsula and serves as a buffer zone between North and South Korea. The DMZ was established as part of the armistice agreement that ended the Korean War in 1953. The purpose of the DMZ is to create a "demilitarized" area where no military activity is allowed. This means that no troops from either North or South Korea are allowed to enter the DMZ with weapons. However, both North and South Korea maintain a military presence near the DMZ to defend their respective sides of the border. So, even though there are armed troops present near the DMZ, they are not allowed to enter the DMZ itself with their weapons. This helps to reduce the risk of conflict between the two Koreas and create a more peaceful atmosphere in the region.
