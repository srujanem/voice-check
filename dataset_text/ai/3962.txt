When an object falls towards the ground, it starts to pick up speed as it falls. This is because of something called gravity, which is a force that pulls all objects towards the ground. However, as the object falls faster and faster, it starts to encounter more and more air resistance. Air resistance is a force that acts in the opposite direction to the object's motion, and it gets stronger as the object goes faster. At some point, the force of gravity pulling the object down and the force of air resistance pushing up on the object will balance out, and the object will stop accelerating and move at a steady speed called its "terminal velocity." This is the fastest speed that an object will reach while it is falling through the air.
