A suicide hotline is a service that provides support and assistance to people who are thinking about suicide or who are in crisis. The goal of a suicide hotline is to help people who are feeling overwhelmed and hopeless to find hope, support, and a sense of belonging. 
Here's how a suicide hotline might work: 
1. Someone who is feeling suicidal or in crisis can call the hotline at any time, day or night. They might call the hotline themselves, or someone else might call on their behalf. 
2. When the person calls the hotline, they will be connected to a trained volunteer or professional who is there to listen and provide support. 
3. The person calling the hotline can talk about their feelings, thoughts, and concerns with the volunteer or professional. They can also ask for help and advice on how to cope with their feelings and situation. 
4. The volunteer or professional will listen to the person and provide emotional support, as well as information about resources and services that may be able to help them. They may also offer suggestions for ways to cope with their feelings and find support. 
5. The volunteer or professional will stay on the phone with the person until they feel better or have a plan in place to stay safe. They may also follow up with the person after the call to check in and see if they are doing okay. 
Suicide hotlines can be very helpful and can save lives. If you or someone you know is feeling suicidal, please don't hesitate to call a suicide hotline or seek help from a trusted friend, family member, or professional.
