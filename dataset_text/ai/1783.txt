Water evaporates when it reaches its boiling point, which is 100 degrees Celsius (212 degrees Fahrenheit) at sea level. When water evaporates, it turns from a liquid into a gas, which is called steam. The steam can then rise into the air and leave the wet towel behind. Evaporation is a cooling process, because when water evaporates, it absorbs energy from its surroundings and cools down the area around it. This is why your wet beach towel will feel cool to the touch when the water on it starts to evaporate. As the water on your wet beach towel evaporates, it will leave behind any dirt or sand that was on the towel. This is why your towel will feel cleaner and drier after it has had a chance to air dry. So to sum it up, when water evaporates, it turns into a gas and leaves behind any solid particles that were in the water. This process helps things dry off and cool down.
