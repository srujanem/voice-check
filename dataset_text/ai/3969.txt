Diplomatic immunity is a set of rules that say that certain foreign government officials and their families do not have to follow the same laws as everyone else while they are in a foreign country. This is because these officials are representing their own country and the rules help them to do their job without fear of being punished by the host country. Family members are often covered by diplomatic immunity because they are living with the official and may be involved in the official's work. It is not true that people with diplomatic immunity can rob banks or murder people and get away with it. Diplomatic immunity is meant to protect officials from being unfairly targeted or punished by the host country, not to allow them to break the law. If someone with diplomatic immunity breaks a serious law, like robbery or murder, the host country can ask the person's home country to waive their immunity so that they can be charged and punished in the host country.
