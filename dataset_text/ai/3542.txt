The United Kingdom, which includes England, is a constitutional monarchy, which means that it has a monarch as the head of state, but the monarch does not have any political power. The current monarch of the UK is Queen Elizabeth II. When the queen dies, her son Prince Charles will become the king. When Prince Charles dies, his son Prince William will become the king. And so on. So, to answer your question, there will always be a "king of England," because the monarchy will continue as long as the United Kingdom exists. But the role of the monarch has become largely ceremonial, and the real power to govern the country lies with the elected government and the prime minister.
