Your body is made up of many different systems that work together to keep you alive and healthy. Some of these systems, like the circulatory system (which helps your heart pump blood) and the respiratory system (which helps you breathe), are controlled automatically by your body. This means that you don't have to think about them or do anything special to make them work. On the other hand, other systems in your body, like the muscular system (which helps you move) and the nervous system (which helps you think and feel), are controlled by your brain. This means that you have to use your brain to tell your body what to do. For example, if you want to walk across the room, you have to use your brain to send a message to your muscles telling them to contract (tighten) and relax in a specific pattern. Your brain also has to send messages to your body to help you balance and keep your body in the right position as you walk. So, while some parts of your body can work automatically, other parts need your brain's help to work properly.
