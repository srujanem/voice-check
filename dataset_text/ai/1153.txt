There are street children in the United States, but they are not as common as in other parts of the world such as Latin America, Africa, and parts of Asia. Street children are children who live and/or work on the streets, often without the support of a parent or guardian. 
There are many reasons why children end up living and working on the streets. In some cases, children may be forced to leave home because of poverty, abuse, neglect, or other difficult circumstances. In other cases, children may choose to leave home because they do not feel safe or supported there. 
In the United States, there are laws and social services in place to help protect children and prevent them from ending up on the streets. For example, there are programs that provide temporary housing, food, and other support to families in need. There are also laws that protect children from abuse and neglect, and that require parents to provide for their children's basic needs. 
However, despite these efforts, some children in the United States do end up living and working on the streets. In these cases, there are organizations and charities that work to help these children find safe and supportive environments, and to get the help and support they need to improve their circumstances.
