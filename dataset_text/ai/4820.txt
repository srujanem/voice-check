A derivative is a way to measure how much a function changes as its inputs (also called variables) change.\n\nImagine you have a function that describes how much money you will have in the bank after a certain number of years. The function might be something like "money in the bank = starting amount + interest rate * number of years." If you know the starting amount and the interest rate, you can use the function to figure out how much money you'll have in the bank after any number of years.\n\nThe derivative of this function tells you how fast the amount of money in the bank is changing at a given moment. For example, if the derivative is positive, that means the amount of money in the bank is increasing. If the derivative is negative, that means the amount of money in the bank is decreasing. If the derivative is zero, that means the amount of money in the bank isn't changing at all.\n\nDerivatives are important because they can help us understand how things change and make predictions about the future. For example, if you know the derivative of the function that describes how much money you have in the bank, you can use it to predict how fast your money will grow over time.\n\nI hope that helps!
