Most hybrid species are sterile because they have differences in the number of chromosomes between the parent species. Chromosomes are long strands of DNA that contain the genetic instructions for an organism's development and function. In most species, each parent contributes half of the chromosomes to their offspring, and the offspring has a complete set of chromosomes that are a combination of those from each parent.\n\nHowever, when two different species mate and produce offspring, the offspring may have an uneven number of chromosomes, which can cause problems with the way their cells divide and grow. When this happens, the cells may not divide properly, which can lead to abnormal development and reduced fertility. This is why most hybrid species are sterile.
