The Trayvon Martin case was a legal case in the United States that arose from the shooting death of Trayvon Martin, a 17-year-old African American, by George Zimmerman, a neighborhood watch volunteer, on February 26, 2012, in Sanford, Florida. Zimmerman was charged with second-degree murder but was acquitted of the charges at trial. The case received national attention and sparked protests and discussions about racial profiling, self-defense, and gun control. Trayvon was walking home from a convenience store, where he had purchased iced tea and a bag of Skittles candy, when he was confronted by Zimmerman, who thought Trayvon looked suspicious. Trayvon was unarmed and was wearing a hoodie at the time. Zimmerman and Trayvon got into a physical altercation and Zimmerman shot Trayvon, who died from his injuries. The case generated a lot of controversy and emotions, with many people believing that Zimmerman should have been found guilty for Trayvon's death. The case also highlighted the issue of racial profiling, which is when people are treated differently based on their race or ethnicity, and sparked discussions about self-defense laws and gun control.
