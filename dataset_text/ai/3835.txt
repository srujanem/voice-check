Personality disorders are a type of mental illness that can affect how a person thinks, feels, and behaves. People with personality disorders tend to have patterns of thinking and behavior that are different from what is considered "normal" in their culture. These patterns can cause problems in a person's life, such as difficulty with relationships, work, or school. The Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM) is a book that lists different types of mental illnesses and provides guidelines for diagnosing them. Personality disorders are included in the DSM because they can cause significant problems in a person's life and may need treatment. However, some people believe that personality disorders are not a "disease" in the same way that other mental illnesses, like depression or schizophrenia, are. This is because personality disorders are often long-lasting and may be a part of a person's personality or identity. Overall, it is important to remember that mental illnesses, including personality disorders, are real conditions that can affect a person's thoughts, feelings, and behaviors. With proper treatment, people with mental illnesses can often improve their symptoms and lead fulfilling lives.
