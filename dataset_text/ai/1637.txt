Satellite television works by using a network of satellites orbiting the Earth to transmit and receive signals from television stations. When you watch TV, you are tuning in to a specific channel, which is a specific frequency of the television signal. The satellite receives the signal from the television station and then transmits it back down to Earth, where it is received by a satellite dish installed at your home. The satellite dish is connected to a receiver box, which decodes the signal and sends it to your television. When you change the channel on your TV, the receiver box sends a signal to the satellite dish to tune in to a different frequency, and the satellite transmits the new channel back down to Earth. So, the satellite does not transmit all 600 channels at the same time. Instead, it transmits a specific channel when it is requested by the receiver box. This way, you can watch any channel you want, and the satellite only has to transmit the one channel that you are currently watching.
