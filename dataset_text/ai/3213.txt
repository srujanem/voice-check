No, there is not a physical dollar (or other unit of money) for every dollar that is in the bank. When you deposit money into a bank, the bank keeps track of how much money you have in your account using a computer system. This is called "electronic money" or "digital money." You can use your debit card or online banking to access and spend the money that is in your bank account, but you don't need to have physical cash in your hand to do so. In the past, banks used to keep a certain amount of physical cash (like paper bills and coins) on hand to give out to customers who wanted to withdraw money from their accounts. But now, most transactions are done electronically, so there isn't always a need for physical money. However, it's important to note that banks are required to keep a certain amount of physical cash on hand in case there is a need for it. This is to make sure that people who want to withdraw cash from their accounts can get it, even if there is a problem with the electronic banking system. So, while there might not be a physical dollar for every dollar in the bank, there is still a small amount of physical cash available for people to use if they need it.
