The concept of absolute zero, which is the temperature at which matter would have no thermal energy and would stop moving, was first proposed in the 19th century by scientists who were studying the behavior of gases. They noticed that as the temperature of a gas decreases, the pressure it exerts also decreases in a predictable way. By plotting these temperature and pressure measurements on a graph, they were able to create a curve called the "ideal gas law," which allows us to predict the behavior of gases at different temperatures.\n\nUsing this curve and other information about the behavior of gases, scientists were able to estimate that absolute zero would be around -273.15°C. However, they couldn't directly measure the temperature at absolute zero because it is impossible to cool any substance to that temperature using ordinary methods.\n\nInstead, scientists had to use various indirect methods to estimate the temperature of absolute zero. For example, they could measure the temperature at which a gas would stop moving and use this measurement to estimate the temperature of absolute zero. They could also study the behavior of certain materials as they are cooled to extremely low temperatures and use this information to make an estimate of absolute zero.\n\nOverall, while we may not be able to directly measure the temperature of absolute zero, we have developed a good understanding of it based on the behavior of gases and other materials at very low temperatures.
