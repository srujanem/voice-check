When a movie is filmed, it is usually captured on high-quality film or digital cameras. This master copy of the movie is then used to create all of the other copies that are distributed to theaters and sold to consumers on DVD, Blu-ray, or digital platforms. To create an HD version of a movie that was filmed decades ago, the studio will start with the original master copy of the film and use modern equipment to scan it and digitize it. This process involves taking high-resolution images of each frame of the film and turning them into digital files. Once the movie has been digitized, it can be cleaned up and restored to remove any damage or imperfections that may have occurred over time. The movie can also be color corrected and re-graded to make it look as good as possible. Finally, the movie can be encoded in a format like 1080p or 4K, which are high-definition resolutions that provide a much clearer and more detailed picture than standard-definition formats like DVD. So to sum it up, studios can release HD versions of movies that were made decades ago by starting with the original master copy, digitizing it, cleaning it up, and encoding it in a high-definition format. This allows them to make the movie look as good as possible for modern audiences.
