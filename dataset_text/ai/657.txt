There is no law that requires employees to give two weeks' notice before quitting their job. However, it is a common practice for employees to give their employer some advance notice before leaving their job, especially if they have been with the company for a while. This is because it gives the employer time to find a replacement for the departing employee and to make any necessary arrangements. 
On the other hand, there is no law that requires employers to give their employees advance notice before firing them. However, some employers may choose to do so out of a sense of fairness or to help the employee transition to a new job. In some cases, employers may be required by their own company policies or by collective bargaining agreements to give their employees a certain amount of notice before terminating their employment. 
In general, the rules around notice periods and employment are different for employees and employers because they are in different positions in the employer-employee relationship. Employees are generally free to leave their job whenever they choose, while employers have more control over the terms of employment and can make decisions about hiring and firing.
