Fresh cut grass smells strong because it releases a chemical called green leaf volatiles (GLVs) when it is cut. These chemicals are produced by the grass and give it its distinctive smell. The grass only releases these chemicals when it is cut because it is a defense mechanism. When the grass is cut, it is damaged and the GLVs are released as a way for the grass to protect itself from being eaten by insects or animals. The strong smell of the GLVs can help to deter these creatures from eating the grass.
