When you lay down in bed at night and experience a change in your depth perception, it could be due to a phenomenon called "floaters." Floaters are small specks or clouds that move around in your field of vision. They are actually small clumps of gel or cells inside the fluid in your eye, and they cast shadows on your retina (the light-sensitive layer at the back of your eye). Sometimes, when you are in a dark room and your eyes are relaxed, these floaters may be more noticeable to you. This can cause the objects around you to appear farther away or blurry. Floaters are usually harmless, but if you notice a sudden increase in floaters or flashes of light in your vision, it's important to speak with an eye doctor to rule out any underlying health issues.
