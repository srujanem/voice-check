Planets are held in orbit around their star by the balance between the star's gravitational pull and the planet's own momentum. Imagine you are on a playground, swinging on a swing. As you swing back and forth, you are pulling against the Earth's gravitational pull with your own momentum. The Earth's gravity is what is keeping you from flying off into space, but your own momentum is what is keeping you from crashing into the ground.\n\nThe same thing happens with planets orbiting their star. The star's gravity is pulling the planet towards it, but the planet is also moving really fast in its orbit. This combination of gravity and momentum keeps the planet in a stable orbit around the star, rather than crashing into it.\n\nEven though the star's gravity is constantly pulling the planet towards it, the planet is able to maintain its distance from the star because of its momentum. As long as the planet has enough momentum, it will continue to orbit the star rather than being pulled into it.\n\nI hope this helps to explain things! Do you have any other questions?\n
