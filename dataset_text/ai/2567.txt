During a recession, cutting government spending (also known as austerity) can be harmful because it can lead to a decrease in demand for goods and services, which can further weaken the economy. When people have less money to spend and businesses have fewer customers, they may be forced to lay off workers or even go out of business. This can lead to a vicious cycle, where less demand leads to more layoffs, which leads to even less demand. On the other hand, increasing government spending, especially on things like infrastructure, can help stimulate the economy by creating jobs and increasing demand for goods and services. This is often referred to as "fiscal policy." So, in short, cutting government spending during a recession can be harmful because it can weaken the economy further, while increasing government spending can help stimulate the economy and promote growth.
