When the Solar System formed about 4.5 billion years ago, a cloud of gas and dust started to collapse under its own gravity. As it collapsed, the material in the cloud started to spin faster and faster, kind of like how an ice skater spins faster when they bring their arms in close to their body. As it spun, the material flattened out into a disk shape, and the planets formed from this disk of material. Since all the material in the disk was moving in the same direction and on the same plane, the planets also formed on the same plane and all orbit the Sun in roughly the same direction. This is why all the planets in the Solar System orbit the Sun in nearly the same plane and in the same direction.
