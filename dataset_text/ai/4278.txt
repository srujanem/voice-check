A hash is a way of taking some data, such as a password, and turning it into a fixed-size string of characters that represents the original data. It's like taking a word and turning it into a code. The hash is generated using a specific set of rules, so if you know the rules and have the original data, you can always generate the same hash.\n\nHowever, the hash is considered irreversible because it's very difficult to figure out what the original data was just by looking at the hash. Imagine you have a code that represents the word "password", but you don't know what the word is. It would be very difficult to figure out what the original word was just by looking at the code.\n\nThis is useful for storing passwords because it means that even if someone gets access to the list of hashes, they still wouldn't be able to easily figure out what the original passwords were. It would be like trying to guess the words that the codes represent, which would be very difficult.\n
