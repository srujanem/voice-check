Canned food lasts longer than other types of food because it is treated with heat to kill bacteria and other microorganisms that can cause spoilage. The heat treatment, called "sterilization," is done in a special machine called a "canner."\n\nAfter the food is placed in a can, the can is sealed shut and then heated in the canner. The heat from the canner helps to kill any bacteria or microorganisms that may be in the food or on the inside of the can.\n\nThe heat also helps to create a vacuum inside the can, which helps to keep air out. When there is less air inside the can, it is harder for bacteria and other microorganisms to grow and multiply, which helps to keep the food fresh for a longer period of time.\n\nOverall, the process of canning food helps to preserve it by eliminating bacteria and other microorganisms, and by creating a vacuum inside the can that keeps air out.
