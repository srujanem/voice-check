Leibnizian monads are a concept that was developed by the philosopher Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz in the 17th century. According to Leibniz, the universe is made up of tiny, indivisible units called monads, which are like the building blocks of the universe. These monads are supposed to be the most basic units of reality, and they are what make up everything in the universe, including matter, energy, and even consciousness. Monads are different from atoms in a number of ways. Atoms are the basic units of matter, and they are made up of protons, neutrons, and electrons. Monads, on the other hand, are not made up of anything and are not physical in the same way that atoms are. Instead, monads are more like ideas or concepts that exist in the mind. Monads are also indivisible, which means that they cannot be broken down into smaller parts, whereas atoms can be broken down into smaller particles. So, to sum up, Leibnizian monads are like the building blocks of the universe because they are the most basic units of reality. They are different from atoms because they are not physical and cannot be broken down into smaller parts.
