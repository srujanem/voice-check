Black holes are objects in space that are so massive and dense that they create a "hole" in space-time. Imagine space-time as a stretchy fabric, and a black hole is like a very heavy rock that has been placed on the fabric. The rock is so heavy that it creates a deep dent or "hole" in the fabric. If you were to throw a ball near the "hole," the ball would roll towards the "hole" because of the dent in the fabric. This is similar to how objects are attracted to a black hole. Black holes are not solid objects like a rock, but rather they are regions of space where the normal laws of physics break down. They are formed when a very massive star collapses in on itself at the end of its life. The gravity of the black hole is so strong that it sucks in anything that comes too close to it, including light! This is why they are called "black" holes, because they are so massive that they don't allow any light to escape their gravitational pull. Black holes come in different sizes. The smallest black holes, called "stellar" black holes, are about the same mass as our Sun but are packed into a region the size of a small city. The largest black holes, called "supermassive" black holes, are millions or billions of times more massive than our Sun and can be found at the center of most galaxies, including our own Milky Way.
