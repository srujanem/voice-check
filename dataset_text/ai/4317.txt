Great question! Magnets have two ends called poles, one called the north pole and the other called the south pole. Opposite poles of magnets are attracted to each other, like the north pole of one magnet being attracted to the south pole of another magnet. This is because opposite poles have opposite charges and opposite charges attract each other.\n\nOn the other hand, when you try to push two magnets together with the same poles facing each other (north-north or south-south), the magnets will push against each other and it will feel harder to push them together. This is because the same poles of magnets are repelled by each other. Just like opposite charges attract each other, the same charges repel each other. So the north pole of one magnet is repelled by the north pole of another magnet, and the south pole of one magnet is repelled by the south pole of another magnet. This repelling force is stronger than the attracting force between opposite poles, which is why it feels harder to push magnets together with the same poles facing each other.\n\nI hope that helps to explain it!
