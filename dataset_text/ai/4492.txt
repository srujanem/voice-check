Rubbing the rim of a glass with your finger produces a sound because it causes the glass to vibrate. When you rub your finger around the rim of the glass, you create friction which makes the glass vibrate. These vibrations create sound waves that travel through the air and reach your ear, allowing you to hear the sound.\n\nThink of it like this: when you rub your finger around the rim of the glass, it's like you're giving the glass a little push. The glass starts to move back and forth very quickly, and these quick movements create sound waves. Just like when you clap your hands or blow a whistle, the sound waves travel through the air and reach your ear, allowing you to hear the sound.
