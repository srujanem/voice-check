When blood comes into contact with air, it begins to dry and change color. This is because the oxygen in the air reacts with the iron in the red blood cells, causing the blood to turn from a bright red color to a brownish color.\n\nAs the blood dries, the water in it evaporates, leaving behind the solid parts of the blood, such as red blood cells and proteins. These solid parts are what give the dried blood its brown color.\n\nSo, when you see an old bandage with brown blood on it, it's because the blood on the bandage has been exposed to air for a long time and has had a chance to dry and change color.
