Golf rangefinders with "slope" feature use lasers to measure the distance to a target and also use an inclinometer to measure the angle of the slope between the rangefinder and the target.\n\nTo understand how a rangefinder with slope works, imagine that you are standing at the top of a hill and you want to measure the distance to a flagstick at the bottom of the hill. The rangefinder will shoot a laser beam to the flagstick and measure the time it takes for the beam to bounce back. From this time, the rangefinder can calculate the distance to the flagstick using the speed of light.\n\nNow, suppose that the flagstick is not at the same elevation as you. In this case, the rangefinder will also use an inclinometer to measure the angle of the slope between you and the flagstick. The inclinometer works like a spirit level that you might use to hang a picture straight on a wall. It measures the angle of the slope by detecting the force of gravity.\n\nUsing the distance and slope measurements, the rangefinder can then calculate the true distance to the flagstick, taking into account the change in elevation. This true distance is called the "slope-adjusted distance," and it is the distance you should use to plan your shot.\n\nI hope this helps! Let me know if you have any more questions.
