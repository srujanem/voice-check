Sure! A kettle is a device that you can use to heat water. It has a handle, a spout, and a base that you plug into an outlet. Inside the kettle, there is a heating element that gets hot when you turn on the kettle. When you fill the kettle with water and turn it on, the heating element starts to heat up the water. As the water gets hotter and hotter, it starts to turn into steam. When the water gets hot enough, it reaches a temperature called the boiling point, which is about 100°C (212°F). At this temperature, the water turns into steam and bubbles start to form in the water. Now, here's the cool part: the kettle has a special part called a thermostat that can sense when the water is getting hot. When the water reaches the boiling point, the thermostat sends a signal to the kettle to turn off the heating element. This is how the kettle knows when the water is boiling. So, in short, a kettle works by using a heating element to heat up the water, and a thermostat to sense when the water has reached the boiling point and turn off the heat. Does that make sense?
