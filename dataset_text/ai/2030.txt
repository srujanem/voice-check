Electrons and protons are both types of subatomic particles, and they have properties that allow them to behave in certain ways. Electrons are much lighter than protons and are also negatively charged, while protons are much heavier and are positively charged. When two particles have opposite charges, they tend to attract each other, while particles with the same charge tend to repel each other. Because the electrons in an atom are negatively charged and the protons in the nucleus are positively charged, the electrons are attracted to the nucleus. However, electrons don't just fall into the nucleus because they also have a property called "spin," which makes them behave like tiny magnets. This spin causes the electrons to sort of "orbit" around the nucleus, rather than falling into it. As for the protons in the nucleus, they do have a tendency to repel each other because they have the same positive charge. However, they are also held together by a force called the "strong nuclear force," which is a very strong force that acts between the protons and helps to keep them together in the nucleus.
