The distance that you can see before your vision "drops off" is called the horizon. The horizon is the line where the Earth's surface and the sky seem to meet.\n\nThe distance to the horizon depends on several factors, including your height above the ground and the curvature of the Earth. If you are standing on a flat plane and you are tall enough, you can see quite far. For example, if you are standing on a flat plane and you are 6 feet (1.8 meters) tall, the horizon will be about 3 miles (4.8 kilometers) away.\n\nHowever, if you are standing on a hill or a mountain, you will be able to see even farther because you are higher up. From the top of a mountain that is 8,000 feet (2,400 meters) tall, you can see the horizon about 200 miles (320 kilometers) away.\n\nAs you get closer to the horizon, objects will appear to get smaller and smaller because they are farther away. Eventually, you will reach a point where you can no longer see any details on the objects because they are too far away. At this point, the horizon will appear to be a straight line where the Earth's surface and the sky meet.
