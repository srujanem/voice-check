The Watergate scandal was a political scandal in the United States that occurred during the presidency of Richard Nixon. It was the result of a break-in at the Democratic National Committee (DNC) headquarters at the Watergate office complex in Washington, D.C. in 1972, and the subsequent cover-up of the administration's involvement by the Nixon administration. Here's a simplified explanation of what happened: Some people working for Nixon's campaign broke into the DNC office to get information about the Democrats' campaign plans. The people who broke in were caught, and it was discovered that they had been paid by the Nixon campaign. Nixon tried to cover up the break-in and the connection to his campaign. He ordered people to lie about what had happened and to destroy evidence. Some of Nixon's staff members refused to go along with the cover-up and told the truth to the FBI and to a special court that was investigating the case. As more and more information came out, it became clear that Nixon had been involved in the break-in and the cover-up. The scandal was a big deal, and many people in the United States were upset with Nixon. Eventually, Nixon resigned as President rather than face impeachment (a process where a President can be removed from office by Congress).
