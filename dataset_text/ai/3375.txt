Airlines require you to turn off your electronics during certain parts of the flight because they can interfere with the plane's navigation and communication systems. Imagine you're driving in your car and you have a GPS system to help you get where you're going. The GPS sends signals to your car telling it how to get to your destination. Now imagine that there are a bunch of other cars around you, all with their own GPS systems, and they're all sending signals at the same time. It would be really hard for your GPS to give you accurate directions because it would be getting so many conflicting signals. It's the same with planes. The plane has a bunch of electronics on board that help the pilots navigate and communicate with air traffic control. When you have a lot of phones, tablets, and other electronics turned on, it can be like a bunch of cars all trying to use their GPS at the same time. It can be hard for the plane's electronics to work properly, so the airline asks you to turn off your electronics to make sure everything runs smoothly.
