The Governor of Texas is able to call for special sessions of the Texas legislature, which are meetings that occur outside of the regular legislative session. The governor has the authority to do this because the Texas Constitution gives him the power to convene the legislature in special session when necessary. During a special session, the legislature can only consider issues that the governor specifies in the call for the session. The governor can call for as many special sessions as he wants, and can specify different issues to be discussed in each session. It's important to note that the legislature is not required to pass any laws during a special session, and can choose to adjourn (end the session) without taking any action. The governor does not have the power to force the legislature to pass a specific bill or take a certain action.
