A semicolon is a punctuation mark that is used to join two independent clauses in a sentence. An independent clause is a group of words that can stand alone as a sentence, because it has a subject and a verb and expresses a complete thought. Here's an example of two independent clauses joined with a semicolon: "I went to the store; I bought some milk." In this sentence, the first independent clause is "I went to the store," and the second independent clause is "I bought some milk." The semicolon separates the two clauses and shows that they are related. You can also use a semicolon to separate items in a list if the items in the list are long or contain commas. For example: "I need to buy bread, butter, and cheese; eggs, bacon, and sausage; and some fruit for breakfast." So, when you're using a semicolon, make sure you have two independent clauses that you want to join, or items in a list that you want to separate.
