Radioactivity is the process by which an atom becomes unstable and releases energy as it decays into a more stable form. This happens because the nucleus of the atom, which is made up of protons and neutrons, is not perfectly balanced. Some atoms, called isotopes, are more stable than others because they have a different number of protons and neutrons in their nucleus. 
When an atom is radioactive, it means that the nucleus is unstable and will eventually break apart, or decay. This process releases energy and sometimes particles, such as alpha particles or beta particles, which are types of radiation. 
At sites like Chernobyl, what is left behind after the radioactive atoms decay is called nuclear waste. This waste is still radioactive and can be dangerous to people and the environment if it is not properly stored and handled. 
Not all atoms or isotopes are radioactive. Some isotopes are stable and do not decay, while others are only slightly radioactive and take a very long time to decay. The stability of an atom or isotope depends on the balance of protons and neutrons in its nucleus. If there are too many or too few of either one, the atom can become unstable and start to decay.
