Plants convert sunlight into chemical energy through photosynthesis. This process occurs in the chloroplasts and involves two stages: the light-dependent reactions and the Calvin cycle. During the light-dependent reactions, water molecules are split, releasing oxygen as a byproduct. The Calvin cycle uses carbon dioxide from the atmosphere to produce glucose, which serves as the primary energy source for the plant. It is also important to consider the broader societal and environmental implications associated with photosynthesis.
