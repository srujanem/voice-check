Free trade is a type of economic policy that allows countries to buy and sell goods and services with each other without any special taxes or tariffs. This means that it's easier and cheaper for companies to sell their products in other countries and for people to buy products from other countries. The North American Free Trade Agreement (NAFTA) is a free trade agreement between the United States, Canada, and Mexico. It was signed in 1992 and went into effect in 1994. The goal of NAFTA was to reduce tariffs and other trade barriers between the three countries and make it easier for companies to do business with each other. Some people think that free trade is a good idea because it can help countries grow their economies and create more jobs. However, others believe that free trade can hurt some workers and industries in a country, because it allows companies to move their operations to other countries where labor is cheaper. This can lead to job losses and lower wages for workers in the country where the jobs are moved. This is one reason why free trade can be controversial.
