Catch-22 is a term that comes from a novel called "Catch-22" written by Joseph Heller. In the novel, Catch-22 is a rule that says that a person can't be considered crazy if they know they are crazy, but if they don't think they are crazy, then they must be crazy. This creates a paradox, or a situation where there is no solution because the options are contradictory. Here's an example of how Catch-22 might work: Imagine that a soldier wants to be declared crazy so that he can be excused from duty and go home. In order to be declared crazy, he would have to go to the doctor and ask to be declared crazy. But if he goes to the doctor and asks to be declared crazy, it shows that he is aware of his own sanity and therefore cannot be considered crazy. So he is stuck in a situation where he can't be considered crazy no matter what he does. Catch-22 is often used to describe situations where there is no good solution because the options available are all equally undesirable or difficult. It can also be used to describe situations where a person is trapped by their own thoughts or actions.
