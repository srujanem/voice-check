Water is almost incompressible, which means that it doesn't compress or get smaller under pressure. This is because the molecules in water are already packed very closely together, so there isn't much room for them to move closer together and compress the water. Other fluids, like air, can be compressed because the molecules are farther apart and have more room to move closer together. This is why you can compress air by squeezing a balloon, for example. To understand why water is almost incompressible, imagine a container filled with bouncy balls. If you try to squeeze the balls together, they will just bounce off each other and the container will not get smaller. This is similar to what happens when you try to compress water: the molecules just bounce off each other and the water stays the same size. However, it's important to note that water can be slightly compressed under very high pressures, such as when it is deep underwater. But for most practical purposes, water can be considered incompressible.
