On a gas stove, the flame is produced when gas flows from the gas line and is mixed with oxygen from the air. The gas is ignited by a spark from the stove's ignition system, which is usually a small electrical device that generates a spark when you turn the burner knob to "light." 
The flame is contained within the burner and does not travel down the gas line because the gas is mixed with just the right amount of oxygen to produce a flame that is stable and does not spread. The burner also has a design that helps contain the flame, such as a small metal ring around the edge of the burner that helps direct the flame upwards. 
So, in short, the flame on a gas stove is kept from traveling down the gas line because the gas is mixed with the right amount of oxygen, and the burner is designed to contain the flame.
