During wartime, countries often allocate a lot of resources towards developing new technologies that can give them an advantage in the war. This might include things like new weapons, better ways to communicate, or ways to protect soldiers. Because the stakes are so high and the consequences of losing the war can be severe, there is often a lot of pressure to come up with new ideas and to make them a reality as quickly as possible. 
In peacetime, there is still progress and innovation, but it might not happen as quickly because there is not the same level of urgency or resources being dedicated towards it. People might also have more flexibility to work on projects at their own pace, rather than feeling the need to rush to complete them. 
Overall, the pace of technological advancement can depend on a variety of factors, including how much resources are being dedicated towards research and development, how much pressure there is to come up with new ideas, and how much competition there is to be the first to create something new.
