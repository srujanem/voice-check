The invention of the printing press by Johannes Gutenberg around 1440 transformed human society by dramatically accelerating the spread of knowledge. Prior to this innovation, books were painstakingly copied by hand, making them rare, expensive, and accessible only to elite institutions. Gutenberg's moveable type allowed texts to be mass-produced quickly and affordably, sparking an explosion in literacy rates across Europe. This democratizing force fueled the Renaissance, accelerated scientific advancement, and fundamentally shifted how information was preserved, shared, and debated across future generations.
