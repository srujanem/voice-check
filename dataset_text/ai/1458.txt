Atoms are never truly touching because they are constantly moving and vibrating, even when they seem to be stationary. However, even though atoms are not in direct contact, they can still interact with each other through the forces of attraction and repulsion. 
For example, when you hold onto an object, the atoms in your hand are attracted to the atoms in the object. These attractive forces allow the atoms in your hand to "stick" to the atoms in the object, even though they are not actually touching. 
Think of it like magnets. Even though the magnets are not physically touching, they can still attract or repel each other because of the forces between them. The same is true for atoms and the objects they make up. 
So even though atoms are not truly touching, they can still interact with each other through the forces of attraction and repulsion, which allows us to hold onto objects and keep them in place.
