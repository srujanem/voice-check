Chinese food in the United States has become standardized in part because many of the Chinese restaurants that you find in the United States are actually owned and operated by Chinese immigrants who came to the United States in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. These immigrants often came from a small region in southern China called Guangdong (also known as Canton), which is known for its cuisine that is characterized by a wide variety of seafood, meat, vegetables, and rice dishes.\n\nWhen these Chinese immigrants started opening restaurants in the United States, they wanted to serve food that was familiar to them and that reminded them of home. As a result, many of the dishes that you find on the menu at Chinese restaurants in the United States are actually from Guangdong and are not necessarily representative of the wide variety of regional cuisines that you find in China.\n\nOver time, these dishes have become popular in the United States, and as a result, many Chinese restaurants in the United States have adopted similar menus, which has contributed to the standardization of Chinese food in the United States.
