Color blindness, also known as color vision deficiency, is a condition that affects how people see colors. People with color blindness have difficulty seeing certain colors or distinguishing between certain colors, such as red and green or blue and yellow. This is because their eyes do not have certain types of cells called cones that help us see different colors. Most people have three types of cones in their eyes that help them see different colors. These cones are sensitive to red, green, and blue light. When light enters the eye, it passes through the lens and hits the retina, which is a layer of cells at the back of the eye. The retina has millions of light-sensitive cells called rods and cones. The rods help us see in low light and the cones help us see colors. In people with color blindness, one or more of these cone types is not working properly, so they have difficulty seeing certain colors. For example, if someone is red-green colorblind, it means that their red and green cones are not working correctly.
