The Tea Party movement was a political movement that emerged in the United States in the late 2000s. It was made up of people who felt that the government was overreaching and infringing on their personal freedoms, and that taxes were too high. The Tea Party movement took its name from the Boston Tea Party of 1773, which was a protest by American colonists against high taxes on tea imposed by the British government. Like the colonists in the Boston Tea Party, members of the Tea Party movement believed in limited government and individual liberty. Tea Party supporters often identified as conservatives and tended to support smaller government, lower taxes, and more individual freedom. They also tended to be opposed to abortion and same-sex marriage, and in favor of stricter immigration policies. The Tea Party movement was particularly active during the presidency of Barack Obama, and many Tea Party supporters were critical of Obama's policies. The movement declined in influence in later years, but some of its ideas and goals continue to be promoted by certain politicians and political groups.
