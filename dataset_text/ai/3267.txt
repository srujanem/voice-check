Classical mechanics is a theory that explains how objects move and behave based on classical principles, such as Newton's laws of motion. Quantum mechanics is a theory that explains how particles, such as atoms and subatomic particles, behave and interact with each other. The transition from classical mechanics to quantum mechanics happens when we start to consider objects or systems that are very small, such as atoms, electrons, and photons (particles of light). At these small scales, the rules of classical mechanics break down and the principles of quantum mechanics become important. To give you an idea of how small these objects are, an atom is about 100,000 times smaller than the width of a human hair. Electrons are even smaller, and photons are even smaller still. In summary, quantum mechanics is used to describe the behavior of very small particles, while classical mechanics is used to describe the behavior of larger objects.
