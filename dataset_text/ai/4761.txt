When you look at an object in a mirror, the light from that object travels to the mirror and is reflected back to your eyes. But the distance the light has to travel is longer than it would be if you were looking directly at the object. This is because the light has to bounce off the mirror and then travel to your eyes.\n\nBecause the light has to travel a longer distance, it becomes spread out and less focused. This is why objects in mirrors appear blurry, even if the mirror is flat.\n\nNearsightedness, or myopia, is a condition where you have trouble seeing things that are far away. This is because the shape of your eye is such that the light coming into your eye doesn't focus correctly on your retina, which is the part of your eye that senses light. When the light doesn't focus correctly on your retina, it can make things appear blurry.\n\nSo, when you look at an object in a mirror, the light from that object is already spread out because it has to travel a longer distance. If you are also nearsighted, this can make the image in the mirror even more blurry because the light isn't focusing correctly on your retina.
