Sure! The Standard Model of particle physics is a theory that explains how particles and forces work in the universe. Quarks are very small particles that make up protons and neutrons, which are particles found in the nucleus (center) of an atom. There are six different types of quarks, and they have a property called "spin." Leptons are also very small particles, but they are different from quarks. There are six types of leptons, too. Leptons don't have spin like quarks do, but they do have a property called "flavor." Bosons are particles that help transmit forces between other particles. There are four types of bosons: the photon, the W and Z bosons, and the gluon. Together, quarks, leptons, and bosons make up all the matter and forces in the universe. Scientists use the Standard Model to try to understand how these particles and forces work and how they fit together.
