Patents are a form of intellectual property that give the owner the exclusive right to make, use, and sell an invention for a certain period of time. This period of time is typically 20 years from the date that the patent application was filed. After the patent expires, anyone is free to make, use, and sell the invention without needing permission from the patent owner. The idea behind this is that patents are meant to encourage innovation by giving inventors a temporary monopoly on their ideas. This provides inventors with the opportunity to recoup their investment in developing the invention and to make a profit from it. After the patent expires, the invention enters the public domain, meaning that anyone can use it freely. This allows other inventors to build upon the original invention and create new and improved versions, which can also be patented. So, when you read that "patents will expire," it means that the exclusive rights granted to the patent owner will come to an end after a certain period of time. This allows others to use the invention and potentially create new and improved versions of it.
