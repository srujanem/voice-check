To measure the height of mountains, scientists and surveyors use a tool called a surveyor's level, which is a small telescope mounted on a tripod. They set up the surveyor's level at the base of the mountain, and aim it at a point on the mountain's peak. They then use a system of lenses and mirrors to measure the angle between the ground and the top of the mountain. By using math, they can calculate the distance between the base of the mountain and the peak.\n\nTo measure the depth of the sea, scientists and surveyors use a tool called a sounding line, which is a long rope with markers on it. They attach a weight to the end of the sounding line and lower it into the water. As the weight sinks, the markers on the sounding line slide down, and the depth of the water is measured by how far down the weight goes.\n\nThere is no specific point on Earth that is considered "ground level," but there is a reference point called sea level, which is the average level of the surface of the sea. Scientists and surveyors use sea level as a reference point when measuring the height of mountains or the depth of the sea.
