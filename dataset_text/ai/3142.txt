The moon looks larger when it is close to the horizon because of an optical illusion called the "moon illusion." This illusion is caused by the way our brains interpret the size of objects in the sky. When the moon is high in the sky, it appears smaller because it is farther away from objects on the ground, such as trees and buildings. Our brains are used to seeing objects that are close to us as being larger, and objects that are farther away as being smaller. However, when the moon is close to the horizon, it appears to be the same size as objects on the ground, such as trees and buildings. Our brains interpret this as the moon being much larger than it really is, which is why it looks so much bigger when it is close to the horizon. So, to sum it up, the moon illusion is caused by the way our brains interpret the size of objects in the sky based on their distance from objects on the ground.
