Shoplifting detectors, also known as electronic article surveillance (EAS) systems, use radio frequency (RF) technology to detect when an item that has not been properly paid for is being taken out of the store.\n\nHere's how it works: when you enter a store that has an EAS system, you'll notice that some of the items for sale, like clothes or electronics, have small, hard tags attached to them. These tags contain a tiny electronic chip that gives off a unique radio frequency signal. When you pay for an item and leave the store, you'll walk through a set of gates or doors that contain RF antennas. These antennas send out a radio frequency signal that activates the chip in the tag. If the chip is not activated, it means that the item has not been paid for and the EAS system will set off an alarm to alert store staff.\n\nSo, to answer your question, the EAS system is able to detect when an item that has not been paid for is being taken out of the store because it is looking for the unique radio frequency signal being emitted by the chip in the tag. If the signal is not present, it means that the item has not been paid for and the alarm will go off.
