Stephen Hawking was a renowned scientist and mathematician who lived with a condition called amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, or ALS, for many years. This is a serious neurological condition that affects the nerve cells in the brain and spinal cord that control muscle movement. People with ALS often experience weakness and difficulty moving their muscles, and the condition can progress quickly, leading to difficulty speaking, swallowing, and breathing.\n\nWhile the average life expectancy for people with ALS is around 5 to 10 years, some people with the condition can live much longer. In Hawking's case, he was diagnosed with ALS in 1963 when he was just 21 years old and he lived with the condition for over 50 years. This is quite unusual, but it is possible for some people with ALS to survive for many years, sometimes even decades.\n\nThere are several factors that can affect a person's prognosis with ALS, including the specific type of ALS they have, their age at diagnosis, and their overall health. Hawking received excellent medical care throughout his life and he also had a strong will to live and continue his work as a scientist. This, along with his unique and determined personality, may have contributed to his survival with ALS for such a long time.
