Math is a powerful tool that helps us understand and solve problems in the world around us. In the case of sending people to the moon, math was used in many different ways. 
For example, math was used to design the rocket that would take the astronauts to the moon. Engineers used math to calculate how much fuel the rocket would need, how much the rocket would weigh, and how much force the rocket's engines would need to produce in order to lift off the ground and reach the moon. 
Math was also used to plan the moon landing itself. Astronauts and mission control used math to figure out the best time and place to land on the moon, as well as how to safely get the astronauts back to Earth. 
Finally, math was used to communicate with the astronauts and the spacecraft. Scientists and engineers used math to send messages to the astronauts and to track the spacecraft's position as it flew through space. 
Overall, math played a crucial role in making the moon landing possible, and it continues to be an important tool in space exploration and many other fields.
