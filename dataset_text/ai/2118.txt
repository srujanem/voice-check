Empire building nations, such as the Ottoman Empire and the British Empire, often justified their actions by claiming that they were bringing civilization, Christianity, or modernity to the people they conquered. They also sometimes claimed that they were fighting against savage or barbaric peoples and were justified in taking over their lands. However, these justifications were often used to mask the true reasons for empire building, which were often economic or strategic. Empires often wanted to control resources, land, and trade routes, and they used their military and political power to expand their territory and influence. In many cases, the conquest and colonization of other lands and peoples was wrong because it involved violence, exploitation, and the denial of basic human rights. The people who were conquered often had their lands and resources taken from them, and they were often treated unfairly and oppressed. In the Ottoman Empire, for example, non-Muslim minorities such as Christians and Jews were often treated unfairly and discriminated against. In the British Empire, native peoples in places like India and Africa were often treated unfairly and subjected to harsh and exploitative policies. Overall, empire building can be justified in some cases, such as when it is done to protect people from harm or to promote peace and cooperation. However, it is often wrong when it involves the exploitation, oppression, and abuse of other peoples and their lands.
