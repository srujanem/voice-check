Fire is a chemical reaction that happens when a material reacts with oxygen in the air and produces heat, light, and flames. Some materials, like wood and paper, are flammable, which means that they can catch fire easily. Other materials, like metal and glass, are not flammable, which means that they do not catch fire easily or at all. The reason that some materials are flammable and others are not has to do with the way their atoms are arranged. When a material is heated up, the atoms in that material start to move around more and more. If the atoms in a material are arranged in a way that allows them to react with oxygen easily, then the material will be flammable. If the atoms are not arranged in a way that allows them to react with oxygen easily, then the material will not be flammable. Heat is one way to start a fire, but it is not the only way. A spark or a flame can also start a fire if it comes into contact with a flammable material. Once a fire starts, it can continue to burn as long as there is a supply of oxygen and a flammable material to burn.
