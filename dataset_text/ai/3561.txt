Sure, I'd be happy to explain the double-slit experiment to you. Imagine that you have a wall with two small slits in it, and you shine a flashlight on the wall. The light from the flashlight will go through the slits and create two bright spots on the wall behind it. Now imagine that instead of using a flashlight, you use a laser pointer and shine it on the wall with the two slits. The laser will also create two bright spots on the wall behind it. Now, let's say that you have a camera set up to take a picture of the wall every time the laser is turned on. When you turn on the laser, the camera will take a picture of the two bright spots on the wall. But here's the interesting part: if you turn on the laser for just a short time, the camera will only see one bright spot on the wall. It's as if the laser beam split into two separate beams that went through both slits, and then came back together again to create the bright spot. This is what is meant by the "interference" of the light particles in the double-slit experiment. When the light is observed (in this case, by the camera), it behaves like a particle and creates a single bright spot on the wall. But when the light is not observed, it behaves like a wave and creates two separate bright spots on the wall. I hope this helps to clarify things for you! Let me know if you have any more questions.
