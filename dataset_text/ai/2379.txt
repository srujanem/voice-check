When you insert a USB drive into your computer, the computer reads and writes data to the drive. This means that the computer is constantly communicating with the drive and using it to store and retrieve information. If you remove the drive without properly "ejecting" it first, you risk disrupting this communication and potentially damaging the data on the drive. Think of it like a phone call. If you hang up the phone without saying goodbye, the other person might not know that you're done talking and could keep talking to you even though you're not there to hear them. Ejecting the drive is like saying goodbye to the drive before you hang up the phone. It lets the computer know that you're finished using the drive and it can stop communicating with it. By ejecting the drive before physically removing it, you're giving the computer a chance to finish any pending operations and safely disconnect from the drive. This helps to prevent data loss and potential damage to the drive or your computer.
