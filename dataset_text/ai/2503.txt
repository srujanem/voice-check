Sure! A processor, also known as a Central Processing Unit (CPU), is the "brain" of a computer. It is responsible for carrying out instructions that tell the computer what to do. The processor works by using tiny electronic switches called transistors to carry out the instructions. These transistors are arranged in a specific pattern on a small, flat piece of material called a microchip. The processor has a clock that ticks, or counts, at a very fast rate. The clock is measured in GHz, or gigahertz, which is a unit of frequency. The higher the GHz, the faster the processor can work. When you open a program on your computer, the processor reads the instructions for that program and carries them out. It does this over and over again, very quickly, until the program is finished.
