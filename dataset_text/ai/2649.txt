ATP (adenosine triphosphate) is a molecule that stores energy in its bonds. When our cells need energy, they use ATP to power various processes. The energy in ATP comes from the third bond between the second and third phosphate groups. When this bond is broken, energy is released and can be used by the cell. One way that ATP gives energy to our cells is through the sodium-potassium pump. This is a protein that moves sodium ions out of cells and potassium ions into cells. It uses energy from ATP to do this. When the sodium-potassium pump is active, it uses up ATP molecules and releases the energy stored in them to power the movement of the ions. So, to summarize, ATP stores energy in its bonds and releases it when those bonds are broken. This energy can be used by our cells to power various processes, such as the sodium-potassium pump.
