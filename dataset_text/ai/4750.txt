Iron is attracted to magnets, but the amount of iron in your blood is very small. The iron in your blood is carried inside red blood cells, which are too small to be affected by magnets. Even if you had a very strong magnet, it would not be able to pull the iron out of your red blood cells.\n\nYour circulatory system, which is made up of your heart, blood vessels, and blood, is an important part of your body that helps to keep you healthy. It is responsible for pumping blood to all the cells in your body, providing them with the oxygen and nutrients they need to function properly. Magnets do not interfere with this process and do not cause any problems for your circulatory system.\n\nI hope this helps to explain why magnets do not affect your blood or your circulatory system.
