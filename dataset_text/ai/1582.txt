Mushroom clouds are formed when an explosion occurs and a large amount of material is rapidly propelled into the air. The shape of the mushroom cloud is determined by the amount and size of the material being propelled into the air, as well as the temperature and pressure of the explosion. 
Nuclear weapons create mushroom clouds that are more mushroom-like because they produce a much larger explosion than other types of explosives. The explosion is so powerful that it can propel a huge amount of material into the air, including dirt, dust, and debris from the ground. This material mixes with the hot gases produced by the explosion and rises up into the atmosphere, forming the characteristic mushroom shape. 
Other types of explosives, such as bombs or dynamite, do not produce explosions that are as large or as powerful as nuclear weapons, so the mushroom clouds they create are not as mushroom-like. They may still have some mushroom-like features, but they will not be as pronounced as the clouds produced by nuclear weapons.
