Pitcher plants are carnivorous plants that use their slippery, deep, cup-like leaves called "pitchers" to trap and digest insects and other small animals. These plants have special enzymes in their pitchers that help to break down and digest the insects and other small animals that they trap. However, if a non-living object like a small marble were to fall into a pitcher plant, the plant would not be able to digest it. Instead, the plant would simply keep the object in its pitcher until it decays or is otherwise removed.\n\nTo remove the object, the plant would have to rely on natural processes such as wind, rain, or the movement of animals to help knock the object out of the pitcher. Alternatively, a person could manually remove the object from the pitcher if they wanted to.\n\nOverall, a small marble would not harm a pitcher plant, but it also would not be able to be digested by the plant.
