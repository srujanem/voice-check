Immanuel Kant was a philosopher who lived in the late 18th and early 19th centuries. His philosophy is known as Kantianism. Kant believed that there are certain things that are universal and true for all people, regardless of their individual experiences or backgrounds. These things, he believed, are known as "a priori" knowledge, meaning that they are known independently of experience. One of Kant's most famous ideas is the "categorical imperative," which is a moral rule that says that people should act in a way that they would want everyone else to act. In other words, people should treat others with respect and kindness, and not just do things that benefit themselves. Kant also believed in the importance of human reason and the use of logic to understand the world. He thought that people should use their own minds to think for themselves, rather than blindly following the beliefs of others. Overall, Kant's philosophy emphasizes the importance of moral behavior and the use of reason in understanding the world.
