When the government wants to put more money into circulation, it can do so by printing new physical currency or by creating new digital currency. The government then distributes this new money to banks, which can then lend it out to people and businesses. For example, let's say the government prints a new $100 bill and gives it to a bank. The bank can then lend this $100 to someone who needs it, like a person looking to buy a new car. The person can then use the $100 to pay for the car, and the car dealership can use the $100 to pay its employees or buy new inventory. As this $100 bill changes hands and is used to buy and sell different goods and services, it begins to circulate throughout the economy. It's important to note that not all money is physical cash like coins and bills. Much of the money in circulation today exists only digitally, as electronic records of transactions.
