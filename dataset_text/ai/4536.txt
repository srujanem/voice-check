Nighttime rainbows, also known as moonbows, are similar to regular rainbows that occur during the day, but they happen at night and are much fainter. They are created when light is refracted, or bent, as it passes through water droplets in the air.\n\nTo see a moonbow, you need three things: moonlight, water droplets, and something for the moonlight to be refracted off of, like mist or spray from a waterfall. When the moonlight passes through the water droplets, it is bent and separated into different colors, just like a regular rainbow. The colors of a moonbow are usually not as vibrant as those of a daytime rainbow because the moonlight is not as bright as sunlight.\n\nTo see a moonbow, you need to be standing somewhere where the moonlight is shining through the water droplets, like at the base of a waterfall on a clear night. If you are facing the moon, you might see a faint, white rainbow in the sky. If you are looking away from the moon, the rainbow might be harder to see because it will be fainter.\n\nI hope that helps! Let me know if you have any other questions.
