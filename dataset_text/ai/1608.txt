A tax bracket is a range of income that is taxed at a specific rate. In the United States, the federal government has a progressive tax system, which means that people who earn more money pay a higher percentage of their income in taxes. The tax brackets are set up so that people who earn more money pay a higher tax rate on the portion of their income that falls within a higher tax bracket. For example, let's say that the tax brackets for a single person are as follows: 10% on the first $9,950 of taxable income, 12% on taxable income between $9,951 and $40,525, 22% on taxable income between $40,526 and $86,375, 24% on taxable income between $86,376 and $164,925, 32% on taxable income between $164,926 and $209,425, 35% on taxable income between $209,426 and $523,600, 37% on taxable income over $523,600, If you earn $50,000 in a year, you would pay 10% tax on the first $9,950 of your income, which comes to $995. You would then pay 12% tax on the remaining $40,050 of your income, which comes to $4,806. Your total tax bill would be $995 + $4,806 = $5,801. So, to summarize, a tax bracket is a range of income that is taxed at a specific rate, and the tax rate increases as the income level increases.
