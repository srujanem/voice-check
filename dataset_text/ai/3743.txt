The universe is not necessarily shaped like a sphere, and it doesn't have an "outside" or a boundary in the way you might be thinking. Instead, scientists believe that the universe is expanding, which means that the space between galaxies is getting bigger. Imagine that you have a piece of bread, and you put a raisin on it. If you put the bread in the oven and bake it, the dough will rise and the raisin will move further away from all the other raisins on the bread. In the same way, as the universe expands, the galaxies within it move farther apart. It's important to note that the expansion of the universe is not happening in any particular direction. It's not like the galaxies are moving away from a central point and into some other place. Instead, the space between all the galaxies is expanding, so they are all moving farther apart from each other. So to answer your question, the universe is not expanding into anything. It's just getting bigger, and the space between galaxies is getting bigger along with it.
