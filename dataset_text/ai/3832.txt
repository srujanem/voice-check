A reverse mortgage is a type of loan that allows homeowners who are 62 years of age or older to borrow money using their home as collateral. The loan is called "reverse" because, instead of the homeowner making payments to the lender, the lender makes payments to the homeowner. Here's how it works: The homeowner agrees to take out a reverse mortgage loan. The lender gives the homeowner a lump sum of money, a line of credit, or a series of payments. The homeowner doesn't have to make any payments on the loan as long as they continue to live in the home. When the homeowner dies, sells the home, or moves out, they or their heirs must pay back the loan, plus interest and fees. Reverse mortgages can be a helpful financial tool for certain seniors who need additional income, but they also come with risks. For example, if the homeowner doesn't make payments on their property taxes or insurance, the lender can foreclose on the home. It's important to carefully consider all of the options and fully understand the terms of a reverse mortgage before deciding to take one out.
