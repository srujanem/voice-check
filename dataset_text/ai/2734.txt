When you hold a seashell up to your ear, you can hear the sound of the sea because the shell is acting like a kind of amplifier. When the waves of the sea crash against the shore, they create a sound that travels through the air. If you put a seashell or any other open object (like a cup) up to your ear, the sound waves can travel through the air and into the shell or cup. The shape of the shell or cup causes the sound waves to bounce around inside, and this bouncing makes the sound waves louder. That's why you can hear the sound of the sea (or any other sound) more loudly when you put a seashell or cup up to your ear.
