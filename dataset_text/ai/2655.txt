Antivenom is a medicine that is used to treat people who have been bitten by poisonous spiders or other venomous animals. When a venomous animal bites someone, it injects a toxic substance called venom into the person's body. This venom can cause a variety of symptoms, including pain, swelling, and difficulty breathing. Antivenom works by neutralizing the venom and helping to reverse the symptoms caused by the bite. Antivenom is made from proteins called antibodies, which are produced by the body to help fight off infections and other harmful substances. These antibodies are collected from animals, such as horses or goats, that have been exposed to small amounts of venom and have developed immunity to it. The antibodies are then purified and combined to create the antivenom medicine. When someone is bitten by a venomous animal and needs treatment, they are given a dose of antivenom through an injection. The antivenom travels through the person's bloodstream and attaches to the venom, neutralizing it and helping to remove it from the body. The antivenom also helps to reduce inflammation and swelling caused by the venom. Antivenom is an important medicine that can save lives and help people recover from venomous animal bites. It is important to get medical treatment as soon as possible after being bitten by a venomous animal, as the venom can cause serious health problems if not treated promptly.
