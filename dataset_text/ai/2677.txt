Great question! The first living cells on Earth probably didn't "decide" to divide in the same way that humans make decisions. Instead, the process of cell division, also called mitosis, is a natural and essential part of the life cycle of all living cells. It helps them to grow, repair themselves, and reproduce. Cell division is a complex process that involves the copying of a cell's DNA, the separation of the copies into two new cells, and the creation of new cell membranes around each new cell. It's a way for cells to make more cells like themselves, which is important for the continuation of life. So, to answer your question, the first living cells probably didn't "decide" to divide, but rather, they had the ability to divide built into their DNA. This ability to divide and replicate is what allowed them to continue living and reproducing, which is an essential part of the process of evolution. Does that make sense?
