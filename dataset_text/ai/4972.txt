Vomiting during or after intense physical activity is generally not a normal response and can be a sign of something serious, such as heat stroke or overexertion. However, there are a few possible reasons why someone might feel sick or vomit after intense exercise:\n\nDehydration: When you exercise, you sweat and lose fluids. If you don't replace those fluids, you can become dehydrated, which can lead to nausea and vomiting.\n\nEating too much before exercising: If you eat a large meal right before exercising, your body might have a hard time digesting all that food while also trying to perform physical activity. This can lead to nausea and vomiting.\n\nOverexertion: If you push yourself too hard during exercise, your body might not be able to keep up. This can lead to nausea and vomiting as a way to try to "cool down" and rest.\n\nOther medical conditions: Sometimes, nausea and vomiting during or after exercise can be caused by other medical conditions, such as heat stroke, low blood sugar, or an underlying health problem.\n\nIn general, it's important to drink plenty of fluids and not overexert yourself during physical activity. If you continue to feel sick or vomit after exercising, it's a good idea to speak with a healthcare professional to determine the cause and get the appropriate treatment.
