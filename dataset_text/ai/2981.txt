The system of time that we use today is based on the Earth's rotation around its axis and the orbit of the Earth around the Sun. A day is the amount of time it takes for the Earth to make one complete rotation around its axis, and a year is the amount of time it takes for the Earth to orbit around the Sun. The concept of dividing a day into smaller units like hours, minutes, and seconds has been around for a long time. In ancient civilizations, people used devices like sundials and water clocks to measure time. These devices were not very accurate, but they allowed people to measure time in a way that was consistent with their daily routines. As civilizations developed and became more sophisticated, people began to use more accurate ways of measuring time. For example, in the 16th century, people began using mechanical clocks to measure time more accurately. These clocks used gears and springs to keep time, and they were much more accurate than sundials and water clocks. Today, we use atomic clocks to measure time. These clocks are extremely accurate and use the vibrations of atoms to measure time. They are so accurate that they only lose or gain a second every few million years. So, to sum it up, people have been measuring time for a long time, and over time, we have developed more and more accurate ways of doing it. Today, we use a system of time that is based on the Earth's rotation and orbit and that is measured using atomic clocks.
