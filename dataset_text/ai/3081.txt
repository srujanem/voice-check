Unitarians believe in the inherent worth and dignity of every person, and they believe that individuals should have the freedom to make their own decisions about their beliefs and values. They do not have a set doctrine or creed, but rather focus on promoting reason, respect, and acceptance. A Unitarian church sermon may include readings from various religious and philosophical texts, as well as discussions of social and ethical issues. The sermons are typically focused on promoting tolerance, compassion, and social justice. There is no specific education or wealth requirement to be a Unitarian, and people of all backgrounds are welcome in the church. Unitarians come from a variety of religious and philosophical backgrounds, and they may hold a variety of beliefs about God, the nature of the universe, and other topics. Unitarians generally believe in working towards a more just and compassionate world, and they may be involved in a variety of social and charitable causes. They also place a strong emphasis on education and personal growth, and may offer classes, workshops, and other educational programs to their members.
