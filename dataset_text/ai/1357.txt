Building and launching a spaceship is a complex and expensive endeavor that requires significant resources and expertise. It also involves a number of legal and regulatory considerations. 
In general, launching a rocket or spacecraft into space is regulated by national governments and international organizations. In the United States, for example, launching a rocket or spacecraft into space is regulated by the Federal Aviation Administration's (FAA) Office of Commercial Space Transportation (AST). The AST is responsible for issuing licenses and permits for rocket launches and other commercial space activities. 
To obtain a license or permit from the AST, you would need to demonstrate that your spacecraft is safe and that you have the technical and financial capability to carry out the launch. You would also need to comply with all relevant regulations and laws, including those related to safety, environmental protection, and national security. 
In addition to obtaining a license or permit from the AST, you may also need to obtain permission from other agencies or organizations, such as the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) and the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA). 
In short, while it is theoretically possible for an individual or organization to build and launch a spacecraft, it is a complex and heavily regulated process that requires significant resources and expertise.
