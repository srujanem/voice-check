Political lobbying is a way for people or organizations to try to influence decisions made by government officials, such as lawmakers or regulators. Lobbying can take many forms, such as meeting with officials, writing letters or making phone calls to express a point of view, or organizing grassroots campaigns to mobilize public support for a particular issue. Bribery, on the other hand, is the act of giving something of value (such as money, gifts, or favors) to someone in a position of power in order to influence their actions or decisions. Bribery is generally illegal because it can be used to unfairly sway decisions and undermine the integrity of the political process. In the case of lobbying, the goal is to persuade officials to support a particular position or policy, not to bribe them into taking a particular action. While it is possible for lobbying efforts to be unethical or to cross the line into illegal activity, lobbying itself is not automatically considered to be bribery.
