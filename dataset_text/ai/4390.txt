Aspartame is a non-nutritive sweetener, which means that it is a sweet-tasting substance that provides very few or no calories when consumed. It is made from two amino acids, aspartic acid and phenylalanine, which are naturally occurring substances found in many foods. Aspartame is about 200 times sweeter than sugar, so it can be used in very small amounts to sweeten foods and beverages.\n\nAspartame and other non-nutritive sweeteners like it, such as saccharin and sucralose, have a sweet taste but can sometimes have a strange aftertaste. This aftertaste is caused by the chemical makeup of the sweetener and how it interacts with the taste receptors on our tongues. Some people are more sensitive to this aftertaste than others, which is why some people may find these sweeteners to be less appealing than others.\n\nIt's important to note that aspartame and other non-nutritive sweeteners are generally considered safe for consumption by regulatory agencies around the world, including the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA). However, as with any food or beverage, it's a good idea to consume these products in moderation as part of a balanced diet.
