During the Middle Ages, the economy was based on a system called feudalism. In this system, people lived and worked on the land belonging to a lord or noble. The lord provided protection and justice for the people living on his land, and in return, the people had to work for the lord and give him a portion of the crops and other goods they produced. This system was based on the exchange of goods and services, rather than the use of money as we know it today. In the Middle Ages, people often used coins made of gold, silver, or copper as a way to exchange goods and services. These coins were made by governments or rulers and were used to pay for things like food, clothing, and other necessities. However, not everyone had access to coins, and so people would also exchange goods and services directly, without the use of money. For example, a farmer might trade a basket of apples for a sheep, or a blacksmith might repair a farmer's plow in exchange for a basket of eggs. Overall, the economy during the Middle Ages was much less complex and interconnected than it is today. People lived and worked in small communities and relied on local resources and trade to meet their needs.
