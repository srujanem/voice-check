A sample size of 30 is a commonly used rule of thumb in statistical analysis because it is generally large enough to provide a good estimate of the characteristics of a population. This is especially true if the sample is randomly selected, which means that each member of the population has an equal chance of being included in the sample.\n\nWhen we perform statistical analysis, we are trying to understand the characteristics of a population by looking at a smaller group of people or things from that population. This smaller group is called a sample. The sample size is the number of people or things in the sample.\n\nIf the sample size is too small, it might not accurately represent the characteristics of the population. For example, if you surveyed only 5 households, you might not get a good understanding of what most households are like.
