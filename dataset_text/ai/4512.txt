Corned beef, pastrami, and brisket are all types of meat that are commonly used in sandwiches. Here are the differences between them:\n\nCorned beef is made from a cut of beef called the brisket. It is soaked in a brine solution (a mixture of salt, water, and spices) for several days, which helps to preserve it and give it its distinctive flavor. After it has been soaked, it is cooked until it is tender. Corned beef is often served thinly sliced on sandwiches, or used in dishes like corned beef and cabbage.\n\nPastrami is also made from beef, usually from the same cut of meat as corned beef (the brisket). However, it is prepared differently. After the meat is cured and smoked like corned beef, it is coated in a mixture of spices (usually including black pepper, paprika, and garlic) and then slow-cooked until it is tender. Pastrami is often served on sandwiches or as a topping for pizza.\n\nBrisket is a cut of beef that comes from the chest of the cow. It is a tough cut of meat, but it becomes tender and flavorful when it is cooked slowly over a long period of time. Brisket can be cooked in many different ways, including being turned into corned beef or pastrami, or being slow-roasted or smoked. It is often served as a main course at dinners or as a sandwich filling.\n\nSo, to sum it up: corned beef and pastrami are both made from beef brisket, but they are prepared differently. Brisket is just a cut of beef that can be used to make corned beef, pastrami, or other dishes.
