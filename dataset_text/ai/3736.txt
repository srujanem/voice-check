There are a few reasons why homegrown tomatoes may taste better to some people. Here are a few possibilities: Freshness: Homegrown tomatoes are often picked when they are fully ripe, which means they have had more time to develop their flavors. Store-bought tomatoes may be picked before they are fully ripe and then shipped long distances, which can affect their taste. Varietal differences: Different types of tomatoes have different flavors, and some people may prefer the taste of certain varieties. For example, some people like heirloom tomatoes, which have a more complex flavor, while others prefer the sweetness of cherry tomatoes. Growing conditions: The soil, climate, and care that a tomato plant receives can all affect its flavor. For example, tomatoes grown in rich, well-draining soil with plenty of sunshine may taste sweeter and more flavorful than those grown in less optimal conditions. Citric acid: Tomatoes do contain citric acid, which can contribute to their tartness. However, the amount of citric acid in a tomato can vary depending on the variety and growing conditions, so it's not the only factor that affects their taste. Overall, there are many factors that can influence the taste of homegrown tomatoes, and different people may have different preferences. However, many people find that homegrown tomatoes have a fresher, sweeter, and more complex flavor than store-bought tomatoes.
