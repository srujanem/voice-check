In the animal kingdom, it is common for certain behaviors or characteristics to become more common within a population over time. This is often due to natural selection, where individuals with certain traits are more likely to survive and reproduce, passing those traits down to their offspring. 
For example, if a bird starts singing a new song and other birds find it attractive, they may be more likely to mate with that bird, and their offspring may also learn to sing the same song. Over time, the song may become more common within the population. 
This process is not the same as what we typically think of as "popularity" in human society, where popularity is often based on personal qualities or social status rather than on the ability to survive and reproduce. However, it is similar in the sense that certain traits or behaviors can become more common within a group over time.
