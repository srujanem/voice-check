Lossless audio refers to audio files that retain all the original data of the original audio recording, without any data being lost or discarded during the process of creating the file. This means that lossless audio files are a high-quality, exact copy of the original audio. FLAC (Free Lossless Audio Codec) is a type of lossless audio file format. It was designed to provide a way to store high-quality audio in a file that takes up less space than a raw audio file. FLAC files are often used by audiophiles, musicians, and audio engineers to store and share high-quality audio recordings. One way to think about lossless audio is to compare it to a photo. If you have a high-quality photo and you make copies of it, the copies will be just as clear and detailed as the original. However, if you make a copy of the photo and some of the details are lost or distorted in the process, the copy will not be as good as the original. Lossless audio works in a similar way - it ensures that all the details of the original audio recording are preserved in the file, without any loss of quality.
