In Japanese, as in many other languages, people can express anger or strong emotion through the use of exclamation marks or interjections. For example, "えっ！" (ehh!) or "わあ！" (waa!) can be used to show surprise or disbelief, while "やっぱり！" (yappari!) or "やめて！" (yamete!) can be used to show frustration or anger. 
In addition to using exclamation marks and interjections, people can also use emoticons or emojis to show their emotions. For example, the "anger" emoji (a red face with a furrowed brow and steam coming out of the nose) can be used to show anger. 
It is also common for people to use certain phrases or words to express strong emotions, such as "ざんねん" (zannen) for disappointment or "イライラ" (iraira) for frustration. 
Finally, people can also use tone of voice or facial expressions to convey their emotions when communicating online or in person.
