Correlation means that two things seem to be related to each other. For example, if you have a chart showing the amount of ice cream that people eat and the number of times they go swimming, you might notice that when people eat more ice cream, they also go swimming more often. This means that there is a correlation between eating ice cream and going swimming. Causation means that one thing is causing the other thing to happen. For example, if you notice that when you turn on the light in a room, it gets brighter, you might say that turning on the light causes the room to get brighter. This is because you know that when you turn on the light, it produces light that makes the room brighter. So, correlation is when two things seem to be related, but causation is when one thing causes the other thing to happen. It's important to remember that just because two things are correlated doesn't necessarily mean that one thing is causing the other. There might be other factors that are causing both things to happen at the same time.
