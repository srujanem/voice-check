The Earth and the Moon are both held in place by their mutual gravitational attraction. The Earth is much more massive than the Moon, so it exerts a stronger gravitational pull on the Moon. This is why the Moon orbits the Earth and doesn't crash into it. At the same time, the Moon also has a gravitational pull of its own, and this helps to keep it in orbit around the Earth. The reason the Moon doesn't float away is because it is moving fast enough as it orbits the Earth. If the Moon were to slow down and stop moving, it would fall towards the Earth due to the Earth's gravity. But because it is moving, it follows a curved path around the Earth, like a ball rolling around a bowl. This is what keeps the Moon in orbit around the Earth. To explain this in a way that a five-year-old might understand, you could say that the Earth and the Moon are like two kids playing on a playground. The Earth is the bigger kid, and the Moon is the smaller kid. The Earth's gravity is like the bigger kid's hand, pulling the smaller kid towards him. But the smaller kid is also running around and playing, so he doesn't get too close to the bigger kid. Instead, he keeps running in a circle around the bigger kid, like the Moon orbits the Earth.
