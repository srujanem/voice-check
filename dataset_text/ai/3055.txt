A hangover is the unpleasant feeling you may have after drinking too much alcohol. When you drink alcohol, your body breaks it down into a chemical called acetaldehyde, which can be toxic. Your liver then breaks down acetaldehyde into another chemical called acetate, which is not toxic and can be safely removed from your body. However, if you drink a lot of alcohol in a short period of time, your liver may not be able to keep up with breaking down the acetaldehyde. This can cause a buildup of acetaldehyde in your body, leading to symptoms like: Headache, Nausea, Dry mouth, Fatigue, Dizziness, Sensitivity to light and sound, Difficulty concentrating, Hangovers can also be caused by dehydration, as alcohol can cause you to lose more fluids through urine. This can lead to symptoms like thirst, dry mouth, and headache. There is no surefire way to prevent a hangover, but there are some things you can do to reduce your risk: Drink alcohol in moderation. Eat before or while you are drinking. Avoid drinking on an empty stomach. Drink plenty of water before, during, and after drinking alcohol. Avoid mixing different types of alcohol. Avoid drinking alcohol that has a high concentration of congeners, which are substances that are produced during the fermentation process. These substances can make your hangover worse. I hope this helps! It's important to remember that the best way to avoid a hangover is to drink alcohol in moderation and to always make sure to hydrate.
