Great question! To understand where all the money is, we first have to understand what money is and how it works. Money is a type of tool that we use to buy and sell things. It can take many different forms, such as cash (like paper bills and coins), or it can be in the form of numbers on a screen or piece of paper, like when you use a credit card or write a check. The amount of money that a country has is called its "money supply." Governments, banks, and other organizations can create new money and add it to the money supply. They can also take money out of the money supply by collecting taxes or by taking loans that need to be paid back. Sometimes, a country might not have enough money to pay for everything it needs, like schools, hospitals, and roads. In this case, the government might borrow money from other countries or from banks by selling bonds. A bond is like a promise to pay back the money later, with interest (extra money). So, where is all the money? It's in the hands of people, businesses, and governments who have it and are using it to buy and sell things, and it's also in banks and other financial institutions, where it is being lent out to people and businesses who need it.
