Voltage, also known as electric potential difference, is a measure of the electric potential energy per unit charge between two points in an electric field. It is a force that drives the flow of electric current through a conductor, like a wire.\n\nThink of voltage as the pressure that pushes water through a pipe. Just like water flows from a higher pressure to a lower pressure, electric current flows from a higher voltage to a lower voltage.\n\nImagine a water tank on top of a hill. The water at the top of the hill has a lot of potential energy because it is high up and has the potential to fall and do work (like turning a water wheel). The water at the bottom of the hill has less potential energy because it is lower and doesn't have as far to fall.\n\nNow imagine a pipe connecting the water tank to a water wheel at the bottom of the hill. The pressure of the water in the tank pushes the water through the pipe and makes the water wheel turn. The pressure of the water in the tank is like the voltage in an electric circuit. It drives the flow of water (or electric current) through the pipe (or wire).\n\nI hope this helps! Let me know if you have any more questions.
