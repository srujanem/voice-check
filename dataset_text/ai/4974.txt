Most animals have two eyes because two eyes give them the ability to see better in different directions and to see more detail. This is because each eye is able to see a slightly different view of the world, which allows the brain to put the two views together to create a more detailed, 3D image. This is called stereoscopic vision.\n\nHaving two eyes also helps animals to judge distance and movement more accurately. For example, if an animal is trying to catch a moving insect, it can use its two eyes to judge how far away the insect is and how fast it is moving.\n\nAs for why most animals do not have three or more eyes, it may be because having too many eyes can be confusing for the brain and may not provide any additional benefits. Additionally, having more than two eyes would require more energy to maintain and may not be worth the extra effort for the animal.
