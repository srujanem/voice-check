Grass can spread and reproduce in a few different ways. Some types of grass, like Bermuda grass and creeping bentgrass, can spread by sending out underground stems called rhizomes. These rhizomes can grow new shoots and roots, which can eventually turn into new grass plants. 
Other types of grass, like Kentucky bluegrass and ryegrass, can reproduce by producing seeds. When you mow your lawn, you may not see grass seeds because they are very small and might not be visible on the ground. 
The grass seed that you buy at the store is produced by grass plants that are grown specifically for this purpose. These grass plants are often grown on farms, and when they are mature, they are harvested and the seeds are collected. The seeds are then cleaned, sorted, and packaged for sale to homeowners and landscapers.
