Gargoyles are decorative stone figures that are often found on the outside of buildings, particularly churches and cathedrals. They were originally used for practical purposes, such as channeling water away from the sides of a building to prevent erosion and water damage. However, they also had a symbolic meaning. In the Middle Ages, people believed that gargoyles protected buildings from evil spirits and bad luck. Some of the gargoyles on old buildings depict mythical creatures or demons, which were thought to be able to ward off evil. So, the purpose of gargoyles was to protect buildings from water damage and to keep away evil spirits. They were also used as decorations to make buildings more interesting and beautiful. You don't see many new buildings using gargoyles these days because people don't believe in evil spirits in the same way that they did in the past, and because there are other, more modern ways to channel water away from buildings.
