When a company pays for a commercial to be aired on television, they typically purchase a certain number of airings at a negotiated rate. The number of airings and the frequency at which they are shown can vary depending on the specific agreement between the company and the television network. In some cases, a commercial may be shown multiple times in the same break because it was part of the agreement for the company to purchase a certain number of airings at that time slot. In other cases, it may be due to a scheduling error or oversight on the part of the television network. But in general, companies pay for the air time that their commercials are shown, and they have some control over how often and when their commercials are shown.
