Scabs are a natural part of the healing process after you get a cut or wound. When you get a cut, your body sends special cells to the area to fix the damage. These cells clean out any dirt or germs and then start building new skin cells to cover the wound. This new skin is called a scab. Scabs can sometimes itch because of the healing process that's happening underneath. As new skin cells are being made, they can sometimes push up against the scab, which can cause an itchy sensation. It's your body's way of telling you that it's working to heal the wound. It's important to try not to scratch the scab, because scratching can damage the new skin cells and slow down the healing process. Instead, you can try applying a moisturizer or using a cool, damp cloth to help soothe the itch. If the itching is really bothering you, you can also talk to your doctor or a pharmacist about safe ways to relieve the itch.
