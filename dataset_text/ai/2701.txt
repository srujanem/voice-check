In a direct democracy, the people make decisions directly, rather than electing representatives to make decisions for them. This means that all citizens have a say in the laws and policies that govern their country. In a representative democracy, the people elect representatives to make decisions on their behalf. These representatives listen to the concerns of their constituents and try to make decisions that reflect the will of the people. Direct democracy can be a successful system, as it allows all citizens to have a say in the decisions that affect their lives. However, it can also be difficult to implement, as it requires a large number of people to participate in the decision-making process. This can be challenging in countries with large populations or in countries where many people are not able to participate in the democratic process, such as due to lack of access to education or other resources. Direct democracy is not used as widely as representative democracy because it can be difficult to implement and may not be practical in all situations. Representative democracy is often seen as a more practical and efficient way to make decisions, as it allows for representation of diverse perspectives and allows for more focused and specialized decision-making.
