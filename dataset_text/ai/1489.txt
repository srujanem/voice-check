Graphing calculators are expensive because they have specialized features that are not found in regular calculators, such as the ability to plot graphs and perform advanced mathematical functions. These features require more complex hardware and software, which increases the cost of manufacturing the calculators. Additionally, graphing calculators are often made by smaller companies that do not have the economies of scale enjoyed by larger electronics manufacturers, which can also contribute to the higher price. Finally, the market for graphing calculators is relatively small, so there may be less competition to drive down prices.
