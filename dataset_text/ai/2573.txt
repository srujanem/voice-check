Hard drives are devices that store digital data using spinning disks and magnetic fields. These disks are made of metal and are coated with a special material that can be magnetized. When you save a file on a hard drive, the hard drive's computer (called a controller) breaks the file down into tiny pieces called bits. The controller then sends these bits to the spinning disks, where they are stored as patterns of magnetism. The patterns of magnetism represent the 1s and 0s of digital information. A 1 is represented by a north magnetic pole, while a 0 is represented by a south magnetic pole. By storing the 1s and 0s as magnetic patterns on the spinning disks, the hard drive can remember the data even when it's not connected to a power source. Hard drives are limited in the amount of data they can store because there is only so much room on the spinning disks.
