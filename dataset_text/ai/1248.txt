Yes, mobile phones can get viruses and malware just like computers. However, it is less common for mobile phones to get viruses because there are fewer ways for viruses to spread to a mobile phone. 
One reason it is less common for mobile phones to get viruses is that most mobile phone operating systems (such as iOS for iPhones and Android for Android phones) are designed to be more secure than computer operating systems. They have built-in protections that make it difficult for viruses to install themselves and spread to other apps or parts of the phone. 
Another reason is that mobile phones usually do not have as many programs installed on them as computers do, so there are fewer opportunities for a virus to spread. Additionally, most mobile apps are downloaded from official app stores (such as the App Store for iPhones or the Google Play Store for Android phones), which generally screen apps for viruses before allowing them to be downloaded. 
However, it is still possible for a mobile phone to get a virus, especially if the phone is jailbroken (i.e., the phone's operating system has been modified in a way that removes some of its built-in security measures) or if the user downloads apps from unofficial or untrustworthy sources. It is important to be careful when downloading apps and to keep your phone's operating system and security software up to date to protect against viruses and other malware.
