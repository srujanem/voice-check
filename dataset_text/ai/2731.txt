Irony and sarcasm are two forms of expression that are often used to convey a message in a subtle or indirect way. Here's the difference between the two: Irony: Irony is when you say something that is the opposite of what you really mean, or when something happens that is the opposite of what you expected. For example, if someone says "Oh great, it's raining on my birthday" when it's actually raining on their birthday, that's irony. Sarcasm: Sarcasm is when you say something that is the opposite of what you really mean, but you say it in a way that is meant to be funny or sarcastic. Sarcasm is often used to make fun of someone or something, or to express frustration or annoyance. For example, if someone says "Oh great, just what I needed, another meeting" when they're actually not happy about having to attend another meeting, that's sarcasm. To sum it up, irony is when something is the opposite of what you expect, while sarcasm is when you say something that is the opposite of what you really mean in a humorous or mocking way.
