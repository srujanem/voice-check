The phrase "calling shotgun" refers to the practice of calling out to claim the right to sit in the front passenger seat of a car, usually before starting a journey. This phrase likely comes from the idea that the person sitting in the front passenger seat has a "good view" or "clear shot" of the road ahead, like a person riding shotgun on a stagecoach or in a Wild West-era carriage. It is possible that this phrase originally came from the practice of having a person armed with a shotgun ride along in the front passenger seat to protect the passengers and driver from bandits or other dangers.
