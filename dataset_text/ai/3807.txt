Condensation is the process of turning a gas into a liquid. It occurs when the temperature of a gas decreases and it becomes cooler than its "dew point," which is the temperature at which the gas will start to turn into a liquid. Imagine you have a cold drink on a hot day. When you take a sip, you might notice that the outside of the glass has tiny drops of water on it. This is because the air around the glass is hot and humid (full of water vapor), and when it comes into contact with the cold surface of the glass, the water vapor in the air turns into tiny drops of liquid water. This is an example of condensation. Another example of condensation is when you see steam coming off a hot cup of coffee or tea. When the hot liquid inside the cup cools down, it gives off steam (a gas), which then turns into tiny drops of water on the outside of the cup. So, in short, condensation is the process of turning a gas into a liquid by cooling it down. It is not related to osmosis, which is a different process that involves the movement of particles through a membrane.
