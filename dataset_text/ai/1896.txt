A pyramid scheme is a type of investment where each person involved recruits others to join. Money made by the new members funnels up to the higher members. It is called a pyramid scheme because it usually takes the shape of a pyramid, with the top layer having one person, the next layer having two people, the next four, and so on. Here's an example of how a pyramid scheme might work: The person at the top of the pyramid (the one who started the scheme) recruits two people to join, promising them that they will make a lot of money. These two people then recruit four more people each, promising them the same thing. The four people each recruit eight more people, and so on. The idea is that as more and more people join the pyramid, the money will keep flowing upwards to the top. However, eventually, the pyramid will collapse because there are not enough new people joining to sustain it. When this happens, the people at the bottom of the pyramid (the ones who joined last) will lose all of the money they invested. Pyramid schemes are illegal in many countries because they are fraudulent and do not provide any real value to the people who invest in them. They are simply a way for the person at the top to get rich by tricking others into investing money.
