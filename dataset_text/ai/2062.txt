Manual transmissions generally get better gas mileage than automatic transmissions because they are more efficient. This is because manual transmissions allow the driver to control the gears, which means that the engine can operate at its most efficient speed for a given road speed. For example, if you are driving up a steep hill, you can shift into a lower gear to give the engine more power to climb the hill, while if you are driving on a flat road, you can shift into a higher gear to use less fuel. Automatic transmissions, on the other hand, use a complex system of gears and hydraulics to automatically shift gears based on the speed and load on the engine. This can lead to less efficient operation, especially if the transmission is not perfectly matched to the engine and the driving conditions. There are also some technical reasons why manual transmissions may be more efficient. For example, they typically have fewer moving parts and less friction, which can reduce wear and tear on the transmission and improve efficiency. In addition, manual transmissions do not use a torque converter, which is a type of fluid coupling that is used in automatic transmissions to transfer power from the engine to the transmission. Torque converters can be less efficient than a mechanical connection, which is what a manual transmission uses to transfer power. Overall, the main reason why manual transmissions get better gas mileage is because the driver can control the gears and choose the most efficient gear for the driving conditions.
