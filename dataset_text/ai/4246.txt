Entropy is a measure of disorder or randomness in a system. In the context of time, it means that over time, things naturally tend to become more disordered or random. For example, if you have a perfectly organized room, over time it will become messier as things get moved around and disorganized.\n\nThe idea that time only goes forwards is known as the "arrow of time," and it's based on the observation that we never see things happening in a way that goes against the increase of entropy. In other words, we never see a room spontaneously becoming more organized on its own, or an egg unscrambling itself and returning to its uncooked state.\n\nSo, the reason that time only goes forwards is because of the inherent increase of entropy, or disorder, over time. This increase in entropy is a fundamental aspect of the way the universe works, and it's what gives us the perception that time only moves in one direction.
