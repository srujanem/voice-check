The temperature scale we use is based on the average kinetic energy of the particles in a substance. The faster the particles are moving, the higher the temperature. At -273 degrees Celsius, also known as absolute zero, the particles in a substance have the lowest possible kinetic energy and are essentially motionless. This is the coldest temperature that can be reached. 
On the other hand, there is no theoretical upper limit to the temperature that can be reached. If you keep adding energy to a substance, the particles will keep moving faster and faster, and the temperature will keep increasing. In fact, some scientists believe that it may be possible to reach temperatures that are billions or even trillions of times hotter than the center of the sun, which is about 15 million degrees Celsius. These incredibly high temperatures are hard to even imagine, and they are usually expressed using very large numbers like "decillions."
