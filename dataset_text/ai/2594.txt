Hong Kong and Macau are both special administrative regions (SARs) of China, which means that they have a high degree of autonomy and are able to maintain their own systems of government, laws, and economic policies. However, they are still part of China and are subject to certain rules and regulations set by the central government in Beijing. Hong Kong was a British colony until 1997, when it was returned to China. As part of the agreement between the two countries, Hong Kong was given a special status known as "one country, two systems," which allows it to maintain its own legal and economic systems and a high degree of autonomy. Macau was a Portuguese colony until 1999, when it was returned to China. Like Hong Kong, it was given a high degree of autonomy and is able to maintain its own legal and economic systems. Both Hong Kong and Macau have their own leaders and governments, and they are not censored in the same way as the rest of China. However, there have been concerns about freedom of expression and the rule of law in both regions in recent years. Some people in Hong Kong have been protesting for greater democracy and autonomy, and there have been calls for independence from China. However, the Chinese government considers Hong Kong and Macau to be an integral part of China and has made it clear that it will not allow them to secede.
