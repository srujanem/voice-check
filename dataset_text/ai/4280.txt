Diuretics are medications that help to increase the amount of urine your body produces. They work by causing your kidneys to filter more fluid out of your bloodstream and into your urine. This can help to lower your blood pressure and reduce the amount of excess fluid in your body.\n\nHowever, diuretics can also cause your body to lose more water and electrolytes, such as sodium and potassium, through your urine. This can lead to dehydration and electrolyte imbalances, which can cause symptoms such as dry mouth, thirst, and fatigue.\n\nCoffee and tea are both sources of caffeine, which can act as a diuretic. That's why they may cause you to urinate more frequently and lose more fluids. However, coffee and tea can also cause water retention, especially if you consume them in large amounts or have a sensitivity to caffeine. This is because caffeine can interfere with the way your body regulates fluid balance and cause your body to retain more water.\n\nIt's important to drink plenty of fluids, especially water, when taking diuretics or consuming caffeine to help prevent dehydration and electrolyte imbalances. It's also a good idea to talk to your doctor or pharmacist if you have any questions or concerns about the effects of diuretics or caffeine on your body.\n
