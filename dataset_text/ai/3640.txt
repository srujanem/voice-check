Escrow is a process that helps make sure that both the buyer and the seller of a house are protected during the transaction. Here's how it works: The buyer and seller agree on the terms of the sale, including the price of the house. The buyer pays a deposit, called a "down payment," to the escrow company. The escrow company holds onto the down payment and other important documents, such as the contract and the title to the house, until everything is ready to close the sale. The escrow company makes sure that all the conditions of the sale have been met, such as getting a home inspection or making sure the seller has paid off any mortgages on the property. Once everything is ready, the escrow company will release the down payment to the seller and transfer the title of the house to the buyer. Escrow is important because it helps make sure that both the buyer and the seller are protected. The buyer knows that their down payment is being held safely until the sale is complete, and the seller knows that they will get the down payment once the sale is final. Escrow also helps make sure that all the necessary steps have been taken to complete the sale, which can help prevent problems down the road.
