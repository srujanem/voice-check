Exchange rates fluctuate because they are determined by the supply and demand for different currencies. The exchange rate is the price of one country's currency in terms of another country's currency. When the demand for a particular currency is high, its value will increase, and the exchange rate will go up. When the demand for a particular currency is low, its value will decrease, and the exchange rate will go down. 
There are many factors that can affect the supply and demand for a particular currency, such as the strength of a country's economy, political stability, and interest rates. For example, if a country has a strong economy and stable political situation, people and businesses around the world may want to buy its currency, which will increase demand for it. This will cause the value of the currency to go up and the exchange rate to increase. 
It is not possible to have a constant exchange rate because the factors that affect the supply and demand for different currencies are always changing. For example, a country's economy may strengthen or weaken, or its political situation may change. These changes can affect the demand for its currency and, in turn, the exchange rate.
