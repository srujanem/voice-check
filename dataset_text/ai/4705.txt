The genotyping process that 23andMe uses is a way to learn about a person's DNA, which is the unique set of instructions that tells our bodies how to grow and function.\n\nTo do this, 23andMe collects a small sample of a person's DNA, usually by having them spit into a tube. They then use special machines to make many copies of the DNA and break it into tiny pieces.\n\nNext, they use even smaller pieces called "probes" that are designed to match up with specific parts of the DNA. These probes are attached to a chip, which is a tiny computer chip that can hold a lot of information. When the probes find their match on the DNA, they stick to it and send a signal to the chip.\n\nBy looking at which probes stick and which ones don't, 23andMe can figure out what your DNA looks like at many different places along the DNA molecule. This information can tell them things about your health, ancestry, and other traits.\n\nI hope this helps! Let me know if you have any other questions.
