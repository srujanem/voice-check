Hiccups are sudden, involuntary contractions of the diaphragm muscle. The diaphragm is a large muscle that separates your chest from your abdomen and helps you breathe. When the diaphragm contracts, it moves downward, and air is drawn into your lungs. At the same time, the vocal cords close, which causes the "hic" sound that you hear when you hiccup. Hiccups can be caused by a variety of things, such as eating too fast, eating spicy or acidic foods, drinking carbonated beverages, or even sudden emotional changes. Hiccups can also be a side effect of certain medications or a sign of an underlying medical condition. In most cases, hiccups are not a cause for concern and will go away on their own after a short period of time. However, if hiccups last for a long time or are causing discomfort, there are a few things you can try to stop them, such as holding your breath, swallowing sugar, or drinking a glass of water slowly.
