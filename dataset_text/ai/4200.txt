An empty stomach is important before having surgery because it can help reduce the risk of complications during the procedure. This is because when you have food in your stomach, your body is busy digesting it. If you have anaesthesia during surgery, your body's muscles, including the ones in your stomach and intestines, will relax. If you have a full stomach, the relaxed muscles can cause food to move through your intestines more slowly or even back up into your stomach. This can lead to nausea, vomiting, or other complications.\n\nTo reduce the risk of these complications, doctors usually recommend that you do not eat or drink anything for a certain period of time before your surgery. This allows your stomach to empty out and reduces the risk of complications during the procedure.\n\nIt's also worth noting that the anaesthesia itself should not be affected by whether or not you have an empty stomach. However, having an empty stomach can help make the anaesthesia more effective because there is less risk of nausea or vomiting, which can interfere with the anaesthesia.
