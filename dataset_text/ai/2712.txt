Gitmo, or the Guantanamo Bay detention camp, is a prison located in Cuba that was established by the US government in 2002 to hold people who were captured during the War on Terror, which was a military campaign launched by the US after the September 11th attacks in 2001. Many people believe that Gitmo should be closed because they feel that the way prisoners are treated there is not in line with American values and principles. Some of the specific reasons that people give for wanting Gitmo to be closed include: The prisoners at Gitmo are often held without charge or trial, which means that they have not had the opportunity to defend themselves in a court of law. Some prisoners at Gitmo have been subjected to harsh treatment, including torture, which is against international laws and human rights standards. Gitmo is located outside of the United States, which makes it more difficult for the prisoners to have access to legal counsel and other resources that they might need to defend themselves. The cost of keeping Gitmo open is very high, and many people believe that the money could be better used elsewhere. Overall, the main argument for closing Gitmo is that it is a symbol of the US's commitment to upholding human rights and the rule of law, and that keeping it open undermines those values.
