A pyramid scheme is a type of investment where each person involved recruits others to join. Money made by the new members funnels up to the higher members. It is called a pyramid scheme because it usually takes the shape of a pyramid, with the top layer having one person, the next layer having two people, the next four, and so on. At the top of the pyramid are the people who started the scheme and are making a lot of money. As the pyramid gets bigger and bigger, it eventually collapses because there are not enough new members to sustain the structure. Most pyramid schemes are illegal because they are based on using new members' funds to pay the earlier backers, rather than any real investment or sale of products or services to the public. Imagine a group of friends who start a club and ask each new member to pay a fee to join. They promise that if you recruit enough new members, you will make a lot of money. As more and more people join, the club becomes very popular and makes a lot of money. But eventually, there are no more new members to recruit, and the club collapses. The people who joined at the very beginning made a lot of money, but most of the people lost their membership fee. This is similar to how a pyramid scheme works.
