Trees are full of moisture, but they also contain other materials that can burn, such as leaves, branches, and bark. When a fire starts in a forest, it can quickly spread from one tree to another, especially if there is a lot of dry leaves or branches on the ground. As the fire burns, it heats up the trees and dries out the moisture inside them, making it easier for the fire to continue burning.\n\nThe heat of the fire can also cause the tree to catch fire from the inside out, which is called "self-ignition." This happens when the heat of the fire causes the tree to release gases that can catch fire, such as methane or hydrogen.\n\nForest fires can also spread quickly because there are often strong winds in the forest, which can blow embers from one tree to another and start new fires.\n\nSo, even though trees are full of moisture, they can still burn down in a forest fire because of the heat, dry materials, and gases inside them, and because the fire can spread quickly through the forest.
