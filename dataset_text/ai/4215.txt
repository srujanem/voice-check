When an object is farther away, it appears smaller because it covers less of your field of view. This is due to perspective, which is the way that things appear to the eye based on their distance and position relative to the viewer.\n\nTo understand how perspective works, imagine you are standing at the end of a long straight road. As you look down the road, the objects that are farther away will appear smaller than the objects that are closer to you. This is because the objects that are farther away are actually much larger, but they are so far away that they take up less of your field of view.\n\nThere is no specific ratio for how much an object gets smaller as it gets farther away, because the amount of change in size depends on the distance between the object and the viewer.
