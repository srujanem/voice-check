Firefighters use a variety of methods to determine the cause of a fire, even if there are no obvious remnants left at the scene.\n\nOne way they can do this is by looking for clues in the burn patterns of the fire. Different materials burn in different ways, and the way a fire burns can provide clues about what caused it. For example, a fire that starts in a pile of papers will burn differently than a fire that starts in a pile of fabric.\n\nAnother way firefighters can determine the cause of a fire is by talking to people who were at the scene. Witnesses may have seen what caused the fire, or they may have information about what was happening when the fire started.\n\nFirefighters can also use specialized equipment to help them determine the cause of a fire. For example, they might use a device called a thermal imaging camera to look for hot spots that could indicate where the fire started. They might also use a gas detector to check for the presence of flammable gases, which could help them determine if the fire was caused by a gas leak.\n\nFinally, firefighters might work with investigators who are trained in determining the cause of fires. These investigators can use a variety of techniques, such as examining the chemical makeup of burned materials, to help determine what caused the fire.\n\nOverall, it takes a combination of different methods and techniques to determine the cause of a fire, even if there are no obvious remnants left at the scene.
