In the United States, a jail is a place where people are held temporarily, usually while they are waiting for a trial or an arraignment. Jails are usually run by local governments, like cities or counties. A prison is a place where people are sent to serve longer sentences, usually for more serious crimes. Prisons are usually run by state governments or the federal government. So, the main difference between a jail and a prison is the length of time that a person stays there and the type of crime they are convicted of. Jails are for shorter stays and less serious crimes, while prisons are for longer stays and more serious crimes. I hope that helps to clarify the difference between jail and prison for you!
