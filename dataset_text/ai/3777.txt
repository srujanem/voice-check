The "kilo" prefix in "kilobyte" and "kilometer" actually refers to different quantities. A kilobyte is a unit of digital information that is equal to 1024 bytes. This is because computers use the binary number system, which is based on powers of 2, rather than the decimal number system that we use in everyday life. The prefix "kilo-" means "1,000," but in the binary number system, "1,024" is the number that is closest to 1,000 and is also a power of 2. Therefore, 1 kilobyte is equal to 1024 bytes in the binary system. On the other hand, the prefix "kilo-" in "kilometer" refers to the metric system, which is based on powers of 10. In this system, 1 kilometer is equal to 1000 meters. I hope this helps to clarify the difference between these two units of measurement!
