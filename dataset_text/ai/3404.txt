The Earth's core is still hot because it was formed from very hot materials when the Earth was first formed about 4.5 billion years ago. The heat in the core is also generated by the decay of radioactive elements, such as uranium and thorium, which releases energy as they break down. This process, known as radioactive decay, is what keeps the core hot even after all these billions of years. The pressure at the center of the Earth is also very high, which helps to keep the iron in the core melted and hot.
