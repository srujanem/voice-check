This can be helpful in situations where there is poor traction, such as on snow or ice, because it helps the vehicle to maintain better control and stability. It can also be helpful in other types of terrain, such as off-road or on slippery roads. Two-wheel drive (2WD) means that only two wheels of a vehicle are driven by the engine. This is the most common type of drivetrain for cars, and it is generally less expensive and lighter than all-wheel drive. In most cases, the two wheels that are driven are the front wheels, but some vehicles have rear-wheel drive (more on that below). Front-wheel drive (FWD) means that only the front wheels of a vehicle are driven by the engine. This can be helpful in situations where there is poor traction, because the weight of the engine is over the front wheels, which can help to improve traction. Rear-wheel drive (RWD) means that only the rear wheels of a vehicle are driven by the engine. This can be helpful in certain types of driving, such as performance driving, because it allows for better control and handling. However, it can be more difficult to drive in poor traction conditions, such as on snow or ice, because the weight of the engine is not helping to improve traction on the driven wheels. As for gas mileage, it is generally more fuel-efficient to have a 2WD or FWD vehicle because these drivetrains are lighter and have less friction (the force that resists motion). However, it is also important to note that other factors, such as the size and weight of the vehicle, can also affect fuel efficiency.
