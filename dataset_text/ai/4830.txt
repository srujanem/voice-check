When a candle is burning, the flame produces heat that breaks down the wax into molecules. Some of these molecules combine with oxygen from the air to form water vapor and carbon dioxide. These gases are invisible, so we don't see them as smoke. However, when you blow out the candle, the flame goes out and the heat decreases. This causes the unburnt wax molecules to cool down and solidify, forming a cloud of visible smoke that you can see. The smoke is actually just small particles of solid wax that are suspended in the air.
