Long-term investments are generally considered safer because they have more time to potentially recover from any short-term market downturns. For example, if you invest in stocks for the long-term (e.g., 10 years or more), you may see some ups and downs in the value of your investment over the short-term, but over the long-term, the stock market has historically trended upwards. This means that, on average, if you hold your stocks for a long time, you may see an increase in their value.\n\nSimilarly, long-term bonds are considered safer because they are typically issued by stable, creditworthy entities (such as governments or large corporations) and tend to have lower interest rates than short-term bonds. When you invest in a long-term bond, you are essentially lending money to the issuer in exchange for regular interest payments. If the issuer is able to make those payments on time, then your investment is considered safe.\n\nOf course, it's important to note that there are no guarantees when it comes to investing. Even long-term investments can lose value if the issuer defaults on its payments or if market conditions change significantly. However, by investing for the long-term, you are giving your investments more time to potentially grow and recover from any short-term fluctuations.
