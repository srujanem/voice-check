A strong currency is generally considered to be good for a country because it means that the country's economy is strong and its money is in demand. When the value of a country's currency increases, it can make the country's exports more expensive for other countries to buy, which can lead to decreased exports and slower economic growth. Imagine that you have a store that sells toys. If you have a strong currency, it means that your toys will be more expensive for people in other countries to buy because they have to exchange their own currency for yours. This might make it harder for you to sell your toys to people in other countries, which could lead to decreased sales and profits for your store. On the other hand, a weaker currency can make a country's exports cheaper for other countries to buy, which can lead to increased exports and faster economic growth. However, a weaker currency can also make it more expensive for people in the country to buy imported goods, which can lead to inflation and make it harder for people to afford certain things. Overall, a strong currency can have both positive and negative effects on a country's economy, and it's important for a country to find a balance that works best for its specific needs and circumstances.
