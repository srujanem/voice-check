When you sleep, your mouth produces less saliva, which helps to wash away bacteria and food particles. Without enough saliva, bacteria in your mouth can grow and cause bad breath. Brushing your teeth before bed can help remove some of the bacteria and food particles in your mouth, but it can't completely prevent the growth of bacteria overnight. That's why you may still have bad breath when you wake up in the morning. To help reduce morning breath, try brushing your teeth right after you wake up, drinking plenty of water, and avoiding foods that can contribute to bad breath before bed.
