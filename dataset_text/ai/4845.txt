The kilogram is a unit of measurement for mass. It is defined as the mass of a specific object called the International Prototype of the Kilogram (also known as the "Big K"), which is a cylinder made of a platinum-iridium alloy. The Big K was made in the late 1800s and has been the standard for the kilogram ever since.\n\nHowever, over time, scientists noticed that the mass of the Big K and its replicas (copies of the original cylinder made to be used in other places around the world) seemed to be changing. This was a problem because the kilogram is used to measure the mass of other things, and if the standard for what a kilogram is supposed to be changes, then the measurements of other things would also be incorrect.\n\nTo solve this problem, scientists came up with a new way to define the kilogram. Instead of using the mass of the Big K as the standard, they decided to use a constant value called the Planck constant. The Planck constant is a value that is based on the laws of physics and does not change over time.\n\nNow, the kilogram is defined as the mass that is equal to the Planck constant multiplied by a certain number. This new definition of the kilogram was adopted in 2019, and it is much more accurate and reliable than the old definition based on the Big K.
