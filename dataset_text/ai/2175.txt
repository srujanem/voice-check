Canceling all the world's debt means that all the money that countries, companies, and individuals owe to each other would no longer have to be paid back. This might seem like a good idea because it would allow people and countries to start fresh and not have to worry about paying back their debts. However, canceling all the world's debt would also have some negative consequences. For example: Governments and companies use debt to finance important projects, like building schools, roads, and hospitals. If they can't borrow money, they might not be able to do these things. If debt is canceled, the people and organizations that lent the money (like banks and investors) might not get their money back. This could lead to financial instability and make it harder for people to borrow money in the future. Canceling all debt could also lead to inflation, which means that the value of money would go down and things would cost more. Overall, canceling all the world's debt might seem like a simple solution to some problems, but it would also create new problems and could have serious consequences for the global economy.
