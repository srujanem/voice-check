Imagine that you have a bucket of water with a hose attached to it. The water in the bucket represents the electrical current, and the hose represents the electrical circuit. If you turn on the faucet, the water will flow through the hose and out of the other end. The pressure of the water flowing through the hose is like the voltage in an electrical circuit. The more pressure there is, the faster the water will flow. In an electrical circuit, the higher the voltage, the more electrical current will flow. Watts: Watts is a unit of power, which is a measure of how much energy is being used or produced in a certain amount of time. Power is like the speed of the water flowing through the hose. The more water that flows through the hose in a given amount of time, the more powerful the flow is. In an electrical circuit, the more power that is being used or produced, the more electrical current is flowing through the circuit and the faster it is flowing.
