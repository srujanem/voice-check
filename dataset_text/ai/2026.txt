Pricing items with a cent value that ends in "99" is called "charm pricing," and it's a common pricing strategy that retailers use to make their products seem more affordable. It's based on the idea that prices that end in "99 cents" feel like they are just under a whole dollar amount, so they seem like a better deal to customers. For example, if something is priced at $9.99, it feels like it's almost $10, but it's actually just under $10, so it seems like a better deal than if it was priced at $10. This is a psychological trick that retailers use to make their products seem more appealing to buyers. There are also some practical reasons for using charm pricing. For example, if a retailer wants to price an item at $10, but they don't want to give the impression that the item is too expensive, they might price it at $9.99 instead. This can help to make the item seem more affordable to customers, and it can also help to increase sales. Overall, charm pricing is a way for retailers to make their products seem more appealing and affordable to customers, and it can be a effective way to increase sales.
