Some hotels do not have a 13th floor because the number 13 is considered unlucky by some people. This superstition about the number 13 dates back many centuries, and it is believed to have originated from ancient Norse mythology. The Norse gods were said to have had a dinner party at Valhalla, their great hall, but the 13th guest, Loki, the god of mischief, arrived late and caused trouble, leading to the death of one of the gods. 
In modern times, this superstition about the number 13 has carried over to many cultures and is still widely believed. As a result, many buildings, including hotels, do not have a 13th floor, and instead go directly from the 12th floor to the 14th floor. This is done to avoid any potential negative associations that some people might have with the number 13.
