We call this kind of infinity "countable infinity," because you could count the number of pieces of string you have, even though you could never get to the end. Now, imagine you have an even bigger kind of infinity, where there are so many things that you couldn't even count them all if you tried. This kind of infinity is called "uncountable infinity," and it's even bigger than countable infinity. Here's another way to think about it: imagine you have a jar with an infinite number of marbles in it. You could take out all of the marbles, one by one, and count them. Even though the jar has an infinite number of marbles, you could still count them, because there are a finite number of marbles in the jar. Now, imagine that instead of marbles, the jar has an infinite number of tiny grains of sand. There are so many grains of sand that you could never count them all, no matter how long you tried.
