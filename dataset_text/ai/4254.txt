Hydropower doesn't violate the law of conservation of energy because it converts one form of energy into another. When water falls from a height, it has gravitational potential energy. As it falls, this energy is converted into kinetic energy, which is the energy of movement.\n\nWhen you put a wheel in a waterfall to generate electricity, the kinetic energy of the falling water is used to turn the wheel. This energy is then converted into electrical energy, which can be used to power homes and businesses.\n\nThe waterfall doesn't lose any energy in this process. Instead, the energy is simply changed from one form (kinetic energy) into another form (electrical energy). The total amount of energy remains the same, it is just being converted from one form to another.\n\nSo, if you have two identical waterfalls and you put a wheel in one of them to generate electricity, both waterfalls will have the same amount of energy. The difference is that the waterfall with the wheel will be using some of its energy to turn the wheel and create electricity, while the other waterfall will not be doing this.
