Preordering games is a practice in which a customer pays for a game in advance of its release, sometimes receiving certain bonuses or perks for doing so. Some gamers have become opposed to preordering games for a variety of reasons. One reason is that it can be risky to preorder a game, because there is no way to know for certain whether the game will be good or not until it is actually released. If the game turns out to be disappointing, the customer who preordered it may feel like they wasted their money. 
Another reason some gamers are against preordering is that it can create unnecessary pressure on game developers to meet certain deadlines or targets, which can lead to rushed or unfinished products. This can lead to a lower quality product and a less enjoyable gaming experience. 
Finally, some gamers believe that preordering games takes money away from smaller, independent game developers and instead supports larger, established companies. By not preordering games, gamers can show support for smaller developers and help promote diversity in the gaming industry. 
Overall, the decision to preorder a game is a personal one, and gamers may choose to do so for a variety of reasons, including to get bonus content or to show support for a particular game or developer. However, it is important to do research and consider the potential risks and rewards before making the decision to preorder a game.
