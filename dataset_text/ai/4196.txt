To tilt a helicopter forward or backward, the pilot adjusts the pitch of the main rotor blades. The pitch of a rotor blade refers to the angle of the blade relative to the plane of rotation. By adjusting the pitch of the blades, the pilot can control the lift generated by the rotor.\n\nTo tilt the helicopter forward, the pilot increases the pitch of the blades on the front of the helicopter and decreases the pitch of the blades on the back. This causes the helicopter to tilt forward because the increased lift on the front makes the helicopter want to lift off the ground, while the decreased lift on the back keeps it from lifting off the ground.\n\nTo tilt the helicopter backward, the pilot does the opposite - they decrease the pitch of the blades on the front of the helicopter and increase the pitch of the blades on the back. This causes the helicopter to tilt backward because the decreased lift on the front makes the helicopter want to sink down, while the increased lift on the back keeps it from sinking down.\n\nThe pilot can also control the tilt of the helicopter by adjusting the speed of the tail rotor, which is a small rotor mounted on the tail of the helicopter.
