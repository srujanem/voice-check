Kosher food refers to food that is considered "fit" or "proper" according to Jewish dietary laws. These laws are found in the Torah (the Jewish holy book) and are followed by many Jews as a way to show their devotion to God and to live a healthy and spiritual life. There are a few main rules that apply to Kosher food: Certain animals are not allowed to be eaten at all, such as pigs, reptiles, and shellfish. Meat and dairy products cannot be mixed or eaten together. For example, you can't have a cheeseburger or a bowl of ice cream with a steak. Meat must come from animals that have been slaughtered and prepared in a specific way, according to Jewish law. This is called "kosher slaughter." Fruits and vegetables are generally considered Kosher, but some people may follow additional rules about how they are grown and prepared. Kosher food is often labeled with a special symbol, such as a "K" or a "U" inside a circle, to indicate that it meets these guidelines. When eating at a Kosher dinner, you can expect to find food that follows these rules and that has been prepared in a Kosher kitchen.
