The price of a product is determined by many factors, including the cost of producing and distributing the product, the demand for the product, and the competition in the market. The cost of producing a larger size of a product may be less than the cost of producing a smaller size of the same product, because the larger size can be made more efficiently. For example, it might cost less to make one large bottle of Coke than to make two smaller bottles of Coke because the large bottle can be made on a single production line, while the smaller bottles would require two separate production lines. In addition, the demand for larger sizes of products may be greater, which can also affect the price. If more people are willing to buy larger sizes of Coke, the company can charge a lower price for those sizes and still make a profit. Finally, competition in the market can also influence the price of a product. If there are other companies selling similar products, the company may need to lower its prices in order to stay competitive. So, in this case, it's possible that the larger size of Coke is cheaper because it costs less to produce, there is a higher demand for it, and there is competition in the market.
