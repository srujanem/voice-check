The National Security Agency (NSA) is a government agency that is responsible for collecting and analyzing electronic communications and other data for the purpose of national security. In 2013, a man named Edward Snowden revealed that the NSA was using a program called PRISM to collect and store data from internet and phone companies without the knowledge or consent of the users. This caused a lot of controversy and concern, as many people felt that their privacy was being violated. The government has always been interested in collecting and analyzing information in order to protect the country, but the revelations about PRISM showed that the NSA was collecting a large amount of data from ordinary people who were not suspected of any wrongdoing. This raised questions about whether the government was going too far in its efforts to gather information, and whether the NSA was violating people's rights to privacy. In the years since the PRISM revelations, there has been ongoing debate about the balance between national security and privacy, and how much information the government should be allowed to collect and store about its citizens. Some people feel that the government needs to be able to gather this information in order to keep the country safe, while others believe that the government should not be able to invade people's privacy in this way.
