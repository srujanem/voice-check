The terms "first world," "second world," and "third world" were originally used to distinguish between the capitalist countries of the West, the communist countries of the Eastern bloc, and the developing countries of the Third World, respectively. The concept of the "second world" referred specifically to the socialist countries that were aligned with the Soviet Union during the Cold War, such as China, Cuba, and several countries in Eastern Europe. However, the Cold War ended in 1991, and the distinction between the "first world," "second world," and "third world" is not used as frequently today. Instead, the terms "developed countries" and "developing countries" are more commonly used to describe the relative levels of economic development and prosperity around the world.
