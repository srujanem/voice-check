In the past, militaries used to fight in straight lines because it made it easier to control the soldiers and coordinate their movements. The soldiers in the front rows would "take hits" because they were protected by the soldiers behind them. They would hold their ground and try to defend themselves as best they could, while the soldiers behind them would try to shoot over their heads or throw weapons at the enemy. As for why things changed, there are a few reasons. First, the development of more advanced weapons made it harder for soldiers to stand in straight lines and fight effectively. For example, the invention of guns and cannons made it easier for soldiers to kill each other from a distance, which meant that standing in a straight line and trying to fight hand-to-hand was less effective. Second, the development of new tactics and strategies also played a role. Military leaders realized that it was more effective to use cover, concealment, and surprise to attack the enemy, rather than just standing in a straight line and trying to shoot them. So, over time, militaries began to adopt more flexible and dynamic ways of fighting, rather than just standing in straight lines facing each other. This allowed them to be more effective in combat and achieve their objectives.
