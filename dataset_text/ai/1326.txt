Video games are made by software developers and are usually tested before they are released to the public. However, it is not always possible to catch every single glitch or bug in a game before it is released. Glitches are mistakes or errors that occur in the game that can cause it to behave in unexpected or unintended ways. 
Sometimes, a glitch can be minor and not affect the overall enjoyment of the game, but other times, a glitch can be more serious and cause problems with gameplay. When a game has a serious glitch that significantly impacts the gameplay, it is considered defective. In this case, the manufacturer of the game may offer a full refund or allow the customer to exchange the defective game for a new, working copy. 
In general, it is important to remember that video games are complex pieces of software and it is not always possible to catch every single glitch before they are released. However, if a game has a serious defect that significantly impacts the gameplay, the manufacturer may offer a refund or exchange to the customer.
