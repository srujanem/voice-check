House of Leaves is a novel by Mark Z. Danielewski that tells the story of a family who moves into a house that is much bigger on the inside than it appears from the outside. The family discovers that the house is full of strange, unsettling, and sometimes dangerous rooms and corridors that seem to shift and change constantly. As the family members explore the house, they also uncover a series of documents and recordings that tell the story of a man named Johnny Truant, who becomes obsessed with the house and its history. Truant is a tattoo artist who becomes fascinated by a film called "The Navidson Record," which documents the experiences of a photographer named Will Navidson and his family as they live in the house. The novel is complex and layered, with multiple narrators and a mix of different types of text, including letters, diary entries, and footnotes. It is also filled with symbolism and metaphor, and readers may find themselves interpreting the story in different ways. Overall, House of Leaves is a horror novel that explores themes of identity, perception, and the nature of reality. It is known for its unconventional structure and use of language, and is considered a challenging but rewarding read.
