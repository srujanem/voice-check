Voltage is used as a measure of electric hazard because it is closely related to the amount of electrical energy that can be delivered to a person. The higher the voltage, the greater the electrical energy that can be delivered, and the more dangerous the situation can become. 
Current, on the other hand, is a measure of the flow of electric charge through a conductor, such as a wire. It is the movement of these electric charges that can cause harm to a person when they come into contact with a high-voltage electrical source. 
So, when we label transformers with warning labels that indicate the voltage, it is a way of letting people know how much electrical energy is present and how dangerous the situation could be if they were to come into contact with the electrical source. 
In general, it is safer to stay away from any electrical sources that are labeled with high voltage warning labels, as these can be very dangerous and can cause serious injury or even death.
