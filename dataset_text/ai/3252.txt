To go back to shore, a sailboat can "tack" or "zig-zag" against the wind. This means that the sailboat will sail at an angle, moving forward a little bit with each "zig" and "zag." The sailboat will keep moving in this way until it gets close enough to the shore, and then it can sail directly to it. To go east when the wind is blowing west, the sailboat can also tack or zig-zag. However, the sailboat might have to sail a longer distance to get to its destination because it is not sailing directly into the wind. Imagine you are blowing up a balloon. The air (or wind) is coming out of the balloon and you are trying to blow it back in. It's hard to do, right? It's much easier to blow the air to the side and then bring the balloon back in. This is similar to how a sailboat tacks or zig-zags against the wind.
