Combining two or more WiFi networks is not possible because each WiFi network is a separate entity that operates on its own frequency band and is not connected to any other WiFi networks. When you connect to a WiFi network, you are connecting to a specific network that is broadcasting on a particular frequency band, and the router that is broadcasting that network is responsible for managing the data that is transmitted over that network. 
In order to combine two or more WiFi networks, you would need a way to connect all of the routers together and merge their frequency bands into a single network. This is not possible with current technology because each router operates independently and does not have the ability to connect to or merge with other routers. 
Additionally, even if it were possible to combine two or more WiFi networks, it would likely not result in a "super fast" WiFi connection. The speed of a WiFi connection is determined by a number of factors, including the strength of the signal, the distance between the device and the router, and the number of devices that are connected to the network. Simply adding more routers or merging multiple WiFi networks would not necessarily increase the speed of the connection.
