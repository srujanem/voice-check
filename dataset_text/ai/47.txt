Jazz emerged in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries in New Orleans as a vibrant fusion of African, European, and Caribbean musical traditions. Characterized by syncopated rhythms, expressive blue notes, and spontaneous improvisation, the genre gave musicians extraordinary artistic freedom. As jazz migrated north toward Chicago and New York during the Great Migration, it evolved into distinct subgenres like swing, bebop, cool jazz, and fusion. Today, jazz remains a globally influential art form, celebrated for its rich historical roots and its continuous innovation across musical boundaries.
