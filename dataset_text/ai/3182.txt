Sure! When you eat food, it starts to move through your digestive system. Your digestive system is a series of organs that help your body break down and absorb the nutrients from the food you eat. The first stop for food in your digestive system is your stomach. Your stomach uses strong acids and muscles to break down the food into a liquidy mixture called chyme. The chyme then moves into your small intestine. Your small intestine is a long, narrow tube that is responsible for absorbing most of the nutrients from the food you eat. As the chyme moves through the small intestine, the walls of the intestine absorb the nutrients and send them into your bloodstream. After the small intestine, the chyme moves into your large intestine. The large intestine's main job is to absorb water and electrolytes (like salt) from the chyme and turn it into solid poop. The poop is then stored in your rectum until you're ready to go to the bathroom. Sometimes, certain foods can irritate your digestive system or cause an allergic reaction. This can cause the muscles in your intestines to contract more quickly or more forcefully than usual, which can cause diarrhea. This is why you might have diarrhea a few hours after eating certain foods, even though it takes about 8 hours for food to move through your entire digestive system.
