Sure! Here's how a car works: You put gas in the car by filling up the gas tank. The gas goes into a small engine in the car called the "combustion engine." The combustion engine has small cylinders where a spark plug creates a spark that ignites the gas. When the gas is ignited, it creates a small explosion that pushes a metal rod called a "piston." The piston moves back and forth inside the cylinder, which makes the engine turn around. The turning engine is connected to the wheels of the car through a system of gears and belts. When the engine turns, it makes the wheels turn, which propels the car forward. You can control how fast the car goes by pressing the accelerator pedal, which lets more gas into the engine and makes it go faster. The car's speedometer tells you how fast you are going. You stop the car by pressing the brake pedal, which slows the wheels down and eventually stops them from turning. So, when you put gas in the car and turn the key, the engine uses the gas to create energy, which makes the wheels turn and propels the car forward. You can control how fast the car goes by pressing the accelerator pedal, and you can stop the car by pressing the brake pedal. Does that help clarify things?
