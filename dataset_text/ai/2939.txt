Genetically modified (GM) foods are foods that have had their DNA changed in a way that does not occur naturally. This can be done for a variety of reasons, such as to make the food more resistant to pests or diseases, to make it grow faster or bigger, or to make it more nutritious. Some people are concerned about GM foods because they are not sure if they are safe to eat. Some people are also worried about the impact that GM crops might have on the environment, and about the possibility that GM crops could cross-breed with non-GM crops and create unintended consequences. However, many scientists believe that GM foods are safe to eat and can be a useful tool for addressing some of the challenges facing the world's food supply, such as feeding a growing population and adapting to a changing climate. In general, the scientific community agrees that GM foods are safe to eat, and they have been consumed by millions of people around the world for more than 20 years without any reported adverse health effects. It's important to note that all new technologies carry some risk, and GM foods are no different. However, scientists and regulatory agencies around the world are working to ensure that GM foods are thoroughly tested and carefully monitored to ensure their safety.
