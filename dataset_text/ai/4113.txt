Net neutrality is the idea that all internet traffic should be treated equally. This means that internet service providers (ISPs) should not be able to slow down or block any websites or services, or charge more money for faster access to them. The debate about net neutrality is a big deal because it affects how you can use the internet. If net neutrality rules are in place, you can use the internet however you want and access any websites or services you want without your ISP getting in the way. But if net neutrality is not in place, your ISP could decide to block or slow down certain websites or services, or charge you more money to access them. For example, let's say you like to watch videos on a website called "VideoLand." Without net neutrality, your ISP might slow down VideoLand so it takes a long time to load, or they might block it completely so you can't access it at all. On the other hand, if your ISP has a deal with another video website called "WatchVideos," they might make that website load faster and be easier to use. Overall, the average user's internet experience would change depending on whether or not there are net neutrality rules in place. With net neutrality, everyone would have the same access to all websites and services. Without net neutrality, some websites and services might be harder to use or not available at all, depending on what your ISP allows.
