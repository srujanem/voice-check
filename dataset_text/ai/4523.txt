Seizures are caused by abnormal activity in the brain. When certain areas of the brain become overactive or abnormal, they can send out abnormal signals that cause the body to react in unusual ways. During a seizure, a person may lose consciousness and control over their movements. They may have spasms or convulsions, or they may become rigid or have other unusual movements.\n\nThere are many different types of seizures, and they can be caused by a variety of things, including head injuries, brain infections, or brain disorders such as epilepsy. Epilepsy is a condition that causes a person to have recurring seizures. It can be caused by genetic factors or other unknown causes.\n\nThere are other medical conditions that can cause a person to lose control over their movements or to have unusual body movements. For example, some people with Parkinson's disease may have tremors or other uncontrolled movements. Some people with multiple sclerosis may have muscle spasms or other involuntary movements.\n\nIt's important to see a doctor if you or someone you know is having seizures or other unusual body movements, as they can be a sign of a serious medical condition that needs treatment.
