Water evaporates when its molecules gain enough energy to break their bonds and become a gas. This can happen even when the water is not being heated to its boiling point. Imagine a pot of water on the stove. As the water is heated, the molecules in the water gain more and more energy. Eventually, they have enough energy to break their bonds and turn into steam, which is a gas. This process is called boiling. But water can also evaporate at lower temperatures, even when it's not being heated at all. This is because the molecules in the water are always moving around and bumping into each other. Sometimes, they might have enough energy to break their bonds and turn into a gas, even if the water isn't very hot. For example, if you leave a glass of water by the window, the molecules in the water will absorb energy from the surrounding air. This energy can cause some of the molecules to break their bonds and turn into a gas, which we call evaporation. The water will eventually evaporate, even if it's not being heated to 100 degrees.
