In the United States, street numbers are usually assigned based on the distance of the building or lot from the beginning of the street or block. For example, if a street starts at 0 or 1, the first building on the left side of the street might be numbered 2, and the second building might be numbered 4, and so on. The numbering continues on each side of the street, so the first building on the right side of the street might be numbered 1, and the second building might be numbered 3, and so on. 
This system can result in seemingly random or irregular numbering, especially on long streets or blocks. For example, if a street runs for several miles, the numbers may increase by 100 or more for each block. This means that you might see a building with the number 22097 next to a building with the number 22092. 
In some cases, the street numbers may also be affected by historical factors or changes in the layout of the street or neighborhood. For example, if a street was originally laid out in a different pattern or if buildings were added or removed over time, it can result in seemingly random or irregular numbering. 
Overall, the system of street numbering in the United States is designed to help people find specific addresses and locations easily and efficiently. Even though the numbers may seem random at first, they usually follow a logical pattern based on the location of the building or lot on the street or block.
