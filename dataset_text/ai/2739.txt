Electricity is controlled across political lines through a combination of agreements between different governments and regulatory bodies, as well as technical infrastructure like power lines and transformers. When electricity is generated at a power plant, it is typically sent over power lines to a substation, where it is transformed to a higher voltage so that it can be transmitted over long distances more efficiently. From there, the electricity is sent to another substation, where it is transformed back down to a lower voltage that is suitable for distribution to homes and businesses. As electricity flows across political borders, it may pass through a number of different substations and be managed by different utilities or government agencies. To ensure that the electricity is delivered safely and reliably, these utilities and agencies must work together and follow certain rules and regulations. For example, if two neighboring towns both receive electricity from the same power plant, they might have an agreement in place that outlines how the electricity will be divided between them and how any issues that arise will be resolved. Similarly, if an electricity grid spans multiple states or provinces, there may be regulatory bodies at the national level that oversee the distribution of electricity and ensure that it is being used efficiently and fairly. Overall, the goal is to make sure that everyone has access to reliable and affordable electricity, regardless of which political border they happen to live on.
