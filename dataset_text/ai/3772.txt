Movies and video games are produced and displayed differently, which can affect how smooth they appear. Movies are typically shot with a high-quality camera that captures a lot of detail, and they are displayed at a frame rate of 24 frames per second (FPS). This means that the movie is made up of 24 still images that are shown in rapid succession, creating the illusion of motion. The smoothness of the movie is also influenced by the way the camera moves and the way the actors perform. On the other hand, video games are produced using computer graphics, which can require more processing power to render. As a result, some video games may not be able to maintain a frame rate of 24 FPS, especially if there are a lot of complex graphics or fast-moving action on the screen. Additionally, some video games may not be optimized for certain devices, which can also affect their performance. Overall, the difference in smoothness between movies and video games at 24 FPS can be due to a combination of factors, including the quality of the camera, the complexity of the graphics, and the optimization of the game.
