Capacitors are like little batteries that can store electricity. They have two metal plates inside them, and when you put electricity into a capacitor, it gets stored on these metal plates. Imagine you have two metal plates and you want to put electricity between them. If you put a lot of electricity between the plates, it will be like there is a big mountain of electricity between the plates. If you put less electricity between the plates, it will be like there is a small mountain of electricity between the plates. Now imagine that you have a rubber sheet that you can stretch between the two metal plates. When you stretch the rubber sheet, it pushes the electricity mountains closer together, which makes it harder for electricity to flow between the plates. This is what a capacitor does - it stores electricity by stretching a rubber sheet (called a "dielectric") between the metal plates. When you want to use the electricity that is stored in a capacitor, you can connect it to a circuit and the electricity will flow out of the capacitor and into the circuit, just like water flowing out of a bucket. Capacitors are used in many different electronic devices to help smooth out the flow of electricity and to store energy for later use.
