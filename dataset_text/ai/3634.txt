Allergies are the body's response to substances that are usually not harmful. These substances are called allergens, and they can be things like pollen, mold, animal dander, certain foods, and more. When someone is allergic to something, their body's immune system mistakes the allergen as a threat and tries to fight it off. This causes symptoms like sneezing, itching, and swelling. There isn't a cure for allergies because they are caused by the body's own immune system. Allergy medicine can help to reduce the symptoms, but it doesn't get rid of the allergy itself. Some people are more likely to have allergies because they have a family history of allergies, but it's not always clear why some people develop allergies and others don't. It's also possible to be allergic to things like the sun or certain types of food. When someone is allergic to the sun, their skin can become red, itchy, and swollen when it is exposed to sunlight. Food allergies happen when the body's immune system mistakes a certain type of food as a threat and tries to fight it off, which can cause symptoms like stomach pain, vomiting, and trouble breathing. Again, there isn't a cure for these types of allergies, but people who have them can try to avoid the things that cause their allergies and take allergy medicine to help manage their symptoms.
