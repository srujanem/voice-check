The Tenth Amendment to the United States Constitution is part of the Bill of Rights and it states that any powers not delegated to the federal government by the Constitution, nor prohibited to it by the Constitution, are reserved for the states or the people. This means that the federal government only has the powers that are specifically listed in the Constitution, and any other powers are reserved for the states or the people. The Tenth Amendment is not necessarily ignored, but it is sometimes interpreted differently by different people. Some people believe that the federal government has the power to do certain things that are not specifically listed in the Constitution, while others believe that the states or the people have more power. This can lead to disagreement and debate about the proper role of the federal government in different areas of life. It is also worth noting that the Constitution and the Bill of Rights, including the Tenth Amendment, are not necessarily set in stone and can be amended or changed over time through the process of constitutional amendment. This process is intended to allow for the Constitution to be amended in order to keep it relevant and responsive to the needs and concerns of the people.
