Tilt-shift photography is a technique that involves tilting and shifting the lens of a camera in order to control the plane of focus in an image. This allows the photographer to choose which parts of the image are in focus and which are blurry. When used in a particular way, tilt-shift photography can make a scene look like it is a miniature model or a diorama, rather than a real location. One way that tilt-shift photography can create this miniature effect is by focusing on a specific part of the scene, such as the ground in front of the camera, and blurring the rest of the image. This can make it look like the scene is a small model, with the in-focus part representing the "ground" and the blurry parts representing the background. Another way that tilt-shift photography can create the miniature effect is by using a wide-angle lens and tilting the lens so that the plane of focus is perpendicular to the camera. This can make objects in the scene appear much smaller than they actually are, giving the impression that they are miniature figures rather than real-life objects. So, in short, tilt-shift photography creates the miniature effect by using clever camera techniques to control the plane of focus and make parts of the image appear smaller than they really are.
