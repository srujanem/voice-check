Fridges work by using a cooling system to make the inside of the fridge colder than the outside air. Inside the fridge, there is a gas called a refrigerant that flows through a system of tubes. When the refrigerant is pumped through the tubes, it absorbs heat from the air inside the fridge. This process cools down the air inside the fridge. The refrigerant then travels to a compressor, which is a kind of pump. The compressor squeezes the refrigerant, which makes it hot. The hot refrigerant is then sent to a set of coils on the back of the fridge. These coils are called the condenser coils. When the hot refrigerant passes through the condenser coils, it releases the heat it absorbed from inside the fridge into the air outside the fridge. This process cools down the refrigerant, so it can be pumped back into the fridge to absorb more heat and keep the inside of the fridge cold. So, in short, the fridge uses energy (usually from electricity) to run the compressor and pump the refrigerant through the cooling system. This helps to remove heat from the inside of the fridge and keep it cold.
