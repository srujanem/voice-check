Affirmative action is a set of policies and practices that are intended to help groups that have been historically disadvantaged or discriminated against, such as people of color, women, and people with disabilities. It is often used in education and employment to help increase the number of people from these groups who have access to opportunities that they may have been denied in the past. One way that affirmative action can be used is by setting goals or targets for the number of people from these disadvantaged groups that an organization or institution wants to admit or hire. For example, a college might set a goal to enroll a certain percentage of students who are African American or Hispanic, in order to increase diversity on campus. Another way that affirmative action can be used is by giving preference or special consideration to people from these disadvantaged groups during the admissions or hiring process. This can mean that someone from a disadvantaged group who may not have the same qualifications as another candidate may still be considered for a position if they are a member of a group that has historically been disadvantaged or discriminated against. Affirmative action is meant to help level the playing field and provide more opportunities for people who have been historically disadvantaged or discriminated against. It is not intended to give anyone an unfair advantage or to discriminate against anyone else.
