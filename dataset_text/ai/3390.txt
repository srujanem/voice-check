The passive voice is a grammatical construction that is used to describe an action in which the subject of the sentence is not the one performing the action. Instead, the subject is the one receiving the action. For example: "The ball was thrown by the boy." In this sentence, the boy is the one performing the action (throwing the ball), but the subject of the sentence is the ball, which is receiving the action. Using the passive voice can be a useful way to convey information in some situations. For example, if you want to focus on the result of an action rather than the person or thing performing the action, the passive voice can be a good choice. However, it can also make sentences longer and less clear, so it's important to use it sparingly. In general, it is usually clearer and more straightforward to use the active voice, which puts the subject of the sentence in the position of the one performing the action. For example: "The boy threw the ball." This sentence is shorter and easier to understand than the passive version. So, to answer your question: the passive voice is not necessarily good or bad, but it's important to choose the right voice for the situation. It's generally a good idea to use the active voice most of the time, but the passive voice can be useful in certain cases.
