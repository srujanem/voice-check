Airlines overbook flights because they know that not every passenger who has a reservation will show up for the flight. This can happen for a variety of reasons, such as a change in plans, illness, or a missed connection. 
When an airline overbooks a flight, it means that they have sold more tickets than there are seats on the plane. This is because the airline is trying to maximize their profits by filling as many seats as possible on each flight. 
The airline tries to carefully estimate how many people are likely to miss the flight so that they can sell just enough extra tickets to make up for those empty seats. However, sometimes they get this estimate wrong, and there are more passengers with tickets than there are seats on the plane. 
When this happens, the airline will try to find volunteers who are willing to give up their seats in exchange for compensation, such as a voucher for a future flight or a cash payment. If they are unable to find enough volunteers, they may have to deny boarding to some passengers, even if they have a valid ticket.
