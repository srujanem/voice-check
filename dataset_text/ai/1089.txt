When you microwave something, the microwave energy causes the water molecules in the food to vibrate rapidly. This vibration creates heat, which cooks the food. When the water molecules are vibrating, they can bump into each other and create a popping or sizzling sound. 
Microwaveable foods often contain water, fat, and sugars, which can cause the food to make a popping or sizzling sound when heated in the microwave. The sound is caused by the expansion and contraction of these substances as they are heated and cooled. 
It's important to note that microwaves are safe to use and do not make food radioactive. The microwave energy is absorbed by the food and does not remain in the food once it is cooked.
