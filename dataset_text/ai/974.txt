Radio stations and TV stations are both businesses that make money by selling advertising. The difference is that radio stations typically make their money from selling ads that are played on the radio, while TV stations make money by selling ads that are shown during commercial breaks on TV. 
TV stations also have the option of charging a subscription fee to viewers in order to watch their programming. This is usually done through a service like cable or satellite TV, which provides access to a variety of channels for a monthly fee. 
As for why commercials are often louder than the show, it's usually because advertisers want to grab people's attention and make their ads stand out. By making their ads louder, they hope that more people will notice and remember their ads. This is a technique known as "volume compression," and it's used to make the ads more noticeable and memorable. However, some people find it annoying, and there are regulations in place to try to prevent commercials from being too loud.
