The film you see on the edges of your cooked eggs is called a "film" or "skin," and it forms when the proteins in the eggs start to cook and solidify. As the eggs cook, the proteins on the surface of the egg whites and yolks will denature (unfold) and then coagulate (thicken and solidify). As the proteins coagulate, they can stick to the sides of the pan and form a thin film. There are a few things you can try to prevent this film from forming: Use a non-stick pan: Non-stick pans are coated with a special material that helps prevent food from sticking, which can help reduce the formation of the film. Use a little bit of oil or butter: Adding a little bit of oil or butter to the pan before cooking the eggs can also help prevent them from sticking and forming a film. Use a silicone or rubber spatula: A silicone or rubber spatula can help scrape the edges of the pan while the eggs are cooking, which can help prevent the film from forming. Cook the eggs over low heat: Cooking the eggs over low heat can help prevent the film from forming because the proteins in the eggs will cook more slowly, which gives them less time to stick to the sides of the pan.
