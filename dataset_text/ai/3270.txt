Lift is a force that acts on an object when it is moving through a fluid, such as air or water. It is the force that allows an airplane or helicopter to fly. In an airplane, lift is created when the air flows over the wings of the airplane. The shape of the wings is designed to create lift. As the airplane moves through the air, the air flows faster over the top of the wing than it does under the wing. This difference in speed creates a difference in pressure, with the air under the wing having a higher pressure than the air over the wing. This pressure difference creates a force that lifts the airplane into the air. In a helicopter, lift is created by the spinning blades on the top of the helicopter, called the rotor. As the rotor blades spin, they push the air downward, creating lift. The helicopter can control the amount of lift it generates by adjusting the speed of the rotor blades and the angle at which they meet the air. So, to answer your question, science does help lift work in flight. Engineers and scientists use the principles of physics and fluid dynamics to design the wings and rotors of airplanes and helicopters in such a way that they can generate enough lift to take off and stay in the air.
