The cost of a death sentence is generally more expensive than keeping someone in prison for a number of reasons. First, the legal process for a death sentence case is much longer and more complicated than a non-capital case. This means that there are more legal proceedings, more lawyers involved, and more time spent in the courtroom. All of these factors contribute to the higher cost of a death sentence case. 
In addition to the legal costs, there are also the costs of carrying out the death sentence itself. This includes the cost of the execution, as well as the cost of housing the prisoner on death row. Death row inmates are often kept in isolation, which can be more expensive than housing prisoners in a general population. 
Finally, there are also the costs of appealing a death sentence. Many death sentence cases are appealed, which adds to the overall cost of the case. 
Overall, the cost of a death sentence can be several times more expensive than the cost of keeping someone in prison for a long period of time. This is one of the reasons that some people argue that it may be more cost-effective to impose a life sentence without the possibility of parole instead of a death sentence.
