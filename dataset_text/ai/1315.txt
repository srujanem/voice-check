A "brain freeze," also known as an ice cream headache or cold stimulus headache, is a type of headache that occurs when you eat or drink something very cold. It is caused by the rapid expansion of blood vessels in the head, which can be triggered by the cold temperature of the food or drink. The scientific term for a brain freeze is "sphenopalatine ganglioneuralgia." 
To get rid of a brain freeze, you can try pressing your tongue against the roof of your mouth or drinking something warm. These actions can help to relax the blood vessels in your head and relieve the headache. Brain freezes are usually not serious and go away on their own after a few minutes.
