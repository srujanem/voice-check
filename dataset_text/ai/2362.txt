Here are brief explanations of each term: Senator: A senator is a member of the United States Senate, which is one of the two chambers (or parts) of the United States Congress. Senators represent their state in Congress and work to make laws for the country. There are 100 senators in the Senate, two from each state. Governor: A governor is the leader of a state in the United States. Governors are elected by the people of their state and are responsible for making sure that the state's laws are followed and for representing the state's interests. Congressman: A congressman (or congresswoman) is a member of the United States House of Representatives, which is the other chamber of the United States Congress. There are 435 congressmen and congresswomen in the House of Representatives, and they represent the people of their district (a specific area within their state). Representative: A representative is a person who speaks or acts on behalf of a group of people or an organization.
