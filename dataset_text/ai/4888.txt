When you yell, you are making a lot of noise by forcing a lot of air through your vocal cords, which are the two thin bands of muscle in your larynx (voice box) that vibrate to make sound when you speak or sing. Yelling puts a lot of strain on your vocal cords, and over time, this can cause them to become inflamed or swollen. When your vocal cords are swollen, it can be harder for them to vibrate properly, which can make your voice sound hoarse or weak. It can also be painful to talk or sing when your vocal cords are swollen. The best way to avoid losing your voice from yelling is to take breaks when you are speaking loudly and to try to use your normal speaking voice as much as possible.
