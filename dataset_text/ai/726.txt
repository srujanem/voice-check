During World War II, the United States used Sherman tanks because they were relatively cheap and easy to produce in large numbers. The United States was a large and industrial country, and it was able to produce a lot of tanks and other military equipment to support the Allied war effort. 
Sherman tanks were not necessarily "inferior" to German tanks like the Tiger. While the Tiger was certainly a formidable tank, it was also expensive and time-consuming to produce, which meant that the Germans could not build as many of them as the Americans could build Shermans. In addition, the Sherman tank had some strengths that made it useful in certain situations. For example, it was faster and more maneuverable than the Tiger, which made it better suited for some types of warfare. 
Overall, the use of Sherman tanks by the Americans was a matter of practicality and strategy. The United States needed a large number of tanks that it could produce quickly and inexpensively, and the Sherman tank fit the bill. While it may not have been the most powerful tank on the battlefield, it played a crucial role in the Allied victory in World War II.
