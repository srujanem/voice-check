Books gain a different smell over time because they are made of paper, which can absorb smells from the environment. This can happen because the paper absorbs things like the smell of the ink used to print the words, the smell of the glue used to bind the book together, and the smell of the materials used to make the cover.\n\nAs the book ages, it may also absorb the smell of the place where it is stored, such as a library or a bookshelf. For example, if a book is stored in a room with a lot of cooking, it may pick up the smell of food. Similarly, if a book is stored in a damp place, it may pick up the smell of mold.\n\nThe smell of a book can also change over time because of the way it is handled. If a book is touched a lot, the natural oils from a person's skin can be absorbed into the paper, which can change the smell.\n\nSo, to sum it up, books can gain a different smell over time because they absorb the smells around them and because they are touched by people.
