Entropy is a measure of the amount of disorder or randomness in a system. In the context of thermodynamics, it is a measure of the energy dispersal in a system. Imagine that you have a box with a bunch of balls inside it. If all the balls are evenly distributed throughout the box, the system has low entropy because the balls are organized. If the balls are all jumbled up and randomly distributed throughout the box, the system has high entropy because the balls are disorganized. In general, systems tend to move towards states of higher entropy over time. This is known as the second law of thermodynamics. For example, if you take a hot cup of coffee and leave it on the counter, the coffee will eventually cool down and the energy will be evenly distributed throughout the cup.
