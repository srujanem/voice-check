The stickiness of snow depends on the temperature and humidity of the air, as well as the type of snow. When the temperature is very cold and the air is dry, the snow will be dry and powdery, and it will be hard to make it stick together. On the other hand, if the temperature is warmer and the air is more humid, the snow will be wetter and stickier, and it will be easier to make snowballs.\n\nAnother factor that can affect the stickiness of snow is the type of snow. Different types of snow have different shapes and textures, which can make them easier or harder to pack together. For example, snow that has a lot of small, round flakes will usually be easier to pack into a snowball than snow that has larger, more irregular flakes.\n\nIn general, the stickier the snow is, the easier it will be to make snowballs. So if you're having trouble making snowballs, you might want to try looking for snow that is wetter and stickier, or try warming up the snow by holding it in your hands before you try to pack it into a snowball.
