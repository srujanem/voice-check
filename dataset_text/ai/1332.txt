A brain aneurysm is a weak spot in a blood vessel in the brain that bulges or balloons out. If the aneurysm ruptures, it can cause bleeding in the brain, which can be life-threatening. This bleeding is called a hemorrhagic stroke. 
When an aneurysm ruptures, it can cause a sudden and severe headache, often described as "the worst headache of my life." Other symptoms may include nausea, vomiting, stiff neck, and a loss of consciousness. 
If the bleeding is severe, it can lead to a brain injury or even death. This can happen very quickly, within just a few minutes or hours. 
Treatment for a ruptured aneurysm usually involves emergency surgery to repair the blood vessel and stop the bleeding. It's important to get medical help as soon as possible if you think you might have a ruptured aneurysm.
