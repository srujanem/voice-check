Filming empty streets and cities can be done in a few different ways, but one common method is to use a technique called "matte painting." This involves using special software to digitally paint or create an image of an empty street or cityscape, and then projecting that image onto a large screen or "green screen" on a set. The actors can then be filmed in front of the green screen, and the digital image of the empty city can be added in later during post-production (editing) to make it look like they are really in an empty city. Another way to film empty streets is to actually go to a location that is naturally empty, such as very early in the morning before people are out and about, or to a location that is not heavily populated. The crew can then film the actors in these naturally empty locations and use special effects to remove any people or cars that might accidentally be caught on camera. Finally, some filmmakers will use a combination of these techniques, filming some shots on a set with a green screen and others on location, and then combining them all together in post-production to create the final scene.
