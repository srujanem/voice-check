Nuclear weapons are so powerful because they use the energy that is released when the nuclei of atoms are either split (in a process called nuclear fission) or combined (in a process called nuclear fusion). These processes release a huge amount of energy, much more than can be released through chemical reactions like those that happen in ordinary explosives.\n\nWhen an atom is split, it releases a lot of energy because the nucleus is held together by a strong force called the "nuclear force." But if the nucleus is split into two smaller pieces, the nuclear force is no longer able to hold the pieces together, and they fly apart. This releases a huge amount of energy, which is what causes the explosion in a nuclear weapon.\n\nNuclear fusion is even more powerful than nuclear fission. In nuclear fusion, two light atoms are combined to form a heavier atom. This also releases a lot of energy, because the combined atom is slightly lighter than the two individual atoms. The difference in weight is converted into energy, according to Einstein's famous equation E=mc^2.\n\nSo, in short, nuclear weapons are so powerful because they release a huge amount of energy by either splitting or combining the nuclei of atoms. This energy is released all at once, causing a massive explosion.
