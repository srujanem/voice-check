Obamacare, also known as the Affordable Care Act, is a law that was passed by the U.S. government in 2010. It was designed to help make health insurance more accessible and affordable for people in the United States. Under Obamacare, employers are not required to provide health insurance to their employees. However, there are certain large employers (those with 50 or more full-time employees) that may face penalties if they do not offer health insurance to their employees and at least one of their employees receives a tax credit to help pay for insurance through the marketplace. Obamacare also established a marketplace, also known as an exchange, where individuals and small businesses can shop for and purchase health insurance. The marketplace offers a range of health insurance plans, and some people may be eligible for financial assistance to help pay for their insurance. Overall, the goal of Obamacare is to make it easier for people to get health insurance, either through their employer or through the marketplace. It also aims to make health insurance more affordable by providing financial assistance to people who need it and by making it illegal for insurance companies to deny coverage to people with pre-existing health conditions.
