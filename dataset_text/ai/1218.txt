When you turn on a vacuum cleaner, it sucks in air through the end of the hose, along with any dirt and debris. The motor inside the vacuum cleaner then uses its power to blow the air back out through a different opening, usually located near the bottom of the machine. 
The reason you can feel the air being blown out of the vacuum cleaner from a meter away is because the air is being pushed out with a lot of force. When you put your hand near the opening where the air is being blown out, you can feel the pressure of the air as it hits your hand. 
On the other hand, the suction on the end of the hose is created when the vacuum cleaner sucks in air through the hose. When you put your hand near the end of the hose, you can feel the vacuum cleaner trying to pull the air (and your hand) towards it. However, the suction force is not as strong as the force of the air being blown out of the machine, so you can only feel it a few inches away.
