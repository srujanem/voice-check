Time is measured the way it is because it is based on natural events that occur on Earth. For example, a day is defined as the time it takes for the Earth to rotate once on its axis, which is about 24 hours. This is why there are 24 hours in a day. Minutes and seconds are used to measure smaller increments of time. There are 60 minutes in an hour because the ancient Egyptians used a base-60 numbering system, which we still use today for measuring time and angles. There are 60 seconds in a minute for the same reason. Using a decimal system, like dividing a day into 10 "long-hours," would be a different way of measuring time, but it would not be based on any natural events that occur on Earth. It might be simpler in some ways, but it would also be less accurate because it would not match the way the Earth moves through space.
