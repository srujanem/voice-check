Immanuel Kant was a philosopher who lived in the late 18th and early 19th centuries. In his book "Critique of Pure Reason," he was criticizing the way that people had been thinking about knowledge and reality up until that point. Before Kant, many philosophers believed that we can know things about the world simply by thinking about them, without any need for experience or observations. They thought that our minds are like blank slates, and we can fill them up with knowledge by using reason alone. Kant disagreed with this way of thinking. He believed that our minds are not blank slates, but are actually pre-wired with certain ways of organizing and understanding the world. These ways of understanding the world are called "categories," and they help us to make sense of the things we see and experience. Instead of the old way of thinking, Kant offered a new system for understanding knowledge and reality. He called this system "transcendental idealism," and it said that we can never know the world as it really is, but only as it appears to us through our pre-wired categories. So, in short, Kant was criticizing the old way of thinking about knowledge and reality, and he offered a new system that said we can only understand the world as it appears to us through our pre-wired categories.
