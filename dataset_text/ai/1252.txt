A no-knock raid is a type of police operation in which officers enter a property without announcing their presence or identity beforehand. This type of raid is often used in cases where there is a risk that the suspects might be armed or dangerous, or where there is a risk that evidence might be destroyed if the suspects are alerted to the presence of the police. 
The rational basis for using no-knock raids is that they can allow police to take suspects by surprise and apprehend them more quickly and safely. This can be especially important in cases where the suspects are known to be violent or have a history of resisting arrest. 
However, no-knock raids can also be risky, as they can lead to misunderstandings and confrontations between the police and the people they are trying to arrest. To prevent police officers from being killed during a no-knock raid, it is important for the police to have a clear plan in place for how to safely enter the property and apprehend the suspects, and to properly train their officers in how to handle these types of situations. It is also important for the police to follow all relevant laws and procedures when conducting a no-knock raid, in order to minimize the risk of harm to both the officers and the suspects.
