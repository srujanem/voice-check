The Wall Street Protests, also known as the Occupy Wall Street movement, were a series of protests that took place in the fall of 2011 in New York City. The protests were organized by a group of activists who were upset about the state of the economy and the role that big banks and financial institutions played in causing the financial crisis of 2008. The protesters set up a camp in a park near Wall Street, which is the financial district of New York City where many big banks and financial institutions are located. They used the camp as a place to gather, plan protests, and make their voices heard. The protesters had a number of demands, including calls for more regulation of the financial industry, more help for people who had lost their homes due to the financial crisis, and a fairer distribution of wealth. The protests spread to other cities around the world, with people organizing their own protests and using the same "Occupy" name. The movement received a lot of attention from the media and attracted the support of many people, but it eventually ended in late 2011 and early 2012 as the protesters were removed from their camps by police.
