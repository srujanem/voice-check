When a spacecraft thruster is fired, it pushes against the gas or other matter that is being expelled from it. This is similar to how a rocket works - the rocket's engine burns fuel and expels hot gases out of the back, and it is the momentum of these gases that propels the rocket forward. In the case of a spacecraft thruster, the gas being expelled can be a variety of substances, such as cold water, nitrogen, or even just plain old air. So to summarize, when a spacecraft thruster is fired, it creates a small amount of gas or matter, and it is the movement of this gas or matter that allows the spacecraft to adjust its position. It works a bit like when you blow up a balloon and let it go - the air rushing out of the balloon pushes the balloon in the opposite direction.
