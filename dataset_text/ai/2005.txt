Lightbulbs use a small device called a resistor to control the flow of electricity through the bulb. The resistor is designed to allow a specific amount of electricity to pass through the bulb, which determines the brightness of the light. A 60 watt bulb has a larger resistor than a 20 watt bulb, so it allows less electricity to pass through and produces a dimmer light. A 20 watt bulb has a smaller resistor, so it allows more electricity to pass through and produces a brighter light. Both bulbs can be used in the same socket because the socket is designed to provide a certain amount of electricity, and the bulb's resistor controls how much of that electricity is used.
