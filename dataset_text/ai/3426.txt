The QWERTY keyboard layout was designed in the 1870s by Christopher Latham Sholes, the inventor of the first practical typewriter. At the time, typewriters had mechanical keys that would jam if they were struck too quickly in succession. To prevent this from happening, Sholes rearranged the letters on the keyboard so that commonly used letters would be spaced as far apart from each other as possible, reducing the likelihood of jams. The QWERTY layout has become the standard for keyboards in the United States, while the QWERTZ layout is used in many European countries. The letters on these keyboards are arranged in a specific order because that is the most efficient and comfortable way to type for many people.
