The unit of pressure called "millimeters of mercury" (mmHg) is still used today because it is a very convenient unit to use for certain types of pressure measurements. One reason it is convenient is because it is easy to visualize. The pressure of a gas can be thought of like a weight pressing down on something, and the unit "millimeters of mercury" is a way to measure how heavy that weight is. Imagine you have a tube filled with a liquid, like water or mercury. If you place that tube on a scale, you can measure how much the liquid weighs by seeing how much the scale weighs. Now, imagine that you put a hole in the bottom of the tube and let some of the liquid out. As the liquid comes out of the hole, it creates a pressure, or force, on the inside of the tube. The more liquid that comes out of the hole, the more pressure there is inside the tube. So, when we measure pressure in millimeters of mercury, we are basically measuring how much liquid is being forced out of a hole in a tube. This is a very useful way to measure pressure because it is easy to do and doesn't require any special equipment. It is also a very accurate way to measure pressure because the density of mercury is very constant, which means that the weight of a certain volume of mercury will always be the same. I hope this helps to explain why we still use millimeters of mercury as a unit of pressure. It may seem like a strange or "jerry-rigged" unit, but it has actually been very useful for many years and is still used today in many different fields.
