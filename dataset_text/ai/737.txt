Cats are naturally skittish and easily scared because they have evolved to be cautious around potential threats. In the wild, cats need to be constantly alert and ready to defend themselves or flee in order to survive. This instinctual behavior helps them avoid danger and stay alive. 
Even though domestic cats are generally safe from predators, they still have this natural instinct to be cautious and alert. This is why they may startle easily or run away when they hear loud noises or sense movement nearby. 
As for why domestic cats don't display more dominant traits like their larger cousins (such as lions and tigers), it's important to remember that domestic cats have been domesticated over many generations. This means that they have been bred to be more friendly and social with humans, rather than displaying the more dominant and aggressive behaviors that are common in their wild counterparts. Domestic cats are also generally much smaller and less powerful than larger cats like lions and tigers, so they may not need to rely on dominant behavior as much in order to survive.
