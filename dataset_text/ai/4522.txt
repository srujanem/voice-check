Crumple zones are a safety feature in cars that are designed to absorb the energy of a collision and protect the passengers inside. When a car crashes, the energy from the collision has to go somewhere. If that energy is not absorbed, it can be transferred to the passengers in the car, which can cause serious injury or death.\n\nCrumple zones are made of materials that are designed to deform or "crumple" when they are subjected to a lot of force. This helps to absorb some of the energy from the collision and reduce the amount of force that is transferred to the passengers.\n\nIt might seem like it would be safer to have a car that is made of materials that do not crumple and can withstand impact, but this would actually be less safe. A car that is made of materials that do not deform would transfer more of the collision energy to the passengers, potentially causing more serious injury or death.
