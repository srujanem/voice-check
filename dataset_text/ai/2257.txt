Sure! Terminal velocity is the maximum speed that an object can reach as it falls through a fluid, like air or water. The reason an object can't keep accelerating is because as it falls, it encounters more and more air or water molecules, which creates a force called drag that slows the object down. The faster the object is moving, the more drag it experiences, until it reaches a speed where the force of gravity pulling it downward is exactly balanced by the force of drag pushing it upward. At this point, the object is moving at its terminal velocity and it can't keep accelerating because the forces acting on it are in balance. Does that make sense?
