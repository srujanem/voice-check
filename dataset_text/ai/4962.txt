Viruses are very small infectious agents that can replicate only inside the living cells of an organism. They are not considered to be alive because they do not have cells of their own and they do not have the ability to carry out many of the functions that are associated with living organisms, such as metabolism and growth.\n\nHowever, viruses do have a specific structure and they contain genetic material, either DNA or RNA, that carries instructions for making more viruses. When a virus infects a cell, it uses the cell's machinery to replicate itself and to produce new viruses. This is how viruses "know" what to do. They are not able to think or make decisions on their own, but they are able to carry out specific functions based on the instructions encoded in their genetic material.\n\nSo, to answer your question, viruses do not "know" to attack cells in the same way that a living organism might. They simply follow the instructions encoded in their genetic material, which leads them to enter and replicate inside the cells of their host organism. It is not by chance, but it is also not a conscious decision made by the virus.
