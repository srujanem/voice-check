Steroids are a type of medication that mimic the effects of hormones that are naturally produced by the body. They are often used to treat conditions in which the body does not produce enough of its own steroids, such as delayed puberty or certain types of cancer.\n\nSteroids work by attaching to specific proteins in the body called "steroid receptors." These proteins are found in many different cells and tissues, and they help to regulate various functions in the body. When a steroid medication attaches to a steroid receptor, it can change the way that the cell functions and can help to reduce inflammation, swelling, and other symptoms of certain conditions.\n\nFor example, if someone has an autoimmune disorder that causes inflammation in the joints, a steroid medication might be prescribed to help reduce that inflammation and improve the person's quality of life. Similarly, if someone is undergoing chemotherapy for cancer, a steroid medication might be given to help reduce the side effects of the treatment.\n\nSteroids can be taken in different forms, including as a pill, injection, or cream. It is important to follow the instructions of a healthcare provider when taking steroids, as they can have side effects and can interact with other medications.
