The bladder is a muscular sac in the lower abdomen that stores urine until it is ready to be expelled from the body. When the bladder is empty of urine, it is not completely empty. It still contains some small amount of tissue and cells that make up the lining and walls of the bladder. The bladder also contains a small amount of fluid called "interstitial fluid," which is the fluid that surrounds and bathes the cells of the body.\n\nThe bladder is not completely empty because it is constantly being refilled with urine. As the bladder fills with urine, it stretches to accommodate the increasing volume. When the bladder becomes full, it sends a signal to the brain to let you know that it's time to go to the bathroom. When you go to the bathroom and urinate, the bladder becomes empty again.\n\nThe bladder is not a void and it is not filled with air. It is a muscular organ that stores urine until it is ready to be expelled from the body.
