It's true that you have the right to remain silent and to not answer questions from the police. This right is protected by the Fifth Amendment of the US Constitution, which is part of the Bill of Rights. The Fifth Amendment protects you from self-incrimination, which means that you cannot be forced to say or do anything that could be used against you in a criminal case. 
However, sometimes people choose to speak to the police even if they have the right to remain silent. There can be many reasons for this. Some people may feel that they have nothing to hide and want to cooperate with the police to help them solve a crime. Others may feel that they can explain their actions or clear up any misunderstandings, and that talking to the police will help them avoid being charged with a crime. 
It's important to remember that if you do decide to speak to the police, you should be very careful about what you say. It's always a good idea to have an attorney present if you are being questioned by the police. An attorney can advise you on your rights and help you avoid saying anything that could incriminate you.
