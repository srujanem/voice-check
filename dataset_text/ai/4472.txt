One way to protect a script is to only provide a "pitch" or summary of the story to potential buyers, rather than the entire script. This way, the buyer can get an idea of what the script is about without having access to all of the specific details and dialogue.\n\nAnother way to protect a script is to have the buyer sign a legally binding document called a "nondisclosure agreement" (NDA). This is a contract that requires the buyer to keep the script a secret and not share it with anyone else. If the buyer violates the NDA, they could be sued for damages.\n\nIt's also a good idea to register the script with the Writers Guild of America or a similar organization, which can provide some legal protection in case the script is stolen or used without permission.
