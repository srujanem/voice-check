In general, mobile devices and computers have different operating systems and security measures in place. On a computer, you might have more control over the system because you have the ability to install and run different software. On a mobile device, the operating system is typically more restricted and you may not have as much control over it. One reason for this difference is that mobile devices are often used for a wider range of purposes and may need to be more secure to protect sensitive information. For example, a mobile device might be used to store personal information, such as contacts, emails, and financial information. It's important to keep this information secure to protect it from unauthorized access. On the other hand, rooting or jailbreaking a mobile device involves making changes to the operating system to allow for greater control and access to sensitive files. This can be useful for certain purposes, such as running specialized software or customizing the device. However, it also carries some risks, such as the possibility of inadvertently introducing vulnerabilities or compromising the security of the device. In summary, mobile devices are often more restricted in order to protect sensitive information and maintain security, while computers may have more flexibility and control due to their different operating systems and use cases.
