A hostile takeover is when a company or group of investors tries to take control of another company without the support or approval of the company's board of directors. This can be done by buying a large number of shares of the company's stock, making an offer to buy the company, or trying to replace the company's board of directors with their own people. A non-hostile takeover, on the other hand, is when the company being taken over is willing to accept the offer and sell their shares to the other company. This type of takeover is often called a "friendly takeover." In both cases, the goal is for the company or group of investors to gain control of the company and make decisions about how it is run. However, a hostile takeover is often more difficult and can involve more conflict, because the company being taken over may not want to be bought or may not agree with the terms of the offer.
