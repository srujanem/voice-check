TV shows and movies are created for entertainment, and they often feature actors, writers, and other creative professionals who contribute their talent and work to the production. These individuals are usually paid for their contributions, and in order to use their work in the final product, the producers of the TV show or movie need to get permission from them in the form of a release form. On the other hand, news broadcasts are typically not created for entertainment, but rather to inform the public about important events and issues. News organizations often rely on footage and information from various sources, such as other news organizations, government agencies, and private individuals, in order to report the news. These sources may not be paid for their contributions, and they may not be asked to sign a release form. Instead, news organizations rely on the principle of fair use, which allows them to use limited amounts of copyrighted material without permission for the purpose of reporting the news. I hope this helps to clarify the difference between TV shows and news broadcasts, and why release forms are sometimes required for one but not the other.
