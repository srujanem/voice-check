The "feels like" temperature, also known as the "apparent temperature," is a measure of how hot or cold it feels to the human body based on a combination of the actual temperature, humidity, and wind speed. It is called the "apparent temperature" because it takes into account how the various weather conditions affect the body's ability to cool or warm itself.\n\nFor example, if the actual temperature is 80 degrees Fahrenheit and there is high humidity, it might feel much hotter because the moisture in the air can make it harder for your body to release heat through sweating. Similarly, if the actual temperature is 50 degrees Fahrenheit and there is a strong wind, it might feel much colder because the wind can blow away the warm air next to your skin and make it harder for your body to stay warm.\n\nSo the "feels like" temperature is not the same as the actual temperature, but rather a measure of how hot or cold it feels to the human body based on a combination of weather conditions.
