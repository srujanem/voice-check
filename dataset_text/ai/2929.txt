The concept of the universe being both infinite and expanding can seem contradictory at first, but it's actually a fundamental part of the Big Bang Theory, which is the most widely accepted explanation for the origins and evolution of the universe. According to this theory, the universe began as a singularity, a point in space and time that was infinitely small and infinitely dense. At some point, this singularity underwent a massive expansion, which is known as the Big Bang. This expansion has been ongoing for the past 13.8 billion years, and it continues to this day. As the universe expands, it creates more space, meaning that the total size of the universe is constantly increasing. This is what we mean when we say that the universe is expanding. At the same time, the universe is also infinite, which means that it has no boundaries or edges. It's not like a balloon that's being blown up, where the edges of the balloon represent the limits of the universe. Instead, the universe is thought to be unbounded, with no end or edge. So, to sum it up: the universe is expanding because it's constantly creating more space, but it's also infinite because it has no boundaries or edges. I hope that helps to clarify things a bit!
