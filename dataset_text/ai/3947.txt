The Social Security system is funded through a payroll tax that is paid by workers and their employers. This tax only covers a portion of the cost of the program, however, and the rest of the money needed to pay benefits comes from the surplus revenue that the program has generated over the years. This surplus is held in trust and invested in special U.S. government bonds. The problem is that the number of people who are receiving benefits from Social Security is increasing, while the number of people who are paying into the system is decreasing. This is because the population is getting older and there are fewer younger people who are working and paying into the system to support the older people who are retired and receiving benefits. As a result, the surplus that has been built up is starting to be used up and there may not be enough money in the future to pay all of the promised benefits. This is why some people are worried that the Social Security system may be in trouble.
