Currency counting machines use sensors to detect the presence and position of a bill as it is fed into the machine. When the machine detects a bill, it uses a mechanism, such as a set of rollers or a vacuum, to grab the bill and move it through the machine. The machine is able to count the bills quickly because it doesn't have to think about what it's doing. It just follows a set of instructions over and over again to count the bills as they pass through the machine. Think of it like a conveyor belt at a factory. The conveyor belt moves products along a set path and workers standing alongside the belt can grab a product off the belt and do something with it. The currency counting machine works in a similar way, except it's grabbing and counting bills instead of products.
