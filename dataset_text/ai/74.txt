The Golden Gate Bridge in San Francisco is an iconic masterpiece of modern suspension bridge engineering. Completed in 1937 under the direction of chief engineer Joseph Strauss, the bridge spans a 1.7-mile strait connecting the San Francisco Peninsula to Marin County. Designed to withstand ferocious ocean winds and strong tidal currents, its distinctive International Orange color was chosen to enhance visibility through dense coastal fog. The suspension cables contain thousands of individual steel wires, making the structure a resilient symbol of human ingenuity and civil engineering capability.
