In a vehicle with an automatic transmission, there is a special component called a transmission control unit (TCU) that controls when the gears should be changed. The TCU uses input from various sensors to determine the appropriate time to shift gears. For example, the TCU may use information from the engine speed sensor, the throttle position sensor, and the vehicle speed sensor to determine when to shift gears. The TCU sends a signal to the transmission to tell it when to shift gears. The transmission then uses a system of hydraulic fluid, clutches, and bands to engage and disengage different gears as needed. When you put the vehicle into "drive" or "reverse," the TCU will automatically select the appropriate gear for you based on the vehicle's speed and the amount of acceleration you are requesting.
