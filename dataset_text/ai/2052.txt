Planets and satellites stay in orbit around a larger body because of the force of gravity pulling them towards it. The force of gravity depends on the mass of the two objects and the distance between them. When an object is in orbit, it is constantly falling towards the larger body, but it is also moving very fast horizontally. This means that it misses the surface of the larger body and continues to fall in a curve, rather than crashing into it. Imagine you are standing on a hill and you roll a ball down the hill. The ball will roll down the hill in a curved path, rather than just falling straight down to the ground. It's the same idea with planets and satellites. They are constantly falling towards the larger body, but because of their horizontal motion, they miss the surface and keep going in a curve. As long as the horizontal speed of the orbiting object is high enough, it will stay in orbit and not crash into the larger body. The distance between the orbiting object and the larger body will stay roughly the same, as long as there are no other forces acting on the object (like the gravity of other objects nearby).
