A citizen's arrest is when a person who is not a law enforcement officer tries to detain someone who they believe has committed a crime. In order to make a citizen's arrest, the person must have witnessed the crime being committed, and they must have probable cause to believe that the person they are trying to detain is the person who committed the crime. If a police officer is breaking the law, it is not appropriate for a citizen to try to make a citizen's arrest. Instead, the citizen should report the officer's behavior to their supervisor or to an external oversight body, such as a police review board or an independent investigator. It is possible that a citizen's arrest has been successful in recent memory, but it is not advisable for a person to try to make a citizen's arrest, especially if the person they are trying to detain is a police officer. It is important for people to remember that law enforcement officers are trained to handle situations that may be dangerous or confrontational, and trying to detain someone who is a police officer could potentially lead to a dangerous situation for the person making the arrest and for the officer.
