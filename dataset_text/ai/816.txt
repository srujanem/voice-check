Scientists think that there could be more than three dimensions because there are some theories in physics that suggest the existence of extra dimensions. For example, one theory called string theory suggests that there could be as many as 10 dimensions. 
As for time being considered the fourth dimension, it's a bit more complicated. In our everyday experience, we move through three dimensions of space (left/right, up/down, and forward/backward) and we experience the passage of time. However, in the language of physics, time is often treated as a dimension just like the three dimensions of space. 
Think of it like this: Imagine you are an ant crawling on a sheet of paper. You can only move left/right and up/down on the sheet, so you are only aware of two dimensions. But if you could somehow "travel" through the paper, you would be moving in a third dimension (into and out of the paper). Time is a little bit like that third dimension - we can move "forward" in time, but we can't move "backward" in time like we can move up/down or left/right in space. Does that make sense?
