The James Webb Space Telescope operates approximately one million miles from Earth, capturing infrared light from the universe's earliest galaxies. Equipped with a massive gold-coated mirror and a tennis-court-sized sunshield, it peers through dense interstellar dust to reveal cosmic structures formed shortly after the Big Bang. Beyond studying deep space origins, the telescope analyzes the atmospheric compositions of distant exoplanets to search for chemical biosignatures. Webb's incredible resolution continues to provide unprecedented insights into star formation, black holes, and the fundamental evolution of our cosmos.
