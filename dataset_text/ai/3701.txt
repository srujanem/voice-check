When you fall, you are being pulled down by the force of gravity. This force is always the same, no matter how far you have fallen. However, as you fall, you are also moving faster and faster. This is because the force of gravity is pulling you down, and there is nothing to stop you from moving faster. As you fall, you will keep accelerating until you reach a certain speed, called terminal velocity. This is the fastest speed you can reach while falling. At terminal velocity, the force of gravity is balanced by the air resistance, or drag, that you experience as you fall. This is why you stop accelerating at some point - the air resistance is strong enough to balance out the force of gravity, so you stop moving faster and faster. Imagine you are holding a ball and you drop it. At first, the ball will fall slowly because it is not moving very fast. But as it falls, it will pick up speed and start moving faster and faster. Eventually, it will reach a point where it is falling as fast as it can, and it will stop accelerating. This is similar to what happens when you fall.
