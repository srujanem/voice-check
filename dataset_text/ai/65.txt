The construction of the Great Wall of China spanned over two millennia, originating during the 7th century BCE as disparate regional fortifications. Under Emperor Qin Shi Huang, these separate walls were unified to protect the northern borders of the Chinese empire against nomadic invasions. The most famous, well-preserved sections were built during the Ming Dynasty using durable brick and stone masonry. Stretching over 13,000 miles across mountains and deserts, the Great Wall served not only as a military defensive barrier, but also as a vital trade control corridor.
