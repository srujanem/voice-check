Allergies are caused when the body's immune system mistakes a normally harmless substance for a dangerous invader, like a virus or bacteria. When this happens, the immune system starts to produce special proteins called antibodies to attack the substance. These antibodies can cause various symptoms, such as itchy skin, sneezing, and trouble breathing.\n\nSometimes, people develop allergies to things that they were previously able to tolerate. This can happen for a variety of reasons. For example, the body's immune system might become more sensitive to a particular substance over time, or the person might be exposed to higher levels of the substance than before.\n\nIt's also possible for people to develop allergies to things that they have never been exposed to before. This is called a "new" allergy. For example, a person who has never had an allergy to peanuts might suddenly develop an allergy to peanuts later in life.\n\nIn general, allergies can appear at any age. Some people are born with allergies, while others develop them later in life. There is no way to predict when someone might develop an allergy. If you are concerned about your allergies, it is a good idea to see a doctor for testing and treatment.
