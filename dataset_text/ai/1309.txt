Rainwater harvesting is the practice of collecting and storing rainwater for future use. It is generally not illegal to collect rainwater in the United States. However, there may be certain regulations or laws in certain states that limit or prohibit the collection of rainwater, or that regulate how it can be collected and used. 
For example, some states have laws that regulate the use of rainwater for drinking water, to ensure that it meets certain quality standards. Other states may have laws that regulate the use of rainwater for irrigation or other purposes, to prevent it from being used in a way that could harm the environment or cause problems for other water users. 
It's also important to note that in some cases, the use of rainwater may be restricted on a local level, rather than at the state level. For example, a city or county might have its own regulations or laws that govern the collection and use of rainwater. 
In general, the laws and regulations surrounding rainwater harvesting are designed to protect public health and the environment, and to ensure that rainwater is used in a responsible and sustainable way.
