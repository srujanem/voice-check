Suicide and assisted suicide are generally illegal because they are considered to be harmful and a violation of the individual's inherent right to life. Many people believe that every person has the right to live their life to the fullest and that it is not up to anyone else to decide when that life should end. Additionally, some people believe that suicide and assisted suicide go against the values of many societies and religions, which often view suicide as a sin or a moral failing. 
There are also practical considerations that can make suicide and assisted suicide difficult to allow. For example, if someone is in immense pain, there may be medical treatments or other forms of support that can help alleviate that pain and make their life more bearable. Additionally, some people who are considering suicide may be experiencing a temporary crisis or mental health issue that could be treated with therapy or medication. 
Overall, the decision to make suicide and assisted suicide illegal is typically based on a combination of moral, ethical, and practical considerations.
