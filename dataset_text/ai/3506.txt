In the United States, movie theaters traditionally did not have reserved seating because it was thought to be too complicated and time-consuming for customers to choose their specific seats when buying tickets. Additionally, theaters in the United States tend to be larger than theaters in other countries, and it can be difficult to predict how many people will want to sit in certain sections of the theater. Instead, movie theaters in the United States typically have an "open seating" policy, which means that customers can choose any available seat when they enter the theater. This allows customers to sit wherever they like, but it can also mean that popular movies or showtimes may have more competition for the best seats. In recent years, however, some movie theaters in the United States have started to offer reserved seating as an option. This allows customers to select their specific seats when they purchase their tickets online or at the box office. This can be convenient for customers who want to guarantee that they will have a specific seat, but it can also mean that theaters may be less flexible in terms of seating arrangements on the day of the movie.
