It's not necessarily that the internet is "priced by speed" and cell phone data is "priced by amount." It's just that these are two different types of services that are typically offered by different types of companies, and so they are priced differently. For internet service, companies often offer a range of speeds at different price points, with faster speeds generally costing more. The reason for this is that faster internet speeds allow you to do more things online, such as streaming high-definition video or playing online games, which require a lot of data to be transferred in a short amount of time. On the other hand, cell phone data plans are typically priced based on the amount of data you can use each month. This is because cell phone companies have to pay to use the networks that allow you to connect to the internet on your phone, and they need to cover their costs. The more data you use, the more the cell phone company has to pay, so they charge you more to use more data. So, it's not really about the internet being "priced by speed" and cell phones being "priced by data." It's just that these are two different types of services that are typically priced differently because of the different ways that they are used and the different costs involved in providing them.
