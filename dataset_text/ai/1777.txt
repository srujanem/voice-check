Manual driving, also known as "stick shift" or "standard" driving, refers to a type of transmission in a car that requires the driver to manually shift gears using a gear stick and clutch pedal. In an automatic transmission car, the gears are changed automatically by the car's computer. Manual driving is considered better by some people because it gives the driver more control over the car. With a manual transmission, the driver can choose when to shift gears based on the speed and power needed for the current driving situation. This can be helpful in certain situations, such as driving up a steep hill or trying to pass another vehicle on the highway. However, manual driving also requires more skill and attention from the driver. The driver must coordinate the use of the gear stick and clutch pedal to smoothly shift gears, and must also pay attention to the car's RPM (revolutions per minute) to ensure that the engine is running at the right speed for the gear that has been selected. Automatic transmission cars are generally easier to drive and may be preferred by some people because they do not require the driver to shift gears manually. They can also be more fuel efficient because the computer can optimize gear shifts for maximum efficiency. However, they may not provide the same level of control as a manual transmission.
