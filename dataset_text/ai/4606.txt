Cold working and hot working are processes used to shape and form metal.\n\nCold working is a process where metal is shaped or formed at a temperature that is below its recrystallization temperature. This means that the metal is not heated up before it is worked on. Cold working can be done by pressing, stretching, or bending the metal. It can also be done using tools such as hammers, dies, and punches. Cold working makes the metal stronger and harder because it changes the structure of the metal at the atomic level.\n\nHot working is a process where metal is shaped or formed at a temperature that is above its recrystallization temperature. This means that the metal is heated up before it is worked on. Hot working is often used to shape metals that are difficult to work with when they are cold, such as aluminum or copper. The rivet is placed in a hole in one piece of metal, and the rivet gun is used to squeeze the other end of the rivet so that it is held firmly in place.
