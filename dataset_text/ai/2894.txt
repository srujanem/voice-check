Wind is caused by differences in air pressure. When the air pressure is higher in one place than it is in another, the air will try to move from the area of higher pressure to the area of lower pressure. This movement of air is what we call wind. Here's an example to help explain it: Imagine you have a balloon that you blow up and then let go. The air inside the balloon will rush out of the opening, causing the balloon to move. The air moving out of the balloon is like the wind moving from an area of high pressure to an area of low pressure. So, wind starts when there is a difference in air pressure between two different places. The wind will then blow from the place with the higher air pressure to the place with the lower air pressure. This can happen for a variety of reasons, such as differences in temperature, humidity, and the amount of sunlight that an area receives.
