Inflation and deflation are both economic terms that refer to changes in the general price level of goods and services in an economy. When prices rise, we call it inflation, and when prices fall, we call it deflation. Inflation can have a number of different causes, but it generally occurs when there is too much money chasing too few goods and services. When this happens, people are willing to pay higher prices for the things they want to buy, and sellers can charge more because people are willing to pay it. This can lead to a general increase in prices over time. On the other hand, deflation occurs when there is not enough money chasing too many goods and services. In this case, people are not willing to pay as much for the things they want to buy, and sellers have to lower their prices in order to attract buyers. This can lead to a general decrease in prices over time. Inflation and deflation can have a big impact on an economy's growth and GDP (gross domestic product). GDP is a measure of the total value of all goods and services produced in a country over a certain period of time. If prices are rising (inflation), then the value of the goods and services produced will also be higher, and this can lead to an increase in GDP. On the other hand, if prices are falling (deflation), then the value of the goods and services produced will also be lower, and this can lead to a decrease in GDP. Inflation and deflation can also affect the purchasing power of money. If prices are rising (inflation), then the same amount of money will be able to buy fewer goods and services. On the other hand, if prices are falling (deflation), then the same amount of money will be able to buy more goods and services.
