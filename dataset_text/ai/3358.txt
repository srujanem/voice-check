Edward Snowden was a former National Security Agency (NSA) contractor who leaked classified information about the U.S. government's surveillance programs to the media in 2013. The information that Snowden leaked showed that the U.S. government was collecting and storing vast amounts of data about the phone calls, emails, and internet activity of ordinary citizens, even if they were not suspected of any wrongdoing. This included information about who people were communicating with, when they were communicating, and the content of their communication. Snowden's leaks caused a major public controversy and sparked a debate about the balance between national security and privacy. Some people believed that Snowden was a hero for exposing the extent of the government's surveillance programs and bringing these issues to the public's attention. Others believed that Snowden had betrayed his country and endangered national security by revealing sensitive information. Overall, Snowden's leaks revealed the existence and extent of several controversial U.S. government surveillance programs, including PRISM, which collected data from internet companies, and Echelon, which intercepted and analyzed global communications. These leaks helped to expose the extent to which the U.S. government was collecting and storing private information about its citizens and sparked a debate about the balance between national security and privacy.
