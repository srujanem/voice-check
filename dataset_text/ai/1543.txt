Christianity has its roots in Judaism, and many of the practices and beliefs of Christianity come from Judaism. However, Christians do not celebrate Jewish holidays for a few reasons. 
First, Christians believe that Jesus is the Son of God and the savior of the world, and that his death and resurrection fulfilled the prophecies of the Old Testament and the requirements of the Jewish law. Because of this, Christians have their own set of holidays that focus on the life and teachings of Jesus, such as Christmas and Easter. 
Second, after the death of Jesus, the early Christian church began to spread throughout the Roman Empire and beyond. As the Christian faith spread to people who were not Jewish, it became more inclusive and focused on the message of Jesus, rather than the specific practices and traditions of Judaism. 
Finally, Christians and Jews have had a complicated relationship over the centuries, with periods of conflict and persecution. As a result, many Christians have chosen to focus on their own beliefs and traditions rather than participating in the holidays of a faith that is not their own. 
Overall, while Christians have a deep respect for the teachings and traditions of Judaism, they have their own set of beliefs and practices that are central to their faith. As a result, they do not celebrate Jewish holidays, but rather focus on the holidays and traditions that are specific to Christianity.
