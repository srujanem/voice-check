Unions are organizations that represent the collective interests of workers. They are made up of workers who come together to negotiate with their employer for things like better pay, safer working conditions, and better benefits. Unions have the authority to negotiate on behalf of their members because the workers have chosen to be represented by the union. The union has the power to negotiate with the employer because it represents a large group of workers, and the employer may be more willing to make changes if it means avoiding a strike or other action by the union. The difference between unions that are considered good and those that are considered bad is often a matter of perspective. Some people might think that a union is good if it is able to negotiate better pay and benefits for its members, while others might think that a union is bad if it causes disruptions or conflicts in the workplace. Ultimately, the effectiveness of a union depends on the specific circumstances and the goals of the workers it represents.
