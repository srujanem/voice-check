The loudness war is a term used to describe the trend of increasing the loudness of music recordings, often at the expense of dynamic range. Dynamic range refers to the difference between the loudest and softest parts of a piece of music. When dynamic range is sacrificed in the process of making a record louder, it can make the music sound less interesting and dynamic because the volume of all the parts of the song are more similar. Some people in the music industry believe that making a record as loud as possible can help it stand out and grab the listener's attention. However, this trend has led to a lot of recordings that are so loud that they are actually unpleasant to listen to because the dynamic range has been compromised. Despite this, the loudness war continues because some people in the music industry still think that loud records are more appealing to listeners. It would make more sense to preserve the dynamic range of a recording, but unfortunately this is not always a priority.
