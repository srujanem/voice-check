A credit union is a type of financial institution that is owned and controlled by its members. It is similar to a bank in that it offers a range of financial services, such as checking and savings accounts, loans, and credit cards. However, unlike a bank, which is owned by shareholders and is primarily focused on making profits for them, a credit union is owned by its members and exists to serve their financial needs. Credit unions are often considered to be more community-oriented and customer-friendly than banks, and they may offer lower fees and better interest rates on loans and savings accounts. Credit unions are typically organized around a particular group of people who share something in common, such as living in the same area, working for the same company, or belonging to the same organization. Members of a credit union must meet certain eligibility requirements in order to join. Once you become a member of a credit union, you are able to take advantage of the financial products and services it offers, and you also have a say in how the credit union is run. So, when people talk about dropping their bank and joining a credit union, they are generally talking about switching from a traditional bank to a credit union in order to take advantage of the potentially lower fees, better rates, and more community-focused approach that credit unions offer.
