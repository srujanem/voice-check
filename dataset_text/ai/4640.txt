Automatic windshield wipers use sensors to detect when the windshield is wet. There are different types of sensors that can be used for this purpose, but one common type is a moisture sensor. This sensor consists of two electrodes that are separated by a small gap. When the windshield is dry, there is very little resistance between the electrodes, but when the windshield is wet, the water acts as a conductor and the resistance between the electrodes decreases. This change in resistance is used to determine when the wipers should be activated.\n\nTo determine how wet the windshield is, some wipers use a combination of sensors to measure both the amount of moisture on the windshield and the speed at which the wipers should move. For example, the wipers might move more quickly when the windshield is very wet, and more slowly when it is only slightly damp.\n\nOverall, automatic windshield wipers use a combination of sensors and algorithms to determine when and how fast to move, helping to keep the windshield clear and your visibility good while driving.
