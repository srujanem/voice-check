In most planetary systems, there is a star at the center. This is because planets form from a disk of gas and dust that surrounds the star, and this material eventually clumps together to form planets. The star is the source of heat and light for the planetary system, and it also provides the gravitational force that keeps the planets in orbit around it. 
There are also some planetary systems that do not have a star at the center. For example, there are planets that orbit around a black hole, or that are not part of any solar system at all (these are known as rogue planets). However, these types of planetary systems are relatively rare, and most planetary systems have a star at the center. 
So, to answer your question: it is not always the case that there is a glowing orb in the middle of a planetary system, but in most cases, there is a star at the center of the system.
