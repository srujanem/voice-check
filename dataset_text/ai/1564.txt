The shooting of Michael Brown in Ferguson, Missouri in 2014, and the subsequent protests and civil unrest, were about race because of the history of racism and discrimination against Black people in the United States. 
In this particular case, the shooting of an unarmed Black man by a white police officer led to outrage and protests in the community, and it was seen as a symptom of a larger problem of racial inequality and police violence against Black people. The protests and demonstrations that followed were not just about this one incident, but about the broader issues of racial injustice and discrimination that have persisted for centuries in the United States. 
It's important to understand that racism is not just about individual acts of hate or prejudice, but about systems and institutions that are built on and perpetuate discrimination against certain groups of people. The events in Ferguson brought these issues to the forefront and sparked a national conversation about race and inequality.
