Switzerland was able to remain neutral during World War II because it was not invaded by Germany or any other country. The country was able to maintain its neutrality because it had a strong military and because it was surrounded by other countries that were also neutral or were allies of Germany. 
Being neutral means that a country does not take sides in a war and does not fight against any other country. Switzerland decided to be neutral in World War II because it wanted to avoid being caught in the middle of the conflict and being damaged or destroyed. 
By staying neutral, Switzerland was able to continue trading with other countries and providing goods and services that were needed by both sides of the war. This helped the country to remain economically strong and stable, which also made it less attractive as a target for invasion. 
In addition, Switzerland was able to use its neutrality to provide a safe haven for refugees and others who were trying to escape the violence of the war. This helped to further solidify its reputation as a neutral country and made it less likely that it would be invaded.
