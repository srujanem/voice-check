Yes, different GPS companies have their own satellites that their customers can use to determine their location. GPS, or Global Positioning System, is a system of satellites that orbit the Earth and transmit signals to GPS receivers on the ground. These signals can be used to determine the location, altitude, and time of the receiver. GPS satellites are owned and operated by the US government, but other countries also have their own satellite navigation systems, such as the Russian GLONASS system and the European Union's Galileo system. GPS receivers can usually communicate with satellites from multiple systems to improve accuracy and reliability. As for the number of devices that can communicate with a satellite, it depends on the specific satellite and the type of communication being used. Each satellite has a limited amount of bandwidth, which is the amount of data that can be transmitted over a given period of time. So if too many devices try to communicate with the satellite at the same time, the satellite might not be able to handle all the requests and some of the devices might not receive a signal. However, satellites are designed to handle a large number of requests simultaneously, so it would take a very large number of devices trying to communicate with a satellite at the same time to overwhelm it.
