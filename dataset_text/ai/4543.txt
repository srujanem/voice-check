Anti-theft scanners, also known as EAS (electronic article surveillance) systems, are used by stores to help prevent shoplifting. These systems usually consist of two parts: a sensor or antenna that is placed at the entrance or exit of the store, and tags or labels that are attached to items for sale.\n\nWhen a customer enters the store with an item that has a tag or label attached to it, the sensor or antenna at the entrance sends out a radio frequency signal. This signal is picked up by the tag or label, which then sends back a response. If the response is not received by the sensor or antenna, it may set off an alarm to alert store staff.\n\nThere are a few reasons why the alarm may not always go off as intended:\n\nThe tag or label may be damaged or not functioning properly, which can prevent it from responding to the radio frequency signal.\n\nThe sensor or antenna may be malfunctioning or not positioned properly, which can also prevent it from detecting the response from the tag or label.\n\nThe item being brought into the store may not have a tag or label attached to it, in which case the sensor or antenna will not detect it and the alarm will not go off.\n\nOn the other hand, the alarm may sometimes go off when someone shoplifts. This could happen if the person tries to remove the tag or label from an item before bringing it into the store, or if they try to block the sensor or antenna from detecting the item. In these cases, the sensor or antenna will not receive the expected response from the tag or label, and the alarm will go off to alert store staff.
