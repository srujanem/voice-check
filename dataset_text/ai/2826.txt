Great question!, Ice does float in water because it is less dense than water. Density is a measure of how much matter is packed into a certain amount of space. A substance that is more dense has more matter packed into a given space, while a substance that is less dense has less matter packed into the same space. Water is a very strange substance when it comes to density. When it is a liquid, it is denser than ice. But when water turns into a solid (like when it freezes into ice), it actually expands and becomes less dense. This is because the molecules in a solid are usually arranged in a more orderly way than they are in a liquid, which means that they take up more space. So when water freezes into ice, it becomes less dense and will float on top of the liquid water. That's why ice floats in water, even though most solids are denser than the liquids they are placed in.
