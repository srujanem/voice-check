Sure! When you "short" a stock, it means that you are betting that the price of the stock will go down. Here's how it works: Let's say you think that the price of Company X's stock is going to fall. You borrow 100 shares of Company X's stock from someone who owns it and agrees to lend it to you. You sell those 100 shares of Company X's stock for $50 each, so you get $5,000. A few weeks later, the price of Company X's stock falls to $40 per share. You buy 100 shares of Company X's stock for $40 each, which costs you $4,000. You return the 100 shares of Company X's stock to the person who lent them to you. You have made a profit of $1,000 because you sold the stock for $5,000 and then bought it back for $4,000. So, when you short a stock, you make money if the price goes down. If the price goes up, you lose money.
