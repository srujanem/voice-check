Heat is the movement of particles. In a star, the particles are atoms. When you squeeze something, you are putting pressure on it. This pressure can cause the atoms to move around more and bump into each other more often. When atoms bump into each other, they can transfer energy to each other. This transfer of energy can make the atoms move around even more, which we experience as heat.\n\nIn a star, the pressure is so strong that it can cause the atoms to move around so much that they start to fuse together. This process is called nuclear fusion, and it releases a huge amount of energy in the form of heat and light. The heat and light produced by nuclear fusion is what makes a star shine.\n\nSo, to summarize, when you squeeze something, the particles inside it start to move around more and bump into each other. This movement and bumping can create heat. In a star, the pressure is so strong that it can cause atoms to fuse together, which releases a lot of heat and light.
