Calories in versus calories out, also known as the energy balance equation, is a way to understand how the body uses and stores energy from the food we eat. When we eat more calories than our body needs for energy, the excess calories can be stored as fat. When we eat fewer calories than our body needs, the body can use stored fat for energy. 
Insulin resistance can cause weight gain because it affects how the body uses and stores energy. When the body becomes insulin resistant, it doesn't respond as well to the hormone insulin, which helps regulate blood sugar levels. As a result, the body may produce more insulin to try to lower blood sugar levels. High levels of insulin can cause the body to store more fat, especially in the abdominal area. Insulin resistance can also lead to increased appetite and cravings for high-calorie, sugary foods, which can contribute to weight gain. 
So, while the law of thermodynamics still applies, insulin resistance can make it more difficult for the body to use and store energy in a healthy way, which can lead to weight gain.
