Sure! Imagine that you have a lemonade stand where you sell lemonade to people passing by. Let's say that you charge $1 for a cup of lemonade. Now, let's say that the government is like a big bank that keeps track of how much money everyone has. If you sell a cup of lemonade, you will earn $1, and the government will record that you have $1 more in your "bank account" (which is just a way of keeping track of how much money you have). Now, let's say that lots of people want to buy lemonade from your stand, so you start to sell a lot of cups. This means that you will be earning a lot of money and the government will have to record that you have a lot more money in your bank account. However, if the government doesn't have enough money to give you when you sell your lemonade, it will have to print more money. This is like the government making more "cups" of lemonade to give to you in exchange for the lemonade you are selling. So, the government has to print more money when there is a lot of buying and selling happening, because it needs to have enough money to give to people in exchange for the things they are selling.
