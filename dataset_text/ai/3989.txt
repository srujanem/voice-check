Sometimes, even very big and successful companies can fail because of things like mismanagement, unexpected changes in the market, or natural disasters. When a company can't pay its bills and debts, it can ask a court to protect it from its creditors while it tries to fix its problems or sell its assets. This is called "filing for bankruptcy." The government might decide to help a company that has filed for bankruptcy if it is important for the country's economy. For example, if a lot of people work for the company or if it provides a service that is important for the country, the government might give the company some money to help it get back on its feet. This is called a "bailout." The government might also bail out a company if it thinks that letting the company fail could cause bigger problems, like a lot of other companies going bankrupt too. This is because when one company has trouble, it can affect other companies that do business with it. Overall, the government might decide to bail out a company to try to prevent bigger problems and to protect the jobs of the people who work there.
