A TV's refresh rate refers to how many times per second the image on the screen is refreshed. A higher refresh rate can result in a smoother and more fluid image, especially when it comes to fast-moving content. However, the refresh rate of a TV is not the same as the frame rate at which content is shot or transmitted. The frame rate of a TV show or movie refers to how many individual images (frames) are shown per second. Most movies are shot at a frame rate of 24 frames per second (fps), while TV shows are usually shot at a higher frame rate, such as 30 or 60 fps. A TV's refresh rate is separate from the frame rate of the content it is displaying. For example, even if a TV has a refresh rate of 600hz, it will still only be able to display content at the frame rate at which it was shot or transmitted. If you are watching a movie that was shot at 24 fps, the TV will only be able to display the content at a maximum of 24 fps, regardless of its refresh rate. In general, a higher refresh rate can result in a better viewing experience, especially when it comes to fast-moving content or sports. However, it is not necessary to have a TV with a very high refresh rate if you are mainly watching content that was shot at a lower frame rate, such as most movies.
