There are a few reasons why medical care is generally more expensive in the US than dental care. One reason is that medical care is typically more complex and requires more specialized training and equipment. For example, a surgeon needs to have a lot of education and experience, and they also need to use expensive equipment like MRI machines and operating theaters. This can drive up the cost of medical procedures. 
Another reason is that there is less competition in the medical field compared to the dental field. There are fewer hospitals and medical centers, and they often have more bargaining power when it comes to setting prices. In contrast, there are many more dentists and dental practices, which can create more competition and drive down prices. 
Finally, there are also different insurance policies and payment systems for medical care and dental care. Medical insurance is often more expensive and covers a wider range of procedures, while dental insurance is usually cheaper and covers a more limited range of procedures. This can also contribute to the difference in prices between medical care and dental care.
