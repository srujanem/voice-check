Things catch fire when they reach their ignition temperature, which is the temperature at which they will start to burn. Some materials have a lower ignition temperature and will catch fire more easily than others. When a material is heated, the heat energy can cause the molecules in the material to vibrate and move around faster. If the heat is high enough, it can cause the molecules to break apart and form new chemical bonds, a process called pyrolysis. As the material breaks down, it can release gases, which can then mix with oxygen in the air and ignite, causing a fire.\n\nSo to answer your question, things can catch fire without a flame, but a flame is often needed to provide the heat energy that causes the material to ignite.
