There are a few reasons why there isn't a secondary market for airline tickets like there is for other types of events and products. 
One reason is that airline tickets are considered to be revocable licenses rather than traditional property that can be bought and sold freely. This means that the airline retains the right to cancel or modify the terms of the ticket at any time, even after it has been sold to someone else. This can make it difficult to transfer tickets to other people or resell them on a secondary market. 
Another reason is that airlines have their own policies and procedures for changing or canceling flights, and these policies may not be compatible with a secondary market. For example, if a person buys a ticket on a secondary market and then needs to change their travel plans, they may not be able to do so through the original airline or may have to pay additional fees to make the changes. 
Finally, there is a lot of government regulation of the airline industry, and some of these regulations may impact the ability to create a secondary market for tickets. For example, there are rules governing how airlines can sell tickets and how they must handle customer complaints, and these rules may make it difficult to create a separate market for reselling tickets. 
Overall, while it might be convenient to have a secondary market for airline tickets, the unique nature of air travel and the various regulatory and logistical issues involved make it challenging to create one.
