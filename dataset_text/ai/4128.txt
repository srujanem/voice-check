Computable numbers are numbers that can be calculated by a computer or other mechanical process. Not every number is a computable number.\n\nImagine that you have a calculator. This calculator can perform basic arithmetic operations like addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division. It can also handle more complex operations like finding the square root of a number or calculating the sine of an angle.\n\nThe calculator can only work with numbers that it is programmed to understand. For example, it may not be able to handle numbers with an infinite number of decimal places, or numbers that are too large or too small to fit in its memory. These numbers are not computable by the calculator.\n\nOn the other hand, there are many numbers that the calculator can work with. For example, it can handle whole numbers like 1, 2, 3, and 4. It can also handle decimal numbers like 3.14 or 0.01. These numbers are computable by the calculator.\n\nSo, to sum up, a computable number is a number that can be calculated by a computer or other mechanical process. Not every number is a computable number, but there are many numbers that are computable.
