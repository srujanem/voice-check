Dark matter is a type of matter that scientists believe exists because of the way that galaxies and other objects in the universe behave. It is called "dark" because it does not emit, absorb, or reflect light, so it is invisible to telescopes and other instruments that scientists use to study the universe. However, scientists believe that dark matter exists because it has a gravitational effect on the things that we can see, like stars and galaxies. In other words, even though we can't see dark matter, we can see how it affects the things around it by the way those things move. One way to think about it is to imagine that you are standing on a trampoline, and you have a bunch of heavy objects that you are trying to balance. If you put all of the objects in one spot, the trampoline will stretch and sag down in that spot. But if you spread the objects out, the trampoline will be more evenly balanced and won't sag as much. Dark matter is like the objects on the trampoline - it has mass and therefore has a gravitational effect on the things around it. So even though we can't see dark matter, we know it must be there because of the way that other things in the universe behave. Scientists are still working to understand more about dark matter and how it fits into our understanding of the universe.
