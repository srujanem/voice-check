Derivatives are financial instruments that derive their value from an underlying asset. This means that the value of a derivative is based on the value of something else, like a stock, bond, or commodity. There are many different types of derivatives, including futures, options, and swaps. Here are some examples of how they can be used: Futures are contracts that allow you to buy or sell an asset at a certain price at a specific time in the future. For example, you might enter into a contract to buy a certain amount of wheat at a certain price in three months. Options are contracts that give you the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell an asset at a certain price at a specific time in the future. For example, you might buy a stock option that gives you the right to buy a certain number of shares at a certain price within the next year. Swaps are contracts that allow two parties to exchange financial instruments, such as loans or bonds, with each other. For example, one party might agree to pay a fixed interest rate on a loan, while the other party agrees to pay a variable interest rate. Derivatives are used in a variety of ways, such as to hedge against risks, speculate on the future price of an asset, or to get exposure to an asset without actually owning it. They can be complex financial instruments, and it's important to understand the risks involved before using them.
