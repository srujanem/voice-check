AC stands for "alternating current," which means that the voltage or current in a circuit is constantly changing direction. The voltage in an AC circuit is often represented using RMS, or root mean square, because it provides a way to compare the average magnitude of the AC voltage to a DC voltage of the same value. Imagine that you have a toy that runs on batteries. If you were to measure the voltage of the batteries using a voltmeter, you would get a reading of the DC voltage. However, if you were to measure the voltage of the toy while it is running on AC power (like from a wall outlet), the voltage would be constantly changing. It might be positive for a while, then negative, then positive again. To compare the AC voltage to a DC voltage, we use RMS. RMS is a way to measure the average magnitude of an AC voltage over time. It's called the "root mean square" because it's calculated by taking the square root of the mean (average) of the squares of the values of the AC voltage over a specific period of time. For example, if you were to measure the AC voltage of your toy over a period of one second, you might get a series of values like this: 1 volt, 0 volts, -2 volts, 3 volts, -1 volt, The RMS value of this AC voltage would be calculated by taking the square of each value, finding the average of those squared values, and then taking the square root of that average. In this case, the RMS value would be about 1.8 volts. This allows us to compare the AC voltage to a DC voltage of the same value. For example, if the RMS value of an AC voltage is 120 volts, it has the same average magnitude as a DC voltage of 120 volts.
