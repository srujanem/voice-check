A PAC, or Political Action Committee, is an organization that raises and spends money to elect or defeat candidates for public office. PACs can be affiliated with a particular political party, a specific interest group, or a corporation. Stephen Colbert, a television host and comedian, created a super PAC as part of a satirical commentary on the role of money in politics. A super PAC is a type of PAC that is allowed to raise and spend unlimited amounts of money from any source, including individuals, corporations, and unions. Super PACs are required to disclose their donors, but they are not subject to the same limits on campaign contributions as traditional PACs. Colbert's super PAC was a way for him to explore and mock the influence of money in politics through humorous skits and interviews on his TV show. The super PAC was not actually used to support or oppose any specific candidates, but rather to make a point about the potential for wealthy individuals and groups to wield disproportionate power in the political process.
