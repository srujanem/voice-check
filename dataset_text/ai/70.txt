Glaciers are massive bodies of dense ice that form over centuries as fallen snow compresses without melting. Moving slowly under their own immense weight, glaciers carve deep U-shaped valleys, transport massive boulders across landscapes, and shape coastal fjords. Today, these ice sheets hold roughly 69 percent of the planet's fresh water supply. Monitoring glacial retreat is essential for climate scientists, as melting glaciers directly contribute to global sea-level rise while altering critical freshwater resources for millions of people downstream.
