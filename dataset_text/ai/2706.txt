A skin graft is a surgical procedure in which skin from one area of the body is transplanted to another area. If someone gets a skin graft from a person of a different skin color, the skin graft will retain the color of the donor's skin. For example, if a white person gets a skin graft from a black person, the skin graft will be black. It's important to understand that the skin is made up of cells called melanocytes, which produce a pigment called melanin. Melanin is what gives our skin its color. People with darker skin have more melanin in their skin cells, while people with lighter skin have less melanin. When a skin graft is done, the skin cells from the donor (the person who is giving the skin) are transplanted to the recipient (the person who is receiving the skin). The skin cells from the donor will continue to produce the same amount of melanin as they did before, so the skin graft will have the same color as the donor's skin.
