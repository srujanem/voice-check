Before computers were used for editing movies, the process was done entirely by hand using physical film reels. This process was called "film cutting" or "film editing." Here's how it worked: First, the film would be shot using a movie camera, which would record the images and sound on physical film reels. Then, these film reels would be sent to a laboratory, where they would be developed and printed onto new film reels. Next, the editor would take these printed film reels and physically cut them into pieces using a pair of scissors. The editor would then rearrange the pieces in the order that they wanted the movie to be shown, and use tape to join them back together. Finally, the edited film reels would be sent back to the laboratory, where they would be printed onto a new set of film reels that could be shown in theaters. Overall, the process of editing a movie before computers was very time-consuming and required a lot of physical labor. It was not until the development of digital editing technology that the process became much faster and easier.
