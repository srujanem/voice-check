Methanol and ethanol are both types of alcohol that can be made from different raw materials. Methanol is made from wood or other plant material, while ethanol is usually made from corn, wheat, or other grains.\n\nTo make methanol, the raw material is first broken down into smaller pieces and then heated with steam. This process, called "gasification," produces a mixture of gases, including hydrogen and carbon monoxide. These gases are then combined in a chemical reaction called "synthesis" to form methanol.\n\nTo make ethanol, the raw material is first ground into a fine powder and mixed with water to form a "mash." The mash is then fermented with yeast, which eats the sugars in the mash and produces ethanol as a byproduct. The ethanol is then separated from the water and other impurities through a process called "distillation."\n\nAlthough methanol and ethanol are both types of alcohol, they are not interchangeable. Methanol is toxic to humans and should not be consumed, while ethanol is the type of alcohol that is found in alcoholic beverages and is safe for human consumption in moderate amounts.
