Plea bargains are a way for people who are accused of a crime to avoid going to trial. Trials can be expensive and time-consuming for everyone involved, including the court system, the lawyers, and the defendant. Plea bargains allow people to avoid a trial by agreeing to plead guilty to a crime in exchange for a lighter sentence, such as less time in prison. The main benefit of a plea bargain for the defendant is that it allows them to avoid the risk of being found guilty at trial and receiving a longer sentence. It also allows the defendant to have some control over the outcome of their case and to resolve the matter more quickly. For the prosecutor, a plea bargain can save time and resources that would have been spent on a trial. It can also help them to secure a conviction in a case that might have been difficult to prove in court. Overall, plea bargains can be a useful tool for resolving criminal cases in a way that is fair to both the defendant and the prosecution.
