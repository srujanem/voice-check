Vending machines use sensors to detect the size, shape, and other physical characteristics of the bills and coins that are inserted. These sensors send information to the vending machine's computer, which uses this information to identify the type of bill or coin that has been inserted. For example, the computer may use the size of the bill to determine whether it is a $1 bill or a $5 bill. It may also use the shape of the coin to determine whether it is a penny, a nickel, or another type of coin. In addition to these sensors, vending machines may also use other techniques to identify the type of bill or coin that has been inserted. For example, they may use special markings or features that are unique to each type of currency, or they may use technology like magnetic strips or microchips to help identify the type of bill or coin. Overall, vending machines are able to differentiate between different types of bills and coins by using a combination of sensors, markings, and other techniques to accurately identify the type of currency that has been inserted.
