Sure! A light bulb works by using electricity to heat a small metal wire called a filament until it becomes white hot and glows. This happens inside a glass bulb that is filled with a gas called argon. The gas helps to keep the filament from burning up too quickly. Inside the bulb, there is also a metal base with two wires coming out of it. One wire is connected to the filament, and the other wire is connected to the metal base. When you turn on the switch, electricity flows from the power source (like a battery or the outlet in your wall) through one of the wires and into the bulb. It passes through the filament, which heats up and starts to glow. The electricity then flows back out of the bulb through the other wire and into the power source again. The glowing filament produces light because it is hot enough to give off energy in the form of visible light. This is called incandescence.
