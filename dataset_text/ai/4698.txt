Scientists can determine what kind of diet a dinosaur had by looking at its teeth and jaw structure. Herbivorous dinosaurs, or those that ate plants, had teeth that were flat and good for grinding and crushing plant matter. Carnivorous dinosaurs, or those that ate meat, had sharp, pointed teeth that were good for tearing and biting into their prey. Omnivorous dinosaurs, or those that ate both plants and animals, had a combination of both types of teeth.\n\nIn addition to teeth, scientists can also look at the shape of the jaw and skull to help determine a dinosaur's diet. For example, herbivorous dinosaurs often had broad, powerful jaws with lots of space for grinding plants, while carnivorous dinosaurs had long, slender jaws with room for lots of sharp teeth.\n\nFinally, scientists can also look at the fossils of the plants that were found in the same rock formations as the dinosaur bones to help determine what the dinosaur might have eaten. For example, if a dinosaur was found with a lot of fossilized ferns and other types of plants, it is likely that the dinosaur was herbivorous.
