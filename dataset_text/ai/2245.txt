Muskets had some advantages over crossbows that made them a popular choice for armies. One of the main advantages of muskets was that they could be fired more quickly than crossbows. Crossbows had to be cocked (loaded) manually by pulling back a heavy string, which took time and effort. In contrast, muskets could be loaded and fired relatively quickly using a simple matchlock mechanism. This made muskets well-suited for massed firepower, where soldiers could fire multiple rounds in quick succession. Another advantage of muskets was that they had longer ranges than crossbows. Crossbows could only shoot accurately up to about 100-150 yards, while muskets could shoot accurately up to around 300 yards or more. This made muskets more useful for battlefields where soldiers needed to shoot at targets that were farther away. Muskets were also relatively cheap and easy to produce, especially compared to earlier firearms such as arquebuses. They were made of simple, readily available materials such as wood, iron, and brass, and they could be mass-produced using simple manufacturing techniques. This made them a cost-effective choice for armies that needed to equip large numbers of soldiers. Finally, muskets were relatively simple to use and required minimal training, which made them a good choice for conscripted or untrained troops. Crossbows, on the other hand, required more skill and training to use effectively, which made them less suitable for armies that needed to quickly train and equip large numbers of soldiers. Overall, the combination of speed, range, cost, and ease of use made muskets an attractive choice for armies, which is why they became popular and eventually replaced crossbows as the dominant infantry weapon.
