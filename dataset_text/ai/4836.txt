When people use American Sign Language (ASL), they are using their hands and body to communicate. They are not speaking out loud, but they might also mouth words or make facial expressions to help convey meaning.\n\nThe words or phrases that people mouth while signing are called "speech reading" or "lipreading." They are not part of ASL, but they can help the person who is signing understand what is being said and help the person who is listening understand what is being signed.\n\nASL is a complete and complex language, with its own grammar and vocabulary. It is used by many deaf and hard of hearing people in the United States and Canada as their primary means of communication. Just like with any language, people use ASL to have conversations, share ideas, and express themselves.\n\nASL is not just a way to communicate, but it is also a way of life and a rich culture.
