The Masons, also known as Freemasons, are a fraternity (a social organization for men) that traces its origins to the local fraternities of stonemasons in the Middle Ages. Freemasons use ritual and symbolism inspired by stonemasonry to teach moral lessons and self-improvement. They also engage in charitable work and promote a sense of community among their members. Freemasonry is a fraternity with a set of moral and ethical values, not a religion. Freemasons believe in a supreme being, but they do not promote any particular faith or religious doctrine. Members come from many different religions and belief systems. The Masons have a hierarchical structure, with different levels of membership and titles for its members. The rituals and symbols used by the Masons are meant to teach moral lessons and promote self-improvement. Some people believe that the Masons have secret or mysterious practices, but the Masons say that their rituals and symbols are meant to be symbolic and allegorical, not secret.
