In a criminal court, the standard of proof is "beyond a reasonable doubt." This means that the evidence against the defendant must be so strong that there is no reasonable doubt in the mind of a reasonable person that the defendant is guilty.\n\nIn a civil court, the standard of proof is "preponderance of the evidence." This means that it is more likely than not that the defendant is liable. This standard is lower than the "beyond a reasonable doubt" standard because the consequences of a civil case are generally not as severe as those in a criminal case.\n\nFor example, if someone is accused of stealing something in a criminal case, the prosecution must prove beyond a reasonable doubt that the person is guilty of stealing. If the person is found guilty, they may go to jail.\n\nIn a civil case, the same person may be sued for the value of the stolen item. In this case, the plaintiff (the person bringing the lawsuit) must prove that it is more likely than not that the person stole the item. If the person is found liable, they may have to pay money to the plaintiff, but they will not go to jail.\n\nSo, the lower standard of proof in civil cases is used because the consequences are not as severe as in criminal cases.
