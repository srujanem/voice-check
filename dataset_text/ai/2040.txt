The rods and cones in your eye are light-sensitive cells that help you see. They are located in a layer at the back of your eye called the retina. When light enters your eye, it passes through the cornea (the clear front part of your eye) and the pupil (the black circle in the center of your eye). The light then hits the retina, which sends a signal to your brain through the optic nerve. Your brain processes the signal and interprets it as an image. The rods and cones in your eye are responsible for detecting different wavelengths of light and sending this information to your brain. The rods are more sensitive to low levels of light and are responsible for helping you see in low light conditions, while the cones are responsible for helping you see color and fine detail.
