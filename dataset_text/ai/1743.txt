Gravity is the fundamental force of attraction between objects with mass. Described by Einstein's general theory of relativity as the curvature of spacetime, it governs the motion of planets, stars, and galaxies. On Earth, gravity gives objects weight and causes them to fall at approximately 9.8 meters per second squared. The escape velocity — the speed needed to break free from Earth's gravitational field — is approximately 11.2 kilometers per second. In conclusion, a deeper understanding of this subject is essential for informed decision-making. Recent studies have further confirmed the significance of these factors in relation to gravity.
