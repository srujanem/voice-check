Polygraph tests, also known as lie detector tests, are designed to measure physiological responses to questions. The theory is that when a person is lying, their body will respond in certain ways, such as by increasing their heart rate or sweating. However, polygraph tests are not considered to be a reliable method for detecting lies. There are many factors that can affect a person's physiological responses, including stress, anxiety, and even medical conditions. Additionally, people can sometimes learn how to control their physiological responses in order to mislead the polygraph. Because of these limitations, polygraph tests are not generally used as evidence in criminal trials. Instead, law enforcement and prosecutors rely on other forms of evidence, such as eyewitness testimony, physical evidence, and confessions, to build a case against a suspect.
