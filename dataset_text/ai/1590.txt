This is a multifaceted topic that requires careful examination from multiple perspectives. Biodiversity refers to the variety of life on Earth at all levels, from genes to ecosystems. It encompasses the estimated 8.7 million species of plants, animals, fungi, and microorganisms, along with the ecosystems they form. High biodiversity ensures ecosystem resilience, provides resources for medicine and agriculture, and maintains natural processes like pollination and water purification. Human activities — including habitat destruction, pollution, invasive species, and climate change — are driving the sixth mass extinction, with species disappearing at rates far exceeding natural background levels. Understanding these principles is foundational to making progress in this important area. Recent studies have further confirmed the significance of these factors in relation to biodiversity.
