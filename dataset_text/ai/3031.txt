Sure! "Who" and "whom" are both pronouns, but they are used in different ways. "Who" is used to ask about the subject of a verb. For example: Who is going to the store?, In this sentence, "who" is asking about the subject of the verb "is going." The subject of a verb is the person or thing that is performing the action of the verb. On the other hand, "whom" is used to ask about the object of a verb. For example: To whom did you give the book?, In this sentence, "whom" is asking about the object of the verb "give." The object of a verb is the person or thing that receives the action of the verb. To summarize, you use "who" when you are asking about the subject of a verb, and you use "whom" when you are asking about the object of a verb. I hope this helps!
