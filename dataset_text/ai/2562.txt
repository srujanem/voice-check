A straw man argument is a type of argument that involves misrepresenting or exaggerating someone else's position in order to make it easier to attack or argue against. It is called a "straw man" because the arguments being made are not the real arguments that the other person is making, but rather a weaker or distorted version of them. Here's an example of how a straw man argument might work: Person A: "I think we should raise the minimum wage to $15 an hour." Person B: "But if we do that, all businesses will go bankrupt and everyone will lose their jobs!", Person B's argument is a straw man, because Person A never said that raising the minimum wage would cause all businesses to go bankrupt or everyone to lose their jobs. Person B is exaggerating or distorting Person A's position in order to make it easier to attack. Straw man arguments are often used as a way to avoid engaging with the real arguments being made, or to distract from the issues at hand. It's important to be aware of straw man arguments and to try to avoid using them in your own arguments.
