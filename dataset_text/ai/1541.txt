It is possible for people who have been vaccinated against a disease to still get that disease, although it is usually less severe and less contagious if they do. This is because vaccines are not 100% effective at preventing disease. However, they are still very effective at protecting against illness and helping to prevent the spread of disease. 
For example, the flu vaccine is usually about 50-60% effective at preventing the flu, but it can still reduce the severity and duration of the illness if you do get sick. This means that even if you have been vaccinated, you can still get the flu, but it is less likely and the symptoms will be less severe. 
It is also important to note that it can take some time for the vaccine to fully take effect and provide protection. This means that if you are exposed to the disease shortly after being vaccinated, you may still be at risk of getting sick. 
Overall, vaccines are an important tool for protecting ourselves and others against disease and it is important to get vaccinated to help reduce the spread of illness.
