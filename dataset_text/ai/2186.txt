Google's search algorithm is a set of rules that helps the search engine understand how relevant and useful a webpage is when it comes to a specific search query. There are many factors that go into Google's search algorithm, but here are a few important ones: Keywords: When you type a search query into Google, the search engine looks for webpages that contain the same words or phrases that you typed in. This helps Google understand what the webpage is about and whether it's relevant to your search. Links: Google also looks at the links that point to a webpage. If a webpage has a lot of links from other websites, it can be a sign that the webpage is popular and useful. Quality: Google wants to show you the best and most accurate results for your search, so it looks for webpages that are well-written and reliable. If a webpage has a lot of spelling mistakes or seems spammy, Google is less likely to show it in the search results. User experience: Google also looks at how easy it is to use a webpage. If a webpage is hard to navigate or takes a long time to load, Google is less likely to show it in the search results. Overall, Google's search algorithm is constantly evolving and becoming more sophisticated, which helps it deliver the most relevant and useful results to users.
