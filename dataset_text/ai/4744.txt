Osmosis is a natural process that occurs when a solvent (like water) moves through a membrane (like a thin sheet of plastic or a filter) from an area of low concentration to an area of high concentration. The goal of both forward and reverse osmosis is to purify water by removing impurities and contaminants, but they work in slightly different ways.\n\nForward osmosis is a process in which water moves through a membrane from a less concentrated solution to a more concentrated solution, in order to equalize the concentration on both sides of the membrane. This is the same process that occurs naturally in osmosis.\n\nReverse osmosis, on the other hand, is a process in which water is forced through a membrane in the opposite direction of the natural osmosis process. This is done by applying pressure to the water on the side of the membrane with the more concentrated solution, which forces the water through the membrane and into the less concentrated side. This process is used to purify water by removing contaminants and impurities that are too large to pass through the membrane.\n\nTo explain it in simpler terms: In forward osmosis, water moves from a place with less salt to a place with more salt, until the saltiness is the same on both sides. In reverse osmosis, water is pushed through a very fine filter, and the things that can't go through the filter stay on one side, while the water goes to the other side.
