The No Child Left Behind Act (NCLB) was a law in the United States that aimed to improve the quality of education and increase the accountability of schools. It was enacted in 2001 and required states to administer standardized tests to students in certain grades in order to receive funding from the federal government. Many people hated NCLB because they believed that it placed too much emphasis on standardized testing and that it did not adequately address the needs of all students, particularly those who were struggling. Some people also argued that NCLB led to teachers "teaching to the test," which meant that they focused on helping students perform well on the tests rather than providing a well-rounded education. Overall, the NCLB Act was controversial and had both supporters and opponents. It was replaced by the Every Student Succeeds Act (ESSA) in 2015.
