When an airplane is flying, it needs lift to stay in the air. This lift is usually provided by the shape of the wings, which are designed to produce lift as the air flows over them. However, an airplane can also use its engines to produce lift. This is called vectored thrust.\n\nVectored thrust is when the airplane's engines are pointed in a different direction than normal. For example, instead of pointing straight forward, the engines might be pointed slightly downward. When the engines produce thrust in this downward direction, it can help to lift the airplane up into the air.\n\nThis is similar to how a helicopter can hover in one spot by pointing its rotors downward. The downward force of the air being pushed by the rotors helps to lift the helicopter up off the ground.\n\nSo, even though the wings of the airplane are not producing lift in the traditional way, the engine's vectored thrust can still help keep the airplane in the air.
