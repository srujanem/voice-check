Computers are very good at following precise instructions, but they have a hard time understanding things that are written in a way that is similar to how humans talk. That's why we use programming languages like Java or C++ to give computers instructions. These programming languages are very precise and specific, which makes it easy for computers to understand what we want them to do. Think of it like this: if you want to make a sandwich, you could give someone a list of instructions written in English, like "take two slices of bread, put some peanut butter on one slice and some jelly on the other, then put them together and cut the sandwich in half." This would probably be easy for you to understand, but it might be confusing for someone else. On the other hand, if you gave someone a set of precise instructions using a programming language, like "take bread slice 1 and bread slice 2, spread 2 tablespoons of peanut butter on bread slice 1 and 2 tablespoons of jelly on bread slice 2, then combine bread slice 1 and bread slice 2 and cut in half," it would be much easier for them to follow the instructions exactly. So, in short, computers are very good at following precise instructions, but they have a hard time understanding instructions that are written in a way that is similar to how humans talk. That's why we use programming languages to give them instructions.
