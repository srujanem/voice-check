Parachutes are not typically included on commercial airplanes because they are not necessary for the safe operation of the aircraft. Commercial airplanes are designed to fly at high altitudes and are equipped with multiple engines, redundant systems, and other safety features to ensure that they can safely reach their destination. In the unlikely event that an emergency does occur, the pilots and crew are trained to handle the situation and guide the plane to a safe landing. Additionally, the use of parachutes would not be practical in most emergency situations on a commercial airplane. For example, if the plane were to experience engine failure at a high altitude, it would not be safe for passengers to jump out with parachutes because the altitude would be too high for a parachute to be effective. The plane would need to descend to a lower altitude before it would be safe for passengers to use parachutes to evacuate the aircraft. In short, commercial airplanes are designed to be safe and reliable, and the use of parachutes is not necessary for the safe operation of the aircraft.
