Pigs that are domesticated, or raised by humans, are usually kept in a controlled environment where they are provided with food, shelter, and protection. When these domesticated pigs are introduced into the wild, they may go through a transformation because they have to adapt to their new surroundings and find their own food and shelter. 
One way that domesticated pigs may transform into wild boars when they are introduced into the wild is by developing behaviors and physical characteristics that help them survive in their new environment. For example, they may become more aggressive and territorial in order to protect themselves and their resources from other animals. They may also grow thicker hair and tusks, which can help them defend themselves and find food. 
It's important to note that not all domesticated pigs will necessarily transform into wild boars when they are introduced into the wild. The extent to which they change will depend on their individual genetics and the specific conditions they face in the wild.
