In the United States, it is common for stores to require customers to show identification (ID) when purchasing certain over-the-counter (OTC) medications, such as Nyquil and acetaminophen, if they appear to be under a certain age. This is because some OTC medications contain ingredients that can be harmful if taken in large amounts or if taken by certain age groups. For example, Nyquil contains a sedating ingredient called diphenhydramine that can cause drowsiness and should not be taken by people under the age of 12. Acetaminophen is generally safe to take, but taking too much can cause liver damage, so it is important to follow the dosing instructions on the label. 
If you are under the age of 18 and in need of medication, you should talk to your parent or guardian about getting the appropriate treatment. If you are unable to do so, you can consider seeking medical attention from a healthcare professional, such as a pharmacist or a doctor. They may be able to provide you with the medication you need or suggest alternative treatment options. 
In summary, it is important to follow the age recommendations for OTC medications to ensure your safety and to avoid potential harm. If you are under the age of 18 and need medication, you should talk to a trusted adult or healthcare professional for help.
