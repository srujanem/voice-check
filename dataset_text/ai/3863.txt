Bond yields are a measure of the interest rate that investors can expect to receive when they buy a bond. When the interest rate on a bond is high, the yield is also high. When the interest rate on a bond is low, the yield is also low. The yield on a government bond is important because it can tell us something about the health of a country's economy. When a country's economy is doing well, people and businesses are more likely to borrow money, which means there is more demand for bonds. As a result, the interest rate on the bond will be lower, and the yield will be lower as well. On the other hand, if a country's economy is not doing well, there may be less demand for bonds, which means the interest rate will be higher and the yield will be higher as well. The yield on a 10-year government bond is particularly important because it can give us a long-term view of the health of a country's economy. By looking at the yield on a 10-year bond, we can see how investors expect the country's economy to perform over the next decade. The Wall Street Journal (WSJ) includes listings of countries with 10-year bond yields on its homepage because this information is important to investors and analysts who are interested in the health of different countries' economies. By looking at the yields on different countries' 10-year bonds, they can see how the economies of those countries are expected to perform and how they compare to one another.
