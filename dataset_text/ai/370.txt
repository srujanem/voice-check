Dry firing a bow is dangerous because it involves releasing the bowstring without an arrow nocked (attached) to it. When you dry fire a bow, the bowstring slams into the end of the bow without the cushioning effect of the arrow. This can cause the bow to break or the string to snap. In addition, the energy from the bowstring can damage the bow's limb pockets, which hold the limbs in place. This can cause the limbs to become loose or even fall off, making the bow unsafe to use. So it's important to always make sure that there is an arrow properly nocked on the string before you shoot a bow.
