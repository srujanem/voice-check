There are a few reasons why animals are smaller today than they were millions of years ago. One reason is that the Earth's climate and environment have changed over time. For example, during the time of the dinosaurs, the Earth was much warmer than it is today, and there was more vegetation available for animals to eat. As a result, animals were able to grow larger because they had more food to eat. 
Another reason is that larger animals often have a harder time finding enough food to sustain their size. This is because they need more food to fuel their bodies and maintain their larger size. In addition, larger animals may also have a harder time finding mates and reproducing, which can impact their ability to survive and thrive. 
On the other hand, smaller animals often have an advantage because they are more agile and can move more quickly to find food and avoid predators. They also require less food to survive, which can be helpful in times when food is scarce. 
Overall, the size of animals is influenced by a variety of factors, including their environment, the availability of food, and their ability to reproduce and survive.
