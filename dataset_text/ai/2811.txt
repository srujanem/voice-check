Consecutive life sentences are issued when a person is convicted of multiple crimes that are punishable by life in prison. This means that the person will have to serve one life sentence after another, rather than serving them all at the same time. One life sentence may not be enough in some cases because the crimes that the person has committed are so serious or numerous that a single life sentence does not seem like an appropriate punishment. For example, if a person has committed multiple murders or other very serious crimes, a single life sentence may not be enough to adequately punish them and protect society from them. In these cases, a judge may decide to issue multiple consecutive life sentences, meaning that the person will have to serve one life sentence after another, rather than all at the same time. This can ensure that the person is punished appropriately and that they remain in prison for a very long time.
