It is generally not appropriate for the IT department to monitor the specific websites that their coworkers are visiting during work hours unless there is a specific business need for doing so. This is because people have a right to privacy, and monitoring their online activity can be invasive and may infringe upon that right. 
There are various laws and regulations that protect the privacy of individuals in the workplace, including the Electronic Communications Privacy Act (ECPA) in the United States, which prohibits unauthorized access to electronic communications, such as email and internet activity. 
However, it is important to note that the IT department may be responsible for ensuring the security and proper functioning of the company's computer systems, which may require them to monitor certain aspects of network activity. For example, they may need to track and block malicious software or identify and resolve network issues. In these cases, the IT department may have access to information about the websites that are being visited on the company's network, but their primary focus should be on maintaining the security and functionality of the systems, rather than monitoring the specific online activities of their coworkers. 
In general, it is a good idea to be mindful of your online activity while at work and to follow your company's policies and guidelines for internet usage. If you have concerns about your privacy at work, you may want to speak with your supervisor or HR representative to get more information about your company's policies and practices.
