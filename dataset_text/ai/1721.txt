Equity refers to the ownership interest in a company. If you own equity in a company, it means you own a small part of that company. For example, if you buy shares of stock in a company, you become a shareholder and you own equity in that company. Securities are financial instruments that represent ownership in a company or a debt owed by a company or government. There are different types of securities, such as stocks, bonds, and derivatives. For example, a stock represents ownership in a company. When you buy a stock, you become a shareholder, which means you own a small part of that company. If the company does well, the value of the stock may go up and you can sell it for more money than you paid for it. Bonds are a type of security that represents a debt that a company or government owes to the bondholder. When you buy a bond, you are lending money to the bond issuer. The bond issuer agrees to pay you back the money you lent, plus interest, at a future date. Derivatives are securities that derive their value from an underlying asset.
