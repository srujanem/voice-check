When people talk about the architecture of a console, they are referring to the way that the hardware of the console is designed and organized. In other words, it's the structure of the console's components and how they work together to run games and other software. Imagine that a console is like a house, and the different components of the console are like the different rooms in the house. Just like how a house can be designed in different ways (for example, a one-story ranch versus a two-story colonial), a console can also be designed in different ways. The architecture of a console can affect how fast it can run games and other software, and how much it can do at once. For example, one console might have a more powerful processor (like the "brain" of the console) or more memory (like a "closet" where the console stores information), which could make it better at running complex or demanding games. So when people say that two consoles have different architectures, they are saying that they are designed and built in different ways, and this can affect how they perform.
