Pricing items with a number like "15.99" instead of "16" is a marketing tactic called "charm pricing." It's a way for businesses to make their products seem more affordable or cheaper to customers. The idea is that the price appears to be just under a round number, like $20 or $100, which can make it feel like a better deal. For example, if an item is priced at $15.99, it might seem like a better deal than if it were priced at $16, even though the difference is only a few cents. Another reason businesses might use charm pricing is because people tend to perceive numbers that end in "9" as being more affordable than numbers that end in "0." So, a price like $15.99 might seem more appealing to people than a price like $16.00, even though they are the same amount. It's important to remember that charm pricing is just a marketing tactic, and it doesn't always mean that an item is actually a good deal. It's always a good idea to compare prices and do some research before making a purchase to make sure you're getting the best deal possible.
