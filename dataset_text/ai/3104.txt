Pluto was reclassified as a "dwarf planet" in 2006 because it did not meet the criteria for being a full-fledged planet. According to the International Astronomical Union (IAU), a planet must meet three criteria: it must orbit the Sun, it must be round (meaning it is roughly spherical in shape), and it must have "cleared its orbit" of other objects. This means that it must be the dominant object in its orbit and not be sharing its orbit with other objects of similar size. Pluto is round, but it shares its orbit with other objects in the Kuiper Belt, a region of the solar system beyond Neptune that is populated by small, icy objects. Because of this, the IAU determined that Pluto does not meet the third criterion for being a planet. Instead, it is classified as a dwarf planet. As the meteor falls through the atmosphere, it leaves a glowing trail behind it, which is what we see as a shooting star. This glowing trail is caused by the heat generated as the meteor vaporizes.
