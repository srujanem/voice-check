Exercise can affect your appetite in a few different ways. Here are a few possibilities:\n\nWhen you work out, your body uses energy (calories) to fuel the activity. This can make you feel less hungry, because your body is already getting energy from the workout instead of needing it from food.\n\nExercise can also increase your metabolism, which is the process your body uses to turn food into energy. An increased metabolism means your body is using energy (calories) more quickly, which can also make you feel less hungry.\n\nExercise can also stimulate the release of certain hormones in the body, such as endorphins and adrenaline, which can help to suppress appetite.\n\nFinally, exercise can simply take up time that you might otherwise have spent eating, which can also contribute to a decrease in appetite.\n\nSo, in summary, working out can curb your appetite because it uses up energy, increases your metabolism, stimulates the release of certain hormones, and takes up time you might have spent eating.
