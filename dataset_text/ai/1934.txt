Dark matter is a type of matter that scientists believe exists because of the way that galaxies and other large structures in the universe behave. It doesn't emit, absorb, or reflect light, which means we can't see it directly. Instead, we infer its presence based on the gravitational effects it has on visible matter, like stars and galaxies. One way to think about dark matter is to imagine a tray of ice cream scoops. The ice cream represents the visible matter in the universe, like stars and galaxies. The tray represents dark matter, and it's holding up the ice cream scoops. Even though we can't see the tray, we know it's there because of the way it affects the ice cream. Scientists believe that dark matter makes up about 27% of the universe, while the rest is made up of visible matter and a mysterious force called dark energy. We don't yet know what dark matter is made of, but we have some ideas about what it might be. Understanding dark matter could help us learn more about the history and evolution of the universe.
