Repressed memories are memories that have been pushed out of a person's conscious awareness. This can happen for a variety of reasons, such as a traumatic event that a person finds too difficult to think about or remember. Repressed memories are still stored in the brain, but they are not easily accessible to the person's conscious thoughts and feelings. Sometimes, something can trigger a repressed memory, causing it to resurface in a person's consciousness. For example, a smell, a sound, or a sight might remind a person of a past event that they had forgotten about. When this happens, the person might have a sudden, vivid recollection of the event and feel strong emotions associated with it. It's important to note that repressed memories are not the same as forgotten memories. People can forget things for many reasons, such as because they were not paying attention at the time or because the event was not important to them. Repressed memories, on the other hand, are memories that have been actively pushed out of a person's awareness because they are too painful or difficult to think about.
