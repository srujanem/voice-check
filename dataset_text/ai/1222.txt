There are several ways that historians validate the accuracy of historical accounts and determine the ages of historical events and documents. Here are a few examples: 
1. Carbon dating: This is a method used to determine the age of organic materials, such as bones or artifacts made of wood or other organic materials. It works by measuring the amount of carbon-14, a radioactive isotope of carbon, in the material. The amount of carbon-14 decreases over time, so by measuring how much is left, scientists can determine how old the material is. 
2. Document analysis: Historians often study the language, style, and handwriting of historical documents to determine their age and authenticity. They may also examine the materials the document is written on and the ink used to see if they are consistent with the time period the document is supposed to be from. 
3. Archaeology: Archaeologists study physical remains, such as artifacts and structures, to learn about the past. They can use various techniques, such as carbon dating and stratigraphy (the study of layers of soil or sediment), to determine the age of the remains they find. 
4. Eyewitness accounts: Some historical events were recorded by people who witnessed them firsthand. These accounts can be valuable sources of information, but they must be carefully evaluated to determine their reliability. 
5. Comparison with other historical accounts: Historians often compare different accounts of the same event to see if they agree with each other and to get a more complete understanding of what happened. 
By using a combination of these and other methods, historians are able to validate the accuracy of historical accounts and determine the ages of historical events and documents.
