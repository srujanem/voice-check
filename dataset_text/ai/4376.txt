Airplanes have seat belts that go across your lap because they are designed to keep you safe in the event of turbulence or rough air while the plane is in the air. The lap belt is similar to the seat belt in a car, but it goes across your lap instead of your shoulder and waist. The lap belt helps to keep you securely in your seat and prevents you from falling out of your seat or hitting the ceiling of the plane if the plane encounters rough air.\n\nIn a car, the seat belt goes across your shoulder and lap because it is designed to keep you safe in the event of a car accident. The shoulder belt helps to keep you securely in your seat and prevents you from hitting the dashboard or steering wheel if the car comes to a sudden stop. The lap belt helps to keep you securely in your seat and prevents you from sliding out of the seat if the car turns or goes around a corner.\n\nAirplanes and cars are designed to keep you safe in different ways, and that's why they have different types of seat belts.
