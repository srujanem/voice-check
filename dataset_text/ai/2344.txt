Smells don't "run out" because they are not a physical substance that can be used up. Instead, a smell is a type of chemical signal that is emitted by a substance and detected by our sense of smell. When you smell something, what you're actually detecting is a group of molecules that have been released into the air. These molecules enter your nose and interact with special cells called scent receptors, which send a signal to your brain telling it what you're smelling. As long as the substance that is emitting the smell is still present and able to release molecules into the air, you will be able to smell it. So, for example, if you have a jar of coffee beans that you open and smell, you will be able to smell the coffee beans as long as they are still in the jar and able to release their aroma. However, if you seal the jar or if the coffee beans start to go stale and lose their aroma, you will no longer be able to smell them. I hope this helps to explain things!
