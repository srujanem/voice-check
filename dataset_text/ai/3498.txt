When you look into a mirror, the image you see is actually a reflection of yourself. The way that reflections work is that light bounces off an object and then reflects back to your eyes. When light reflects off a mirror, it bounces off in a straight line. If you were to hold a mirror in front of you and trace the path of the light with your finger, you would see that it goes straight from your finger, to the mirror, and then back to your eye. Now, let's think about what would happen if the mirror reversed the image vertically instead of horizontally. When you look at an object, the top of the object is usually above the bottom. But if the mirror reversed the image vertically, the top of the object would be below the bottom, which doesn't make sense. On the other hand, if the mirror reversed the image horizontally, the left side of the object would be on the right side of the reflection, which does make sense. So, to sum it up, mirrors reflect images horizontally because it makes more sense to us when the left side of an object is on the right side of the reflection.
