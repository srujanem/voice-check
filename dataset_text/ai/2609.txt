Fighter jets have a system called a radar that helps them locate other objects in the air, such as other jets. When a jet wants to "lock on" to another jet, it uses its radar to find the location of the other jet and then follows it. The other jet can realize that it has been locked onto by another jet by using its own radar to detect the radar of the other jet. Here's an example of how it might work: Imagine that there are two jets flying in the sky. Jet A wants to lock onto Jet B. Jet A turns on its radar and starts scanning the sky to find Jet B. When Jet A's radar finds Jet B, it sends a signal back to Jet A telling it where Jet B is. Jet A can then use this information to follow Jet B and stay close to it. Meanwhile, Jet B has its own radar turned on and is also looking for other jets in the sky. When Jet B's radar detects the radar of Jet A, it knows that Jet A is nearby and may be trying to lock onto it. Jet B can then take evasive action, such as changing direction or altitude, to try to break the lock and escape. So, to sum up, a fighter jet can lock onto another jet by using its radar to locate the other jet and then following it. The other jet can realize that it has been locked onto by detecting the radar of the other jet and taking evasive action.
