Great question! When a government wants to print more money, they send an order to the mint, which is a special factory that makes coins and paper money. The mint uses special machines to print the money and cut it into the correct size and shape. After the money is printed, it is packed into bags or boxes and shipped to banks and other financial institutions. The banks then give the money to customers when they make withdrawals from their accounts or exchange it for other currencies. So, in short, the government orders new money to be printed at the mint, and then the mint sends it to banks, which give it to customers when they need it. Does that help explain the process?
