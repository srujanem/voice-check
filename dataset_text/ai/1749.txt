Light is made up of tiny particles called photons. Photons are so small that they are usually thought of as waves instead of particles. But they can also act like particles when they are being studied in certain experiments. For example, imagine you are shining a flashlight on a wall. The flashlight is made up of a lot of tiny particles called atoms. The atoms have a lot of energy, and when they release that energy, it creates light. The light is made up of photons, which are moving really fast and traveling in straight lines. When the photons hit the wall, they can be absorbed by the atoms in the wall or they can bounce off. When the photons bounce off, you can see the light. But when the photons are absorbed, you can't see the light anymore. This is why the light from a flashlight looks different when it hits a white wall versus a black wall. The white wall reflects more of the light (bounces more of the photons off), while the black wall absorbs more of the light (takes more of the photons inside). So, light can be thought of as both a wave and a particle. It behaves like a wave when it is traveling through space, but it can also behave like a particle when it is interacting with other objects. This idea is called wave-particle duality.
