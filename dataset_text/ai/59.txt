The Amazon Rainforest is the world's largest tropical rainforest, spanning nine South American countries and covering over two million square miles. Often described as the "lungs of the Earth," it absorbs vast quantities of atmospheric carbon dioxide while generating a significant portion of global oxygen. The basin hosts an unparalleled density of life, containing tens of thousands of plant species, millions of insect varieties, and thousands of bird and mammal species. Protecting the Amazon from deforestation and industrial exploitation is essential for preserving worldwide biodiversity and stabilizing global climate systems.
