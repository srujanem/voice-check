Cats, like many animals, have an instinctive fear of being in water. This is because in the wild, water can be dangerous for cats. For example, cats could drown in deep water or become prey for other animals while trying to drink from a river or lake. Cats also don't have many ways to dry off after they get wet, so they may be uncomfortable and chilled if they get soaked. This could be especially unpleasant for a domestic cat who is used to being warm and dry in a cozy home. It's also possible that your cat's fear of water is due to a negative experience he had with water in the past. Cats can be sensitive to changes in their environment, and if your cat was ever accidentally splashed or sprayed with water and became frightened, he may have developed a fear of water as a result. In any case, it's important to respect your cat's fear of water and not force him to be around it if he doesn't want to be. You can try slowly introducing your cat to water in a controlled and positive way, such as by offering him a small dish of water to drink from, but always be mindful of his comfort level and never force him to interact with water if he seems afraid or anxious.
