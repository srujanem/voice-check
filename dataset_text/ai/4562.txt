The animal fur industry can hurt animals in a number of ways. First, many animals used for their fur, such as mink and foxes, are raised on fur farms where they are kept in small, crowded cages and may not have access to the same types of environments and social interactions as they would in the wild. These conditions can cause stress and suffering for the animals.\n\nAdditionally, even if an animal's fur is shaved rather than being removed through other methods, the process of catching and handling the animal can be stressful and potentially harmful. For example, animals may be caught in traps or nets, which can cause injury or trauma.\n\nSheep, on the other hand, are often sheared for their wool rather than their fur, and the process of shearing is generally considered to be less harmful than other methods of removing fur. However, there are still concerns about the conditions in which some sheep are raised and the potential for them to be subjected to mistreatment or abuse.\n\nIn general, it's important to consider the welfare of animals when using products made from their fur, wool, or other materials. There are often alternatives available that are more humane and do not involve exploiting or harming animals.
