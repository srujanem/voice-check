"Shall" and "will" are both auxiliary verbs that are used to indicate the future tense in English. However, they are used in slightly different ways.\n\n"Will" is used to express a strong intention or determination to do something in the future. For example:\n\n"I will finish this project on time." (I am determined to finish it on time.)\n"We will go to the beach tomorrow." (We have decided to go to the beach tomorrow.)\n"Shall" is used to offer or suggest something, or to ask for someone's opinion or advice. For example:\n\n"Shall we go to the park this afternoon?" (I am suggesting that we go to the park.)\n"What shall I wear to the party?" (I am asking for your opinion on what I should wear.)\nIn formal writing and speech, "shall" is often used instead of "will" to make a statement more formal or polite. For example:\n\n"The guests shall be served dinner at 8 PM." (This is a more formal way of saying "The guests will be served dinner at 8 PM.")\n"Will not" and "shall not" are negative forms of "will" and "shall," respectively. They are used to indicate that something will not happen in the future. For example:\n\n"I will not be able to attend the meeting." (I will not be there at the meeting.)\n"We shall not be responsible for any damages." (We will not take responsibility for any damages.)\nTo explain it simply: "will" is used to express determination or intention, while "shall" is used to offer or suggest something or ask for someone's opinion. "Will not" and "shall not" are used to say that something will not happen in the future.
