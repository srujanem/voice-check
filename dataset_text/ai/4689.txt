The perceived loudness of a sound can be affected by a variety of factors, including the environment in which the sound is heard, the frequency of the sound, and the duration of the sound.\n\nIn a store, the sound of people talking and moving around, as well as the background music and other sounds, may all contribute to the overall noise level. When you play a recording of the store in your office, you are only hearing a part of the noise, and it may sound louder because there are fewer other sounds to mask it or compete with it.\n\nThe frequency of a sound, or the pitch, can also affect how loud it sounds. Some frequencies are more easily heard by the human ear, and so a sound with a lot of these frequencies may sound louder even if it is the same volume as a sound with fewer of these frequencies.\n\nThe duration of a sound can also play a role in how loud it seems. A short, sharp sound may seem louder than a longer, more drawn-out sound, even if they are both the same volume.\n\nSo even though two sounds may have the same decibel level, they can still sound different to the human ear because of these other factors.
