Rocket thrusters work by using a fuel source and an oxidizer to create a chemical reaction that produces hot gases. These hot gases are then expelled out of the back of the rocket through a nozzle, which is a specially shaped opening that helps to accelerate and direct the flow of gases. The shape of the nozzle determines the shape of the flame that is produced by the rocket.\n\nIn the case of a rocket, the nozzle is usually shaped like a cone, with a small opening at the front and a wider opening at the back. This cone-shaped nozzle helps to create a narrow, concentrated jet of hot gases that can be directed in a specific direction. The gases are expelled at a very high speed, which generates a lot of thrust and propels the rocket forward.\n\nThe flame of a rocket is different from the flame of a flamethrower, which is designed to produce a wide, diffuse flame. A flamethrower is used to create heat and light, rather than to produce thrust, so it doesn't need a nozzle to focus the gases. Instead, the flamethrower simply allows the gases to escape in all directions, which creates a wide, spreading flame.
