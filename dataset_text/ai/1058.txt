Bail is a system that allows people who have been arrested to temporarily leave jail while they wait for their trial. If someone can't afford to pay the full amount of their bail, they might be able to pay a bail bondsman a smaller, non-refundable fee to post bail for them. 
The amount of bail that a person must pay is usually determined by a judge or a bail schedule, which is a list of standard bail amounts for different types of crimes. The judge or bail schedule will consider the seriousness of the crime, the defendant's criminal history, and whether the defendant is considered a flight risk (meaning they are likely to run away and not come back for their court date). 
Sometimes, wealthier people can afford to pay higher bail amounts because they have more money. This can make it easier for them to get out of jail while they wait for their trial. However, it's important to note that everyone has the right to a fair trial and to be treated equally under the law, regardless of their wealth.
