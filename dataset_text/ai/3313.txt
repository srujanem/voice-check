Section 4 of the Voting Rights Act (VRA) is a key provision that determines which states and localities are subject to the preclearance requirement in Section 5 of the VRA. Preclearance is a process in which certain states and localities with a history of discrimination in voting must get federal approval before making any changes to their voting laws or procedures. The Supreme Court declared Section 4 of the VRA unconstitutional in the 2013 case Shelby County v. Holder. The Court ruled that the formula used in Section 4 to determine which states and localities are subject to the preclearance requirement was outdated and no longer reflected current conditions. The formula was based on data from the 1960s and 1970s and did not take into account the significant progress that had been made in combating discrimination in voting since that time. The Court did not rule that the preclearance requirement itself was unconstitutional, but rather that the formula used to determine which states and localities were subject to the requirement was no longer valid. This effectively made the preclearance requirement inoperable, as there was no longer a way to determine which states and localities were subject to it. In simple terms, the Supreme Court ruled that the way that the Voting Rights Act was being used to decide which states and localities needed to get federal approval before changing their voting laws was no longer fair, because it was based on old information and did not take into account the progress that had been made in fighting discrimination.
