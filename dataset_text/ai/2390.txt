Piezoelectricity is a property that some materials have, which means that they can change shape when an electric current is applied to them, or produce an electric current when they are squeezed or deformed. For example, if you have a piezoelectric material and you apply an electric current to it, the material will start to vibrate or move. On the other hand, if you squeeze or deform a piezoelectric material, it will produce an electric current. It's a bit like a rubber band that you can stretch and let go of. When you stretch the rubber band, it stores energy, and when you let go, the energy is released and the rubber band snaps back to its original shape. Piezoelectric materials work in a similar way, but instead of storing and releasing energy as movement, they store and release energy as electricity. Does that make sense?
