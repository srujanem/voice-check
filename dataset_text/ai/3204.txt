Human eyesight is typically measured in terms of visual acuity, which is a measure of how clearly a person can see small details. The standard unit of measurement for visual acuity is the "decimal," which is the distance at which a person can correctly identify a particular detail on a standard eye chart. This distance is then converted to a fraction, with the denominator representing the distance in feet and the numerator representing the size of the detail being viewed. For example, a visual acuity of 20/20 means that a person can correctly identify a particular detail from a distance of 20 feet. In general, however, objects that are moving very quickly, such as a bullet or a fast-moving car, may appear to be a blur or a streak to the naked eye because our brains cannot process the rapid movement in real-time. On the other hand, objects that are moving more slowly may be more easily perceived by the naked eye, even if they are moving quickly relative to their size.
