For example, if the exchange rate between the US dollar and the Japanese yen is 1 dollar to 100 yen, then it means that if you want to buy something that costs 100 yen, you would have to pay 1 dollar. Exchange rates can change for many reasons, such as changes in the economy of a country or differences in interest rates between countries. When the exchange rate for a particular currency goes up, it means that the currency is worth more in other countries, so it's easier to buy things from those countries. When the exchange rate goes down, it means that the currency is worth less in other countries, so it's harder to buy things from those countries. Exchange rates can have a big impact on trade between countries. If a country's currency becomes more valuable, it can make the country's exports (goods and services sold to other countries) more expensive, which can lead to less demand for those exports. On the other hand, if a country's currency becomes less valuable, it can make the country's imports (goods and services bought from other countries) cheaper, which can lead to more demand for those imports. So when you hear that a currency is "climbing" or "losing value," it just means that the exchange rate for that currency is changing, which can have both positive and negative effects on the country's economy.
