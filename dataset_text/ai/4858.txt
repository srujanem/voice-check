Sure! "Who" and "whom" are pronouns that we use to ask questions or to refer to people in sentences. "Who" is the subject pronoun, which means it is used when the person is doing the action in the sentence. "Whom" is the object pronoun, which means it is used when the person is receiving the action in the sentence.\n\nHere are some examples to help clarify the difference:\n\n"Who is coming to the party?" - In this sentence, "who" is the subject pronoun because it is referring to the person who is coming to the party.\n\n"Whom did you invite to the party?" - In this sentence, "whom" is the object pronoun because it is referring to the person who is being invited to the party.\n\nIf you're still unsure about which one to use, you can try substituting "he" or "she" for "who" and "him" or "her" for "whom" in the sentence to see which one makes more sense.\n\nFor example:\n\n"Who is coming to the party?" can be changed to "She is coming to the party."\n\n"Whom did you invite to the party?" can be changed to "You invited her to the party."\n\nIf the sentence still makes sense with the substitution, then you know you have the right pronoun.\n\nI hope this helps!
