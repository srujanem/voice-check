Our eyes move in rapid jolts, or saccades, because it helps us see things more clearly and efficiently. When we look at something, our eyes don't stay completely still. Instead, they make tiny movements all the time to help us see things more clearly. These movements are called saccades. Saccades help us to quickly move our gaze from one place to another and to focus on different objects. They also help us to scan a scene and gather information about our surroundings. When our eyes make saccades, we don't see the rapid eye movement because our brains process the information from our eyes in a way that makes it seem like the image is smooth and continuous. This is called saccadic masking. So, even though our eyes are making rapid jolts, our brains make it seem like we are seeing a smooth, seamless image. This helps us to see things more clearly and efficiently.
