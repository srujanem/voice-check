Hanlon's Razor is a principle that suggests that you should not assume that someone is being intentionally mean or malicious when they do something that causes a problem. Instead, you should try to understand whether there is a simpler explanation, such as that the person made a mistake or did not know any better.\n\nHere's an example of how Hanlon's Razor might be applied:\n\nImagine that your friend accidentally spills a drink on your phone and it stops working. You might be angry and think that your friend did it on purpose to harm you. However, Hanlon's razor would suggest that you consider the possibility that your friend simply made a mistake and did not mean to cause any harm. In this case, it would be more accurate to attribute the problem to stupidity (or carelessness) rather than malice.\n\nIn other words, Hanlon's Razor reminds us to not jump to conclusions about someone's intentions and to try to understand the situation from their perspective before reacting.
