When a bee stings a person or animal, the stinger becomes stuck in the skin and then tears away from the bee's body, along with a part of the bee's digestive tract, muscles, and nerves. The bee is then unable to eat or defend itself and dies as a result. As for why bees have evolved to die after losing their stingers, it's actually a strategy that benefits the hive as a whole. When a bee stings a predator, it releases a chemical called pheromone that attracts other bees to attack. This helps to protect the hive and the rest of the bees from harm. However, this defense mechanism comes at a cost to the individual bee, which sacrifices its own life to protect the hive. So, while it might seem like it would be beneficial for bees to evolve to not die after losing their stingers, the death of individual bees helps to protect the hive and ensure the survival of the colony as a whole. This is an example of something called "altruistic behavior," where an individual organism behaves in a way that benefits others at its own expense.
