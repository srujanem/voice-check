A sore throat is a common condition that can be caused by many things, such as a cold or the flu, or even allergies. When you have a sore throat, it means that the inside of your throat is inflamed, or swollen. This can make it feel scratchy, dry, or painful to swallow. The soreness in your throat is caused by inflammation in the tissues that line the inside of your throat. This inflammation can be caused by a virus or bacteria that has infected the tissues, or it can be caused by irritation from things like smoke or pollution. When you have a sore throat, your body may also try to get rid of the virus or bacteria by producing extra mucus. This extra mucus can make you feel like you want to cough, which can help to clear your throat of any irritants. Overall, a sore throat is your body's way of trying to fight off an infection or irritation, and the symptoms you're experiencing are a result of that inflammation and your body's attempts to get rid of the problem.
