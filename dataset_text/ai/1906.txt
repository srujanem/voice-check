Centrifugal force is not a "real" force in the sense that it is not something that is caused by one object acting on another object through direct contact. It is actually a fictitious force that is created as a result of an object moving in a circular path. Imagine you are on a carousel at an amusement park. As the carousel spins, you feel like you are being pushed outward, away from the center of the carousel. This is the sensation of centrifugal force. In reality, you are not being pushed outward by any force at all. You are just sitting on a platform that is spinning in a circle. The sensation of being pushed outward is due to the fact that you are moving in a circular path and your body is trying to continue moving in a straight line (which is called inertia). To understand this better, you can try this simple experiment: hold a ball on a string and swing it in a circle around your head. As you swing the ball in a circle, you will feel a tug on the string as the ball tries to fly off in a straight line. This is the same sensation that you feel on the carousel. The ball is not being pushed outward by any force, it is just trying to continue moving in a straight line due to its inertia. So to sum it up, centrifugal force is not a real force, it is just a sensation that we feel when we are moving in a circular path. It is caused by the inertia of our bodies trying to continue moving in a straight line.
