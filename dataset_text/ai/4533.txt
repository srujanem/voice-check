Sure! Fiber optic cables work by using thin strands of glass or plastic called "optical fibers" to transmit information. These optical fibers are very thin, about the width of a human hair, and they are surrounded by a protective coating.\n\nLight travels through the optical fibers by bouncing off the walls of the fiber. This is possible because the inside of the fiber is very smooth and the light waves do not get absorbed or scattered as they travel through it.\n\nTo transmit information, the fiber optic cable uses different colors of light, or different wavelengths of light, to represent different bits of information. For example, one color of light might represent a "1" and another color might represent a "0".\n\nAt each end of the fiber optic cable, there is a device called a "transmitter" that converts electronic signals (like the ones your computer sends) into light signals. The transmitter sends these light signals through the fiber optic cable to the receiver at the other end.\n\nThe receiver at the other end of the fiber optic cable then converts the light signals back into electronic signals that can be understood by other devices, like a computer or a television.\n\nDoes that help explain how fiber optic cables work?
