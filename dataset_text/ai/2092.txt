The Troy Davis case was a legal case in the United States in which Troy Davis was convicted of murder and sentenced to death. In 1991, Davis was charged with the murder of a police officer in Savannah, Georgia. The case received a lot of attention because there were questions about whether Davis was actually guilty of the crime. There was evidence against Davis that seemed to suggest he was involved in the crime. For example, several witnesses testified that they saw him at the scene of the crime, and there was physical evidence linking him to the crime. However, there was also evidence that raised doubts about Davis's guilt. Some of the witnesses who had testified against him later changed their stories or recanted their testimony. Additionally, there were other people who claimed that they saw someone else at the scene of the crime. Despite these doubts, Davis was convicted and sentenced to death. Many people believed that he was innocent and that he had been wrongly convicted. The case sparked a lot of debate and controversy, and there were efforts to have the conviction overturned or to grant Davis a new trial. However, these efforts were ultimately unsuccessful, and Davis was executed in 2011.
