There are a few reasons why some toll road booths don't accept credit cards.\n\nFirst, it can be expensive for the toll road operator to install and maintain credit card payment machines. These machines need to be connected to the internet or a phone line in order to process the transactions, and they also need to be regularly serviced and replaced. This can add up to a lot of money over time.\n\nSecond, some toll road operators may not want to pay the fees that come with accepting credit cards. When you use a credit card to pay for something, the credit card company charges the merchant (in this case, the toll road operator) a percentage of the purchase price as a fee. This fee can be significant, especially if a lot of people use credit cards to pay the tolls.\n\nFinally, some toll road operators may simply not see the need to accept credit cards. If most people who use the toll road are paying with cash or a toll pass, it may not make sense for the operator to invest in credit card payment machines.\n\nOverall, there are a lot of factors that can influence whether or not a toll road booth accepts credit cards. Some toll road operators may choose to accept credit cards in order to make it easier for customers to pay, while others may decide not to accept them due to the cost and hassle involved.
