Sure, I can try to explain the concept of burden of proof in a simple way. Imagine that there are two sides to an argument, and each side is trying to prove that their side is right. The burden of proof is the responsibility of one of these sides to provide evidence and arguments to support their claims. In the context of climate change, the burden of proof might be on those who deny that climate change is happening or that it is caused by human activity. They would need to provide evidence and arguments to support their claims, while those who believe that climate change is real and caused by human activity would need to provide evidence to support their claims as well. So, the burden of proof is about who has the responsibility to provide evidence for their side of an argument. It helps to make sure that both sides of an argument are carefully examined and that decisions are based on solid evidence and reasoning.
