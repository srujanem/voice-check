In hockey, players are organized into "lines" of forwards, which consist of a left winger, a center, and a right winger. These lines usually stay together for a shift, which lasts for about a minute or two, before coming off the ice and being replaced by a fresh group of players. The first line is usually considered the best and is expected to score the most goals and make the most plays. However, this is not always the case, and a team's best players may be spread out over multiple lines. During a power play, when one team has a numerical advantage due to a penalty being called on the other team, the coach may choose to change the lines to try to take advantage of the extra player. For example, they may put out a line with three skilled forwards in an attempt to score a goal. Overall, the coach has a lot of discretion in deciding which lines to use and when to change them. They may make changes based on the score of the game, the matchups they want to create on the ice, or the performance of the players.
