Explosives and flammable materials are not mutually exclusive because they can both be ignited or set off by a spark or flame. However, the way in which they react to being ignited is different. Flammable materials, like wood or paper, burn slowly and produce heat and light when they are ignited. They do not produce a sudden, powerful explosion like explosives do. Explosives, on the other hand, are specially designed to produce a sudden, powerful explosion when they are ignited. This is because they contain a lot of energy that is released very quickly when the explosive is set off. A blasting cap is a small device that is used to set off explosives. It contains a small amount of a highly sensitive explosive that can be easily ignited by a spark or flame. When the blasting cap is ignited, it sets off the main charge of the explosive, causing it to detonate. So, while both flammable materials and explosives can be ignited, the way in which they react when they are ignited is very different. Flammable materials burn slowly, while explosives produce a sudden, powerful explosion.
