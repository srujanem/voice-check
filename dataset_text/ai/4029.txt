In the years leading up to 2007, banks and other financial institutions made a lot of loans to people who were buying homes. Some of these loans were called "subprime mortgages," which means that the people taking out the loans had lower credit scores and might not have been able to get a regular mortgage. The banks and financial institutions made these subprime mortgages because they thought that the housing market was going to keep going up, so even if some of the people who took out the loans couldn't afford to pay them back, the banks would still be able to sell the houses for more than they had paid for them and make a profit. Unfortunately, the housing market didn't keep going up. In fact, it started to go down, and a lot of the people who had taken out subprime mortgages couldn't afford to pay them back. This caused the banks and financial institutions to lose a lot of money, and it also made it harder for other people to get loans because the banks were afraid of losing more money. This is part of what caused the financial crisis of 2007, which was a very bad time for the economy. The government didn't force the banks to give out the subprime mortgages, but some people think that the government could have done more to regulate the housing market and make sure that the banks were being careful about who they gave loans to.
