When you brush your teeth, you use toothpaste that contains a chemical called sodium lauryl sulfate. This chemical helps to break up and remove food particles and plaque from your teeth. It also creates a foamy texture in your mouth that helps to spread the toothpaste around. The flavor of toothpaste is usually minty or fruity, and it can overpower the taste of other things you eat or drink. That's why orange juice and other fruit juices might taste bad after you brush your teeth. The toothpaste can mask the natural flavors of the juice, making it taste strange or unpleasant. It's also possible that the toothpaste can change the way that your tongue and mouth perceive the flavors of the juice. The chemicals in the toothpaste might affect your taste buds or the nerves in your mouth, causing you to taste things differently. It's a good idea to wait at least 30 minutes after brushing your teeth to eat or drink anything, especially acidic or sugary foods and drinks. This can help to protect your teeth and allow the toothpaste to fully do its job of cleaning your mouth.
