Japan attacked the US in WWII because they were trying to expand their empire and gain more resources. At the time, the US had a large military presence in the Pacific, and Japan saw attacking the US as a way to try to weaken that presence and gain more control in the region. The attack on Hawaii, which was a surprise attack on the US naval base at Pearl Harbor, was part of this effort to try to damage the US military's ability to defend itself and its interests in the Pacific. Japan likely hoped that by attacking the US, they could convince the US to retreat from the region and allow Japan to continue to expand its empire without interference.
