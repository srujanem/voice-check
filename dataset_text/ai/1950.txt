Fire is a chemical reaction that occurs when certain materials, called fuels, are exposed to oxygen and a source of heat. When the heat causes the fuel to reach a high enough temperature, a chemical reaction called combustion occurs. Combustion is the process of burning, and it releases heat and light as a result. Wood is made up of organic matter, which means it is made up of molecules that contain carbon and hydrogen. When wood is exposed to oxygen and a source of heat, the heat causes the molecules in the wood to break apart and release energy in the form of heat and light. This is why wood can burn and cause a fire. Rocks, on the other hand, are not made up of organic matter. They do not contain carbon and hydrogen, so they do not have the necessary fuel to burn. Therefore, rocks cannot burn or cause a fire.
