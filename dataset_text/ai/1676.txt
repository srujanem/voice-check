The phone hacking scandal involving Rupert Murdoch's company, News Corporation, was a series of criminal investigations that took place in the United Kingdom in the early 2010s. The scandal centered on allegations that the News of the World, a British newspaper owned by News Corporation, had hacked the phones of thousands of people in order to get information for their stories. This included celebrities, politicians, and even victims of crime and terrorism. The scandal was considered a major breach of ethics in journalism, as journalists are supposed to gather information through legitimate means, not by illegally accessing someone's private information. It also raised concerns about the power and influence of the media, and whether news organizations were acting in the public interest or just trying to sell newspapers. The scandal led to the closure of the News of the World, and several people, including high-ranking executives at News Corporation, were arrested and charged with crimes related to the phone hacking. The scandal also sparked debates about media regulation and the need for stricter oversight of the press.
