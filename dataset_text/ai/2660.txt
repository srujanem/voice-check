In humans, the pitch of a person's voice is determined by the size and shape of their vocal cords and the length of their vocal tract. The vocal cords are two bands of muscle located in the larynx, or voice box, that vibrate to produce sound when we speak or sing. In males, the vocal cords are usually longer and thicker, which gives their voices a deeper, lower pitch. In females, the vocal cords are usually shorter and thinner, which gives their voices a higher, more delicate pitch. Other animals, such as dogs, do not have this same distinction in their voices because their vocal cords and vocal tracts are not as variable as those of humans. In dogs, the pitch of their bark or howl is largely determined by their size and breed, rather than their gender. There are some animals that do have distinct vocalizations for males and females, however. For example, many bird species have distinct songs or calls for males and females.
