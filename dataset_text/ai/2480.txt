Flammability is the ability of a substance to burn or ignite, and this depends on the chemical makeup of the substance. Some substances have a chemical structure that allows them to easily react with oxygen in the air, and this reaction releases energy in the form of heat and light. These substances are said to be flammable. Hydrogen and helium are flammable gases because they can react with oxygen to produce water and heat. Nitrogen, on the other hand, is not flammable because it does not react easily with oxygen. To understand this better, think about a candle. A candle is made of wax, which is a flammable substance. When you light a candle, the heat of the flame causes the wax to melt and vaporize. The vaporized wax reacts with the oxygen in the air, and this reaction produces heat, light, and water vapor (which is just steam). In the same way, when hydrogen or helium burns, it reacts with oxygen to produce heat, light, and water (or water vapor).
