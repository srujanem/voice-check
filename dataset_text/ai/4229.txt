Mouth-to-mouth resuscitation, also known as CPR (cardiopulmonary resuscitation), is a technique used to help a person who is not breathing or whose heart has stopped beating. It involves blowing air into the person's lungs through their mouth, which can help to provide oxygen to the body's cells and tissues.\n\nWhen a person is not breathing, their body is not getting the oxygen it needs to function properly. This can cause damage to the brain and other organs, and can eventually lead to death. By blowing air into the person's lungs through their mouth, we can help to get oxygen into their body and keep their organs and tissues healthy.\n\nInhaling air through the mouth and exhaling carbon dioxide through the nose is a normal part of the breathing process. When we exhale carbon dioxide, it is because our body has used up the oxygen in the air we inhaled and converted it into energy. When we inhale oxygen, it helps to replenish the supply of oxygen in our bodies and keep us healthy.\n\nMouth-to-mouth resuscitation is not a perfect solution, but it can be an effective way to help a person who is not breathing get the oxygen they need until more advanced medical help arrives. It is important to remember that CPR should only be performed by trained professionals or under the guidance of a trained professional.
