The process of a drug being made available without a prescription is called "over-the-counter" (OTC) status. In the United States, the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) is responsible for approving drugs for sale and determining their appropriate use. When a new drug is developed, it must go through a series of clinical trials to determine its safety and effectiveness. If the drug is shown to be safe and effective, the company that developed it can apply to the FDA for approval to sell the drug. If the FDA approves the drug, it can be sold with a prescription, which means that a person must see a doctor or other healthcare provider and get a prescription in order to obtain the drug. However, the FDA can also decide to make a drug available without a prescription, if it determines that the drug is safe enough for people to use without the supervision of a healthcare provider. This is called "over-the-counter" status. In order to be approved for OTC status, a drug must meet certain criteria, such as being safe for use without medical supervision and having clear instructions for how it should be used. The FDA reviews these criteria when deciding whether to approve a drug for OTC status. So, in summary, a drug can be made available without a prescription if the FDA determines that it is safe enough for people to use on their own and has clear instructions for how it should be used.
