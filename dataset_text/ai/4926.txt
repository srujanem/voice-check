Banks create money by making loans. When a bank makes a loan, it creates a new deposit in the borrower's account. This deposit can be used to make payments or to withdraw cash, just like any other deposit. The deposit is created out of thin air, and it represents a new liability for the bank.\n\nThe borrower can use the money from the loan to buy goods and services, and this can lead to an increase in economic activity. However, it's important to note that the loan has to be repaid with interest, so the borrower has to pay back more money than they received.\n\nThis practice of creating money through lending has been around for centuries. It allows banks to help fund economic growth and provide a way for people and businesses to borrow money. However, it can also lead to problems if banks make too many risky loans and people or businesses struggle to repay them. This can cause a financial crisis, like the one that occurred in 2008.\n\nOverall, the practice of banks creating money through lending can have both positive and negative effects on the economy. It's important for banks and regulators to carefully monitor lending to ensure that it is done in a responsible way.
