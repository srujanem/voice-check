The Wall Street Protests, also known as the Occupy Wall Street movement, was a series of protests that began in September 2011 in New York City. The protesters were mainly young people who were upset about a variety of issues, including income inequality, corporate greed, and the influence of money in politics. The protesters were protesting because they believed that the economic and political systems in the United States were not working in the best interests of ordinary people, and that the wealthy and powerful were able to use their money and influence to get what they wanted while the rest of the country struggled. Some of the protesters were arrested because they broke the law by engaging in acts of civil disobedience, such as blocking traffic or occupying public spaces. Others were arrested because they were involved in violent or destructive behavior. Overall, the Wall Street Protests were a way for people to voice their concerns and frustrations about the state of the country, and to try to bring about change in the ways that they saw as necessary.
