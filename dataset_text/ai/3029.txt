Occupy Wall Street (OWS) was a protest movement that began in 2011 in New York City's Zuccotti Park, with the goal of bringing attention to economic inequality and the influence of corporate interests in government. The movement was loosely organized and did not have a single, unified theory of justice or philosophy. However, some of the issues that OWS demonstrators were concerned about, such as income inequality, corporate greed, and the influence of money in politics, have been addressed by various philosophers throughout history. For example, OWS's focus on income inequality and the concentration of wealth among a small group of people might be seen as related to the ideas of philosophers such as Karl Marx, who argued that capitalism creates a system in which the rich get richer while the poor get poorer. Other philosophers, such as John Rawls, have argued that societies should be structured in a way that ensures that the most disadvantaged members are given the greatest possible level of support and opportunity. It's also worth noting that OWS was a diverse movement that included people with a wide range of political views and philosophical beliefs.
