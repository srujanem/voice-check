China's undervalued currency, also known as the Chinese yuan or renminbi, can be a problem for the global economy because it makes Chinese goods cheaper for people in other countries to buy. For example, let's say a toy is made in China and costs 100 yuan to produce. If the exchange rate (which is the rate at which one country's currency can be exchanged for another's) between the Chinese yuan and the US dollar is 1:1, then the toy would cost $100 to buy in the United States. However, if the exchange rate is 1:2 (meaning it takes two yuan to equal one dollar), then the toy would only cost $50 in the United States. This would make Chinese toys cheaper than toys made in the United States, which could make it harder for American toy makers to sell their products. This can be a problem for the global economy because it can make it harder for industries in other countries to compete with Chinese industries. It can also lead to trade imbalances, where a country exports (sells) more goods than it imports (buys). This can lead to economic problems in the long run. Overall, an undervalued currency can have negative effects on the global economy because it can make it harder for industries in other countries to compete and can lead to trade imbalances.
