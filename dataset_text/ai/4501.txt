Homeschooled kids can be evaluated for college admissions in a few different ways. One way is through standardized tests like the SAT or ACT, which can help colleges compare homeschooled students to other applicants. Another way is through transcripts, which are documents that show the courses a student has taken and the grades they received. For homeschooled students, these transcripts are often created by the student's parents or guardians.\n\nWhile it's true that the grades given to homeschooled students by their parents or guardians may not be the same as the grades given to students in a traditional school, colleges still consider these grades as part of the application process. They may also consider other factors, such as the student's extracurricular activities and any awards or recognition they have received.\n\nOverall, the process of evaluating homeschooled students for college admissions can be somewhat different from the process used for students who attend traditional schools. However, colleges and universities have experience working with homeschooled students and have developed ways to fairly assess their qualifications for admission.
