The observer effect refers to the idea that the act of observing or measuring a subatomic particle can affect its behavior. This happens because the process of observation often involves interacting with the particle in some way, such as by shining light on it or hitting it with a photon. This interaction can disturb the particle and change its behavior, just as a rock will move if you throw a ball at it.\n\nOne way to think about it is to imagine that you are trying to observe a bug crawling on a leaf. If you just watch the bug from a distance, it will continue crawling around as it normally would. But if you try to get closer to the bug to get a better look, you might accidentally bump the leaf, which will cause the bug to move. In the same way, observing a subatomic particle can cause it to change its behavior because the act of observation involves interacting with the particle.
