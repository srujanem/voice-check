Public accounting and private accounting are two different types of accounting that serve different types of organizations.\n\nPublic accounting involves working for a public accounting firm, which provides accounting, tax, and consulting services to a variety of clients, including businesses, government agencies, and individuals. Public accountants often work on financial statements, tax returns, and audits, and they may also provide advice on financial planning, budgeting, and other business-related matters.\n\nPrivate accounting, on the other hand, involves working for a specific company or organization to handle their financial affairs. Private accountants are responsible for managing the financial records and transactions of their employer, which can include tasks such as preparing financial statements, budgeting, and analyzing financial data.\n\nIn terms of education and degrees, both public and private accountants typically need to have a bachelor's degree in accounting or a related field. Some public accounting firms may also require candidates to have a master's degree or to pass the Certified Public Accountant (CPA) exam. Private accounting positions may require different certifications or degrees depending on the specific company or organization.
