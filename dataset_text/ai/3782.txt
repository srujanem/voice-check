DJ mixers are used to control and manipulate the audio signals of music tracks. They allow DJs to blend and mix different tracks together, adjust the levels of each track, add effects, and more. There are several different types of knobs, sliders, and buttons on DJ mixers, and each one serves a specific purpose. Here are some general categories of controls you might find on a DJ mixer: Channel faders: These are long, vertical sliders that control the volume of each individual track. DJs use these to adjust the levels of each track as they mix them together. EQ controls: These are knobs or sliders that allow DJs to adjust the bass, mid, and treble frequencies of each track. This allows them to fine-tune the sound of each track and make it fit better with the other tracks they are mixing. Effects controls: Some DJ mixers have built-in effects, like reverb or delay, that DJs can use to add extra flair to their mixes. The controls for these effects might include knobs, buttons, or sliders. Crossfader: This is a horizontal slider that allows DJs to blend two tracks together smoothly. As one track fades out, the other track will fade in, creating a seamless transition between the two. Cue buttons: These buttons allow DJs to listen to a track in their headphones before bringing it into the mix. This allows them to get a feel for the track and prepare for the next mix. Loop controls: Some DJ mixers have buttons or knobs that allow DJs to create loops, or repeating sections, of a track. This can be used to create special effects or to extend a track. There are many other types of controls on DJ mixers, and the specific controls on any given mixer will depend on the model and features it has. But these are some of the most common controls you'll find on DJ mixers.
