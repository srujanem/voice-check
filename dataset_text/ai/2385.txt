The Second Egyptian Uprising, also known as the Egyptian Revolution of 2011, was a series of protests and demonstrations that took place in Egypt in 2011. The people were protesting against the government of Hosni Mubarak, who had been in power for over 30 years. They were calling for political reforms, including free and fair elections, and for an end to corruption and repression. The protests began on January 25, 2011, when a group of young people gathered in Cairo's Tahrir Square to protest against the government. They were inspired by similar protests that had taken place in other countries, such as Tunisia and Libya, and they used social media to organize and spread the word about their demonstrations. The protests quickly gained support from other people in Egypt, and soon there were protests and demonstrations taking place all across the country. The government responded with violence, arresting and attacking protesters, but the protesters continued to speak out and demand change. After several weeks of protests, President Mubarak stepped down and handed over power to the military. The people of Egypt celebrated, but the situation in the country has remained uncertain since then, with ongoing political struggles and unrest.
