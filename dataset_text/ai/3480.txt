Being a juror is not typically considered a career because it is a temporary role that is typically only done for a few days or weeks at a time. Jurors are selected from the general public to serve on a jury for a specific trial, and then they are finished serving once the trial is over. Jurors do not need to have any special training or education in the law. In fact, it is often considered important for jurors to be "average" members of the community, without any particular legal expertise, so that they can represent the perspectives of ordinary people. Judges and lawyers do need to have specialized training in the law because they have more permanent roles in the legal system. They are responsible for interpreting and applying the law on a daily basis, and they need to have a deep understanding of the legal system in order to do this effectively. So while being a juror is an important part of the legal system and can be a very meaningful and fulfilling experience, it is not typically considered a career in the same way that being a judge or lawyer is.
