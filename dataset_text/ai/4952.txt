Yes, there are different types of snow! Snowflakes are made of ice crystals, and the way the ice crystals form and group together can affect the texture and structure of the snow. When the temperature is cold enough, the ice crystals can form in a way that creates snowflakes with a lot of flat, tightly packed surfaces. This type of snow is called "packed snow" or "firm snow", and it is easy to make objects with because it sticks together well and doesn't fall apart easily. On the other hand, if the temperature is slightly warmer, the ice crystals might not form as tightly together and the snowflakes might have more rounded, fluffy shapes. This type of snow is called "fluffy snow" or "light snow", and it is not as easy to make objects with because it falls apart more easily. So the type of snow that you get depends on the temperature and humidity conditions when the snow is forming in the clouds.
