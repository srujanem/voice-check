Fizzy drinks, also known as carbonated beverages, contain a lot of sugar and acid. The sugar can provide food for the bacteria that live in your mouth, and these bacteria can produce acid as a byproduct of breaking down the sugar. The acid can then attack the enamel on your teeth, which is the hard, outer layer that protects your teeth from damage. Even if you don't swish the fizzy drink around in your mouth, the acid can still come into contact with your teeth when you swallow the drink. Over time, this acid can wear away the enamel on your teeth and lead to problems like cavities and tooth decay. It's a good idea to drink fizzy drinks in moderation and to brush your teeth after drinking them to help prevent damage to your teeth.
