In photography, "f-stop" refers to the size of the aperture, or the opening in the lens through which light passes. The smaller the f-stop number, the larger the aperture and the more light that is allowed to pass through the lens.\n\nThe f-stop of a lens is not the same across all lenses. Different lenses have different maximum and minimum f-stop values, depending on their design and construction. For example, one lens might have a maximum aperture of f/2.8, while another lens might have a maximum aperture of f/5.6.\n\nWhen you set the f-stop of your camera to a specific value, such as f/4, this determines the size of the aperture that will be used to take the photograph. The amount of light that is allowed to pass through the lens will depend on the aperture size and the other settings of the camera, such as the shutter speed and ISO.\n\nIn general, setting the f-stop to a lower value (e.g. f/4) will allow more light to pass through the lens, while setting the f-stop to a higher value (e.g. f/16) will allow less light to pass through the lens. However, the actual amount of light that is allowed to pass through the lens will depend on the specific lens and camera that you are using.
