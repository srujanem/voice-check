The assassination of President John F. Kennedy on November 22, 1963, remains one of the most controversial and heavily debated events in modern American history. Many people believe that Lee Harvey Oswald, a former Marine who had defected to the Soviet Union and then returned to the United States, was the sole gunman who killed Kennedy. However, there are also many people who believe that Oswald did not act alone, and that there was a conspiracy involving multiple gunmen or even a government cover-up. The evidence against Oswald includes eyewitness testimony, forensic evidence, and his own past association with Marxist ideologies and the Fair Play for Cuba Committee, a pro-Castro group. However, there are also many inconsistencies and discrepancies in the official investigation, which have led some people to believe that there was a cover-up. There are many different conspiracy theories about the Kennedy assassination, ranging from the involvement of the CIA or the Mafia, to suggestions that Kennedy's vice president, Lyndon B. Johnson, was involved. However, most of these theories lack credible evidence and have been debunked by researchers. Jack Ruby, a Dallas nightclub owner, killed Oswald on November 24, 1963, while Oswald was being transferred from the city jail to the county jail. Ruby later claimed that he was angry about Kennedy's assassination and wanted to spare Jacqueline Kennedy the ordeal of a trial. However, some people believe that Ruby was part of the conspiracy or that he acted on behalf of someone else. In summary, it is generally accepted that Oswald was the gunman who killed Kennedy, but there are many questions and inconsistencies surrounding the circumstances of the assassination that have fueled conspiracy theories for decades.
