Movies are typically shot at 24 frames per second (fps) because that is the frame rate that has traditionally been used in the film industry. One reason for this is that 24 fps is the minimum frame rate required to create the illusion of continuous motion. At lower frame rates, the motion in a film can appear jerky or choppy.\n\nAnother reason that 24 fps is used is because it is the frame rate that is most compatible with the way that film stock and movie projectors work. Film stock is a type of celluloid material that is used to record movies, and it is typically shot at 24 fps. Similarly, most movie projectors are designed to play film at 24 fps.\n\nWhile it is true that higher frame rates, such as 60 fps, can provide a more smooth and realistic appearance, there are also some trade-offs to consider. For example, shooting at a higher frame rate can require more powerful and expensive equipment, and it can also result in larger file sizes. In addition, some people argue that the "look" of a movie shot at a higher frame rate can be less cinematic and more "video game-like," although this is a matter of personal preference.
