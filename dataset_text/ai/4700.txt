In general, fluids (liquids and gases) are compressible because their molecules can move closer together or farther apart, depending on the pressure applied to the fluid. This means that the volume of a compressible fluid can change when it is subjected to pressure.\n\nOn the other hand, non-compressible fluids have molecules that are packed very tightly together and do not have much space to move around. As a result, the volume of a non-compressible fluid does not change much when it is subjected to pressure.\n\nWater is a non-compressible fluid because its molecules are packed very closely together and do not have much space to move around. This is why the volume of water does not change much when it is subjected to pressure.\n\nOther fluids, such as air, are more compressible because their molecules have more space to move around and can be squeezed closer together under pressure.\n\nI hope this helps to explain the difference between compressible and non-compressible fluids.
