Sound is a type of energy that travels through the air (or through any other medium, like water or metal) as a series of vibrations or waves. In order for sound to be heard, there must be a source of the sound (like a speaker or a person talking), and there must be a medium (like air or water) for the sound waves to travel through. The source of the sound vibrates the molecules in the medium, and this causes the molecules to vibrate as well. These vibrations then travel through the medium and reach our ears, where they are picked up by tiny sensors called hair cells. The hair cells send a signal to our brain, which interprets the signal as sound. Now, the reason there is no sound in space is because there is no medium (like air or water) for the sound waves to travel through. In space, there is a vacuum, which means there are no molecules for the sound waves to vibrate. Without a medium to carry the sound waves, there can be no sound. So, even if there were a source of sound in space (like an explosion), we would not be able to hear it because the sound waves would have nowhere to go.
