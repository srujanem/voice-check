Great question! The months of the year are not in numerical order because the calendars we use today are based on the movements of the Earth around the sun. It takes the Earth about 365 and a quarter days to orbit the sun, and to keep track of this movement, we have to divide the year into months. Each month is about a fourth of a year, or about 30 or 31 days. There are 12 months in a year because it's a number that is easy to divide the year into. But because the Earth takes a little bit longer than 365 days to orbit the sun, we have to add an extra day every four years. That's why we have a leap year, which has an extra day in February. So, to make sure we're keeping track of the Earth's orbit correctly, the months are arranged in a certain order, with February at the end because it's the shortest month. This way, we can add the extra day in February every leap year and make sure we're staying on track with the Earth's orbit around the sun.
