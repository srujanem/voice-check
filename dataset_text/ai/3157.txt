Catholics, Orthodox, and Protestants are all Christians, but they have some differences in their beliefs and practices. Here is a simple explanation of the main differences: Catholics and Orthodox Christians believe that the Pope, who is the leader of the Catholic Church, is the successor of Saint Peter and has a special role in leading the Church. Protestants do not believe that the Pope has this special role. Catholics and Orthodox Christians have more elaborate liturgies (worship services) and often use more symbolic rituals, such as the use of incense and holy water. Protestants generally have simpler liturgies and less emphasis on ritual. Catholics and Orthodox Christians have more hierarchical structures, with bishops and priests having authority over local congregations. Protestants have more decentralized structures, with each congregation being independent and having its own leaders. Catholics and Orthodox Christians have more traditional views on matters such as contraception and the role of women in the Church. Protestants generally have more progressive views on these issues. I hope this helps to give you a basic understanding of the differences between these three Christian traditions.
