Passive-aggressive behavior is a way of expressing anger or frustration in a way that is not direct or open. It's a way of acting that can be difficult to recognize and deal with, because the person behaving this way might not admit that they are upset or angry. Imagine that you asked a friend to help you with a project, and they agreed to do it. But then, instead of completing the project on time, they keep finding excuses to delay it. They might say things like "I'm too busy" or "I can't do it right now." This can be frustrating, because it's not clear what the problem is. You might feel like your friend is being unhelpful or not taking your request seriously. This is an example of passive-aggressive behavior. The friend is not openly saying that they don't want to help with the project, but they are also not following through on their promise to do so. They are expressing their feelings indirectly, rather than directly saying what they mean. Passive-aggressive behavior can be annoying and confusing, and it can create tension in relationships. It's often better to communicate openly and honestly, rather than using passive-aggressive tactics.
