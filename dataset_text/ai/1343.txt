Back burning is a firefighting technique that involves intentionally setting a fire in a controlled area in order to burn away fuel that could potentially feed a larger wildfire. This can be an effective way to prevent the spread of a wildfire or to reduce the intensity of the fire. 
Back burning is typically done during the summer months because that is when conditions are most favorable for it. During the summer, the weather is generally dry and hot, which means that there is more fuel available to burn (such as dry grass and leaves) and the conditions are more conducive to starting and controlling a fire. In the winter, there is generally less fuel available to burn and the weather is often wet and cold, which makes it more difficult to start and control a fire. Additionally, winter weather can make it more difficult for firefighters to access the area where the back burn is being conducted. 
It's important to note that back burning is a risky and complex operation that requires careful planning and coordination. Firefighters take many factors into consideration when deciding whether or not to conduct a back burn, including the weather, the fuel conditions, and the potential risks to people and property. They also take steps to ensure that the back burn is conducted safely and effectively, such as using fire breaks (barriers designed to stop the spread of fire) and monitoring the fire closely.
