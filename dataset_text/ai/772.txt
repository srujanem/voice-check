In movies and in real life, soldiers are usually told not to fire their weapons until they are close enough to the enemy because it increases the chances of hitting their target. When a person shoots a gun, the bullet travels through the air and can be affected by things like wind, gravity, and other forces. The farther away the target is, the more time the bullet has to be affected by these forces, which can make it more difficult to hit the target. 
So, if a soldier is too far away from the enemy, their bullets might not hit the enemy even if they are aiming at them. This is why it's important to wait until the soldier is close enough to the enemy before firing their weapon. This way, the bullet has less time to be affected by external forces and is more likely to hit the target.
