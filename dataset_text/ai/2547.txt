Air traffic control is the job of coordinating the movement of airplanes in the sky and on the ground at airports. There are many people working together to make sure that planes can safely take off, fly to their destination, and land without running into each other. Here's how it works: When an airplane wants to fly somewhere, it has to ask permission from the air traffic controllers. The controllers will check to make sure there is a clear path for the airplane to fly and that it won't be too close to any other planes. The controllers will also look at the weather to make sure it is safe for the airplane to fly. If there is a storm or other dangerous conditions, the controllers may tell the airplane to wait until it is safe to fly. Once the controllers have given permission, the airplane will take off and fly to its destination. The controllers will continue to keep track of the airplane and make sure it stays on course and at the right altitude. When the airplane is getting close to its destination, the controllers will help it land by giving instructions to the pilots. They will tell the pilots when to turn, how high or low to fly, and when to line up with the runway. After the airplane has landed, the controllers will help it taxi to the gate and make sure it is safely parked. Air traffic control is a very important and complex job, but it helps to make sure that airplanes can fly safely and efficiently.
