In war, there are rules that are meant to protect civilians and ensure that soldiers are treated humanely. These rules are called the "laws of war" or "international humanitarian law." War crimes are actions that violate these laws. 
For example, it is a war crime to intentionally target civilians or to use weapons that cause unnecessary suffering. It is also a war crime to attack enemy soldiers who are surrendering or who are no longer able to fight, such as paratroopers who have landed and are no longer in the air. 
The purpose of these rules is to try to limit the harm and suffering caused by war, and to ensure that people are treated with dignity and respect, even in the midst of a conflict.
