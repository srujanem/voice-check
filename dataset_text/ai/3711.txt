Great question! The human eye is actually really good at seeing in low light because it has something called rods and cones. Rods are special cells in your eye that help you see things in low light, and cones are special cells that help you see colors. When it's really dark, your eyes will use mostly rods to see because they are better at picking up faint light. Cameras, on the other hand, don't have rods and cones like your eyes do. They have sensors that detect light, but they aren't as good at picking up faint light as your rods are. That's why cameras often produce grainy or blurry images in low light, while your eyes can still see fairly well. Another reason your eyes might be better at seeing in low light is because they can adjust to different levels of light much faster than a camera. When you walk into a room that's really dark, your eyes will automatically start to adjust and let in more light so you can see better. Cameras take a little longer to adjust to different light levels, so they might not be able to pick up as much detail in low light as your eyes can. I hope that helps!
