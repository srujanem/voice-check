Burns blister because the heat or chemicals that caused the burn also damaged the layers of skin and tissues underneath. When this happens, the body's natural response is to produce fluid and send it to the affected area to help protect the wound and begin the healing process. This fluid collects under the top layer of skin and forms a blister.\n\nBurn wounds can remain warm long after the injury occurred because the body is still working to heal the wound. The blood vessels in the area may be dilated, which can cause increased blood flow and warmth. In addition, the body's immune system may be working to fight off any infection that could occur in the wound.\n\nIt's important to keep burn wounds clean and protected to prevent infection and help the healing process. If the burn is severe or covers a large area of the body, it's important to seek medical attention as soon as possible.
