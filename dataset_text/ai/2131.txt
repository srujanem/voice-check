The Pope, bishops, and the Vatican are all part of the Catholic Church, which is a Christian denomination that traces its roots back to Jesus and his apostles. According to the Catholic Church, Jesus founded the Church and chose Peter, one of his apostles, to be the leader of the Church. Peter is considered the first Pope, or the Bishop of Rome, and the Catholic Church believes that the Pope has a special role as the successor of Peter and the leader of the Church. Over time, the Church grew and spread to different parts of the world. Bishops were appointed to lead local communities of believers, and they answered to the Pope as the head of the Church. The Vatican is the headquarters of the Catholic Church and is located in Rome, Italy. It is the home of the Pope and the central governing body of the Church. So, in short, the Pope, bishops, and the Vatican are all part of the Catholic Church, which was founded by Jesus and has a long history dating back almost 2,000 years.
