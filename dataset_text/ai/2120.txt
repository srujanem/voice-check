Sure! Short selling is a way for investors to make money when the price of a stock or other asset is falling. Here's how it works: Imagine that you think the price of a certain stock is going to go down. You can "short sell" that stock by borrowing shares of it from someone else, and then selling those shares on the market. If the price of the stock does go down, you can then buy the shares back at the lower price and return them to the person you borrowed them from. The difference between the higher price you sold the shares for and the lower price you bought them back for is your profit. For example, let's say that you think the price of XYZ stock is going to go down. You borrow 100 shares of XYZ stock from someone and sell them on the market for $50 each, for a total of $5,000. A few weeks later, the price of XYZ stock has fallen to $40 per share. You buy 100 shares of XYZ stock back for a total of $4,000 and return them to the person you borrowed them from. Your profit is $1,000, which is the difference between the $5,000 you got from selling the shares and the $4,000 you paid to buy them back. Short selling can be risky, because if the price of the stock goes up instead of down, you could end up losing money. That's why it's important to be careful when deciding to short sell a stock.
