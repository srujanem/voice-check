Hair on different parts of the body grows at different rates and stops growing at different lengths because of genetics, hormones, and other factors. Hair on the head and facial hair tends to grow longer because the hair follicles in these areas are programmed to produce longer, thicker hair. On the other hand, hair in certain areas like the armpits and pubic region tends to be finer and grows to a shorter length because the hair follicles in these areas are programmed to produce shorter, finer hair. As for animal fur, it also grows at different rates and lengths depending on the animal and the location of the fur on its body. For example, a lion's mane may grow longer than the fur on its legs because the hair follicles in the mane area are programmed to produce longer, thicker fur. Similarly, the fur on a cat's belly may be shorter and finer than the fur on its back because the hair follicles in these areas are programmed to produce different types of fur.
