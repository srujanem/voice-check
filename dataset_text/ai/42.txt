Coral reefs are among the most biologically diverse ecosystems on Earth, often referred to as the rainforests of the sea. Built by tiny organisms called coral polyps, these structures provide food, shelter, and breeding grounds for nearly a quarter of all marine life. Beyond supporting ocean biodiversity, reefs protect coastal communities from storm surges, reduce erosion, and drive local tourism economies worldwide. However, rising sea temperatures and ocean acidification threaten their survival through widespread coral bleaching, making active reef conservation vital for global ocean health.
