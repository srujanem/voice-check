Pension schemes, whether they are private or run by the government, are designed to pay out to people when they retire and are no longer working. They do this by collecting money from people while they are working and saving it up so that it can be used to pay for their retirement later on.\n\nOne way that pension schemes can deal with the "demographic time bomb" (meaning the fact that there will be fewer working age adults to pay for the pensions of retired people in the future) is by investing the money that they collect from people while they are working. This can help the money grow over time, so that there is more available to pay out when people retire.\n\nAnother way that pension schemes can deal with the demographic time bomb is by making sure that they have enough money saved up to pay for all the pensions that they have promised to pay. This can be a challenge, especially if a lot of people are retiring at the same time, but pension schemes can use a variety of strategies to try to make sure that they have enough money to pay all the pensions they have promised.\n\nIt is important to remember that pension schemes are not guaranteed to pay out a certain amount of money, and the amount of money that people receive from their pension can vary depending on a number of factors, including how much they paid into the pension while they were working and how well the pension scheme's investments have performed.
