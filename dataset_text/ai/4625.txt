Oil is used to lubricate the parts in an engine to reduce friction and wear. Different oils have different viscosities, or thicknesses. Thinner oils can flow more easily and can be used in engines that operate at high temperatures and high speeds, like those in some cars. Thicker oils are better for engines that operate at lower temperatures and speeds, like those in some trucks.\n\nThe numbers on the bottles of oil, like 5w-20 or 10w-20, refer to the viscosity of the oil at different temperatures. The first number, like 5 or 10, refers to the oil's viscosity when it is cold. The "w" stands for "winter," and it means that the oil is tested at a low temperature to see how well it flows. The second number, like 20 or 50, refers to the oil's viscosity at a higher temperature, like when the engine is running.\n\nDifferent engines need different viscosities of oil because they operate at different temperatures and speeds. For example, a car engine that runs at high speeds and high temperatures might need a thinner oil to flow easily and reduce friction. A truck engine that operates at lower speeds and temperatures might need a thicker oil to provide better protection and lubrication.
