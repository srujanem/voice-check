The United States Postal Service (USPS) is a government agency that delivers mail and packages to addresses in the United States. It is funded by the sale of postage and other products, but it does not receive tax dollars for its operating expenses. There are several reasons why the USPS has been losing money in recent years. One reason is that it has been facing increasing competition from private delivery companies like FedEx and UPS, which have been able to offer faster and more reliable service. In addition, the USPS has been facing declining mail volume due to the increasing use of email and online bill pay, which has reduced the number of letters and packages it delivers. Another reason for the USPS's financial difficulties is that it has a large and aging infrastructure, with many old buildings and vehicles that need to be maintained or replaced. This can be expensive, and the USPS has had difficulty keeping up with these costs. Finally, the USPS has also been struggling with financial challenges due to the COVID-19 pandemic, which has reduced the number of people sending mail and packages and has increased the cost of personal protective equipment for its employees. Overall, the USPS is facing a combination of economic, technological, and operational challenges that have made it difficult for the agency to break even and maintain financial stability.
