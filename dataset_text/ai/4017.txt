The process of determining the cause of a wildfire is called "fire investigation" and it is done by specially trained experts called "fire investigators." Fire investigators use a variety of techniques to determine the cause of a wildfire, including looking for the "point of origin" (where the fire started), analyzing the burn patterns, and speaking to witnesses. For example, if a wildfire was started by a campfire, the fire investigator might be able to find the campfire site and see that the fire spread from there. They might also look for things like campsite equipment or other evidence that someone had been camping in the area. If the wildfire was started by a dropped cigarette, the fire investigator might look for evidence of smoking, such as cigarette butts, and they might also speak to any witnesses who saw someone smoking near the area where the fire started. Even if the wildfire burned for a long time and destroyed a lot of evidence, the fire investigator might still be able to find enough clues to figure out what caused the fire. It can be a difficult and time-consuming process, but fire investigators are trained to be very thorough and to use all of the tools at their disposal to determine the cause of a wildfire.
