Prehistoric animals, also known as dinosaurs and other ancient creatures, were often much larger than animals we see today. There are a few different reasons why this might have been the case. One reason is that the Earth's atmosphere was different in the past. During the time when dinosaurs lived, the air contained much more oxygen than it does today. This extra oxygen may have helped dinosaurs and other prehistoric animals to grow larger, as it would have made it easier for them to breathe and get the energy they needed. Another reason is that there were no large predators to hunt these animals, so they didn't need to be as fast or agile. Without the need to constantly be on the lookout for danger, these animals were able to grow larger and more robust. Finally, some scientists believe that the Earth's climate was warmer in the past, which could have allowed prehistoric animals to grow larger. Warmer temperatures would have meant more food was available, which would have provided these animals with the resources they needed to grow and thrive. Overall, there are a lot of different factors that may have contributed to the large size of prehistoric animals, including the Earth's atmosphere, the lack of large predators, and the warmer climate.
