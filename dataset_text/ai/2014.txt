Economic profit is the profit that a business owner earns after taking into account all of the costs of running the business, including both explicit costs (like the cost of raw materials and labor) and implicit costs (like the opportunity cost of using your own money to start the business). If a business owner earns an economic profit, it means that their revenue is greater than all of their costs, so they are making money. However, it's important to note that it's not always necessary for a business owner to earn an economic profit in order to make money. For example, if a business owner's revenue is enough to cover all of their costs, they are said to be earning a "normal" or "zero" economic profit. While they may not be earning an economic profit, they are still able to pay themselves a salary and make a living from running the business. In some cases, a business owner may even choose to operate their business at a loss (i.e. with negative economic profit) in the short term in order to invest in the business and position it for future growth. For example, a business owner may choose to invest in marketing and advertising in order to attract more customers, even if this means operating at a loss in the short term. In the long term, this investment could lead to increased revenue and profits for the business. Overall, it's important for a business owner to carefully consider all of the costs of running their business and make strategic decisions about how to balance their short-term and long-term goals.
