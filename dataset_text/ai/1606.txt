The price of a work of art, such as a painting, is not necessarily determined by its visual appearance alone. There are many factors that can contribute to the value of a painting, including the artist who created it, the historical context in which it was made, and the rarity of the work. 
In the case of the painting "When Will You Marry?", it was created by the French artist Paul Cézanne, who is widely considered to be one of the most important figures in the history of modern art. His work has had a major influence on the development of many artistic movements, including cubism, and his paintings are highly sought after by collectors. 
The rarity of a work of art can also contribute to its value. If a painting is unique or one of a very small number of works by the artist, it may be considered more valuable than other paintings. In addition, the condition of the painting can also affect its value. If a painting is in good condition and has been well-preserved over time, it may be more valuable than a similar painting that is in poor condition. 
So, while the appearance of a painting may be a factor in determining its value, it is not the only factor. The value of a work of art is often based on a combination of its aesthetic appeal, the reputation of the artist, and its rarity and condition.
