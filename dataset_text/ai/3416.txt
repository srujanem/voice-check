Washing machines have different temperature settings so you can choose the right one for your clothes. Here's what each temperature does: Cold/Cold: This setting uses cold water for both the wash and rinse cycles. It's good for clothes that are very delicate or have bright colors that might fade if they get too hot. Cold/Warm: This setting uses cold water for the wash cycle and warm water for the rinse cycle. It's good for clothes that are not very dirty and don't need a lot of hot water to get clean. Warm/Warm: This setting uses warm water for both the wash and rinse cycles. It's good for clothes that are not too dirty, but need a little bit more heat to get clean. Warm/Hot: This setting uses warm water for the wash cycle and hot water for the rinse cycle. It's good for clothes that are very dirty or have stains that need to be cleaned with hot water. Hot/Hot: This setting uses hot water for both the wash and rinse cycles. It's good for clothes that are very dirty or have stains that need to be cleaned with hot water. As a general rule, you should use cold water for delicate fabrics like silk and rayon, and hot water for heavily soiled or greasy clothes like work uniforms or towels. Warm water is good for most other clothes. You can also check the care label on your clothes to see what temperature the manufacturer recommends.
