Instant runoff voting, also known as ranked choice voting, is a method of conducting elections in which voters rank their preferences for candidates rather than choosing just one candidate. In the United States, the voting system is determined by each individual state, so each state would need to decide whether to adopt instant runoff voting and how to implement it. Some states have already adopted instant runoff voting, while others have not. To change the voting system in a state, the state legislature (which is made up of elected representatives) would need to pass a law to adopt instant runoff voting. In some states, the law might also need to be approved by the governor (the head of the state government) or by voters through a referendum (a vote by the people). To change the voting system at the national level, a law would need to be passed by the United States Congress (which is made up of elected representatives from all 50 states) and signed into law by the President. In this case, a constitutional amendment (a change to the U.S. Constitution) might also be required, depending on the specific provisions of the law. Overall, changing the voting system in the United States involves a process of passing laws and possibly amending the Constitution. This process can be complex and may require the support of a large number of elected officials and voters.
