GPS stands for Global Positioning System. It is a system that uses satellites orbiting the Earth to help determine the location of something on the Earth's surface. To understand how GPS works, it is helpful to know a bit about time. Time is measured in seconds, and it is always ticking away. The time on your watch or phone is based on the time kept by very accurate clocks. Now, imagine that you have a clock on a satellite orbiting the Earth. Because the satellite is moving very fast, time on the satellite's clock will run slightly slower than time on a clock on the ground. This is due to the effect of gravity on time, known as "time dilation." GPS uses this effect to help determine the location of something on the ground. The satellite sends a signal down to the ground, and the GPS receiver (such as a GPS in a car or on a phone) receives the signal. The GPS receiver also has a clock, so it can measure the time it takes for the signal to reach it. Because the signal has to travel through the atmosphere and through the Earth's gravity field, it takes a tiny bit longer to reach the GPS receiver than it would if the signal were just going straight from the satellite to the receiver. The GPS receiver can use this information, along with the known positions of the satellites, to calculate its own position on the Earth's surface. So, in a way, GPS works by using the fact that time moves at different speeds in different places, due to the effects of gravity. This allows the GPS receiver to determine its location on the Earth's surface.
