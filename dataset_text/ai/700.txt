It's important to note that not all protesters or people who are anti-government are considered terrorists. The term "terrorist" is typically used to describe individuals or groups who use violence, intimidation, or fear to achieve a political, ideological, or religious goal. This can include attacks on civilians or infrastructure, and can be motivated by a desire to create change or to disrupt the status quo. 
It's also important to understand that different governments and organizations may have different definitions of what constitutes terrorism, and may use the term to describe different groups or individuals. In some cases, the label of "terrorist" may be used to discredit or delegitimize the actions of a group or individual, even if their actions do not meet the legal definition of terrorism. 
Ultimately, the label of "terrorist" is a subjective and often controversial one, and whether or not a group or individual is considered a terrorist can depend on the context in which they are operating and the motivations and actions they are perceived to have. It's important to be thoughtful and critical when evaluating whether or not a label such as "terrorist" is appropriate, and to consider the perspectives and motivations of all parties involved.
