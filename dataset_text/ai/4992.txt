\nA candle burns because the heat of the flame melts the wax near the wick. The melted wax then moves up the wick and is drawn into the flame, where it is vaporized and burned. The heat of the flame also breaks down the molecules in the wax, causing them to react with oxygen from the air to create heat, light, and water vapor.\n\nThe reason a candle can burn for a long time is because the wax acts as a fuel source for the flame. As the wax burns, the heat of the flame melts more wax, which is drawn up the wick and into the flame. This process can continue for a while as long as there is enough wax to keep the flame going.\n\nSo, to sum it up, a candle burns because the heat of the flame melts the wax, which is drawn up the wick and into the flame. The wax then reacts with oxygen to create heat, light, and water vapor. As long as there is enough wax, the candle can continue to burn.
