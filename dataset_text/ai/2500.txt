Different countries have different electrical plugs because they use different types of electrical systems. An electrical system includes the type of voltage (the strength of the electrical current) that is used and the frequency (the number of times the current changes direction in a second) of the electrical current. The voltage and frequency of an electrical system are chosen based on the needs of the country and the equipment that will be used with it. In Europe, the standard voltage is 220-240 volts, and the standard frequency is 50 hertz (cycles per second). In the United States, the standard voltage is 120 volts, and the standard frequency is 60 hertz. The different voltage and frequency requirements mean that electrical plugs and outlets in different countries are not compatible with each other.
