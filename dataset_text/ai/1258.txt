Centuries-old buildings like Notre Dame and The Parthenon are able to look as good as new or remain standing even when they are decrepit because they were built using strong materials and construction techniques. These materials and techniques have stood the test of time and are able to withstand the wear and tear of the elements and the passage of time. 
However, over time, these buildings may start to show signs of deterioration, such as cracks in the walls, wear on the surfaces, or damage caused by natural disasters or human activity. When this happens, people may decide to restore the building to bring it back to its original condition. 
During a restoration, workers may repair or replace damaged materials, clean and repair the surfaces, and make other necessary repairs to the building. In some cases, the original materials may be used to repair the building, but it is also common for modern materials to be used. This is because the original materials may no longer be available, or they may not be suitable for use due to their age or condition. 
So, while centuries-old buildings may be able to remain standing for a long time, they may need to be restored or repaired from time to time to keep them in good condition. The amount of original material that is left in the building after it has been restored will depend on how much of the original material was able to be preserved and how much needed to be replaced during the restoration process.
