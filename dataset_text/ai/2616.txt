Helium is a gas, so it can escape through small openings or holes in a balloon. When a balloon is filled with helium and tied off, the gas inside is under pressure, which makes it try to escape. The rubber or other material of the balloon stretches to contain the gas and keep it inside, but over time, the pressure of the gas can cause the balloon to stretch or thin out enough that the gas can leak through. As for why a balloon stops floating after a day or so, it's because the helium inside is gradually leaking out. When a balloon is filled with helium, it becomes lighter than the air around it, so it floats. However, as the helium leaks out and the balloon becomes heavier, it will eventually stop floating and fall to the ground. The rate at which a balloon loses helium and stops floating can depend on a variety of factors, including the size and type of balloon, the temperature and humidity, and whether the balloon is exposed to sharp objects or other factors that might cause it to burst or leak.
