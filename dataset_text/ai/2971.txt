Perpetual motion is the idea of a machine that can work indefinitely without any external energy input. It is impossible because it violates the first and second laws of thermodynamics. The first law of thermodynamics is the law of conservation of energy. It says that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only converted from one form to another. This means that a perpetual motion machine would have to create energy out of nothing, which is not possible. The second law of thermodynamics is the law of increasing entropy. Entropy is a measure of the disorder or randomness of a system. The second law says that the entropy of a closed system (one that is not receiving energy from the outside) will always increase over time. This means that the energy in a closed system will become less organized and less useful over time. So, a perpetual motion machine would have to break both of these laws of thermodynamics in order to work. Since these laws are fundamental principles of the universe, perpetual motion is impossible.
