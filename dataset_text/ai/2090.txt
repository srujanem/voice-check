To understand this topic fully, it is important to consider the key concepts involved. Biodiversity refers to the variety of life on Earth at all levels, from genes to ecosystems. It encompasses the estimated 8.7 million species of plants, animals, fungi, and microorganisms, along with the ecosystems they form. High biodiversity ensures ecosystem resilience, provides resources for medicine and agriculture, and maintains natural processes like pollination and water purification. Human activities — including habitat destruction, pollution, invasive species, and climate change — are driving the sixth mass extinction, with species disappearing at rates far exceeding natural background levels. It is also important to consider the broader societal and environmental implications associated with biodiversity.
