Manufacturers often put stickers on their products that say that the warranty will be void if the sticker is removed. These stickers are usually put on products to let people know that the product has not been tampered with or opened by someone other than the manufacturer or a authorized repair technician. The stickers are meant to protect the manufacturer's interests by ensuring that any damage or problems with the product are not the result of someone other than the manufacturer or a authorized repair technician opening or tampering with the product. 
Imagine that you bought a brand new phone, and a few weeks later it stopped working. You take it back to the store where you bought it and ask for a replacement or a refund, but the store tells you that the warranty is void because the sticker on the back of the phone has been removed. This might seem unfair, but the manufacturer is trying to protect itself from people who might try to claim that the product was defective when it was actually damaged or tampered with by someone else. 
So, in short, manufacturers use these stickers to help protect their interests and ensure that they are not held responsible for any problems with the product that were not caused by them or an authorized repair technician.
