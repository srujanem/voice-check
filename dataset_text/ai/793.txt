Reservoir dams are usually curved towards the lake because it helps to increase their stability. When a dam is built, it is subjected to a variety of forces, including the weight of the water in the reservoir, the pressure of the water against the dam, and the forces of the earth and rocks on either side of the dam. 
If a dam is straight, these forces can cause it to bend or even break. But if the dam is curved, the forces are distributed more evenly along the length of the dam, which makes it stronger and more stable. 
Imagine holding a piece of paper straight up in front of you. If you push down on the top of the paper, it will bend easily. But if you curve the paper slightly, it will be much stronger and harder to bend. It's the same principle with reservoir dams - the curve helps to distribute the forces more evenly, which makes the dam stronger and more stable.
