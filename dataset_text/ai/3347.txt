The dew point is the temperature at which air becomes saturated with water vapor and dew (tiny water droplets) forms. When air is cooled, it can hold less water vapor. So if the air is cooled enough, the excess water vapor will condense into dew. The dew point is measured in degrees because it is the temperature at which this condensation occurs. Just like temperature, the dew point is measured on a scale (in this case, the Celsius or Fahrenheit scale). Imagine you have a glass of ice water on a hot day. The air around the glass is hot and humid, which means it has a lot of water vapor in it. As the air touches the cold glass, it cools down. When the air gets cold enough, it can't hold onto all of the water vapor it had before. Some of the water vapor turns into tiny droplets of water on the outside of the glass. This is just like how dew forms on grass and other surfaces when the air is cooled to the dew point.
