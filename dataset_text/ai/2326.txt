Life insurance is a type of insurance that pays a benefit to your designated beneficiaries (such as your spouse or children) in the event of your death. It can help provide financial security and peace of mind to your loved ones in the event that you are no longer able to provide for them. Savings accounts are a type of financial product that allow you to save and earn interest on your money. While savings accounts can be a good way to set aside money for the future, they do not provide the same level of financial protection as life insurance. There are two main types of life insurance: term life insurance and permanent life insurance. Term life insurance is a type of insurance that provides coverage for a specific period of time, such as 10, 20, or 30 years. If you die during the term of the policy, your beneficiaries will receive the death benefit. If you outlive the term of the policy, the coverage will expire and you will not receive any benefit. Term life insurance is generally less expensive than permanent life insurance, but it does not build cash value over time. Permanent life insurance is a type of insurance that provides coverage for your entire life, as long as you continue to pay the premiums. Permanent life insurance policies also have a savings component, which allows you to build cash value over time. This cash value can be used to pay premiums or can be withdrawn or borrowed against. Permanent life insurance is generally more expensive than term life insurance, but it provides lifelong coverage and the ability to build cash value.
