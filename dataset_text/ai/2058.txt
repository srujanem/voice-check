Eggs are made up of proteins, which are long chains of amino acids. When you heat an egg, the proteins start to unwind and then re-form new bonds with each other. This process is called denaturation, and it causes the egg to change from a liquid to a solid. Most liquids, like water, don't have proteins in them, so they don't have the same kind of structure that eggs do. When you heat up water, it doesn't have anything to bond with, so it just turns into steam or vapor. But when you heat up an egg, the proteins can bond together and create a solid structure. That's why eggs are the only liquid that turns into a solid when you heat it up.
