Ayn Rand's philosophy, which she called "Objectivism," is based on the idea that reason, individualism, and capitalism are the most important values. According to Rand, reason is the most important human faculty because it allows us to think and make sense of the world. Individualism is the idea that each person is an individual and should be free to pursue their own happiness and goals, as long as they do not harm others. Capitalism is an economic system in which people are free to own businesses and produce and sell goods and services without interference from the government. Rand believed that the only way for people to truly be happy and fulfilled is to act on their own rational self-interest, and that this is best achieved in a capitalist society. She also believed that the government should have a very limited role, and should only be responsible for protecting the individual rights of its citizens. One of the main ideas in Objectivism is the concept of "objective reality." This means that there are facts about the world that are true and can be known through reason and observation, and that these facts are independent of people's feelings, desires, or beliefs. According to Rand, accepting objective reality and acting on it is the key to living a good life. Overall, Rand's philosophy emphasizes individual freedom, reason, and capitalism as the key ingredients for a happy and successful society.
