Optical illusions happen when our brains are tricked by the way something looks. For example, if you look at a picture of a spiral that is moving, your brain might think that it is actually moving even though it is just a static image. This is because our brains use clues from the environment to help us understand what we are seeing. When these clues are misleading or confusing, it can cause our brains to interpret the image in a way that doesn't match what is really happening. Another example of an optical illusion is when you look at a picture of a square that has been divided into smaller squares, and some of them are different shades of gray. Your brain might think that the squares are moving or changing color even though they are not. This is because our brains are trying to make sense of the image by grouping the different shades of gray together and treating them as one solid color. Overall, optical illusions are a fun way to see how our brains work and how they can be tricked by what we see.
