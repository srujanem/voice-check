Sports arenas in the US are often funded by taxpayers, but that does not mean that people do not have to pay to watch games or events in those arenas or on TV. The cost of building and maintaining sports arenas is typically paid for through a combination of public funds and private investment. While taxpayers may contribute to the construction and upkeep of the arenas, they are still operated as businesses, and the teams and organizations that use the arenas generate revenue by charging people to attend games or events and through television contracts. 
The NFL (National Football League) is a professional sports league in the US that consists of 32 teams. The NFL generates revenue through various sources, including ticket sales, merchandise sales, and television contracts. The NFL Network is a television network that is owned and operated by the NFL, and it broadcasts live NFL games and other programming related to the league. The NFL Network is able to exist because it is able to generate revenue through subscriptions and advertising. 
While it might seem unfair that people have to pay to watch sports games or events on TV or in person, it is important to remember that professional sports teams and leagues are businesses that need to generate revenue to cover their expenses, including player salaries, arena maintenance and operations, and other costs.
