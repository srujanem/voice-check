The Monty Hall Problem is a probability puzzle that is based on a television game show called "Let's Make a Deal." In the game show, a contestant is presented with three doors and is told that behind one of the doors is a prize. The contestant is asked to choose a door, and then the host of the game show, Monty Hall, reveals one of the doors that does not have the prize behind it. The contestant is then given the option to stick with their original choice or to switch to the other unopened door. The question is, should the contestant switch doors?, The answer is that the contestant should switch doors. Here's why: Imagine that you are the contestant and you choose Door 1. The probability that the prize is behind Door 1 is 1/3, because there are three doors and one of them has the prize. After Monty Hall reveals one of the doors that does not have the prize, the probability that the prize is behind Door 1 remains 1/3, because the revelation of a door without the prize does not change the fact that one of the three doors has the prize. However, the probability that the prize is behind Door 2 or Door 3 is now 2/3, because there are two doors left and one of them has the prize. Therefore, switching to Door 2 or Door 3 gives you a better chance of winning the prize.
