The Kony 2012 viral video, which was made by the organization Invisible Children, sought to raise awareness about Joseph Kony, a notorious warlord in Uganda who was accused of abducting and forcibly conscripting thousands of children into his rebel group, the Lord's Resistance Army (LRA). The video, which was released in 2012, aimed to make Kony "famous" in order to put pressure on governments and international organizations to take action against him and bring him to justice. While the video and the campaign it was part of had good intentions, it was met with a lot of criticism and controversy. Some people pointed out that the video oversimplified the conflict in Uganda and didn't accurately represent the situation on the ground. Others criticized the organization behind the video, Invisible Children, for its finances and how it used its funds. Additionally, some people were concerned that the campaign focused too much on Kony and not enough on the broader issues affecting Uganda, such as poverty and corruption. Some people also pointed out that Kony had not actually been active in Uganda for several years and that the LRA had significantly diminished in size and power, making the campaign feel out of touch and not very effective. Overall, the controversy surrounding the Kony 2012 video and campaign highlights the importance of being informed about the issues we care about and being aware of the potential limitations and drawbacks of advocacy efforts.
