Socratic paradox is a statement made by the ancient Greek philosopher Socrates, who is known for his method of questioning and dialogue to arrive at truth. When he said "I know that I know nothing," he was pointing out that he did not believe he had any certain knowledge or wisdom. This might seem strange, but Socrates believed that the only way to truly understand something was to question it and explore it through dialogue. By constantly questioning and examining his own beliefs and assumptions, he came to realize that he did not have all the answers and that there were many things he did not know. So, in a way, Socrates was saying that he was always learning and seeking to understand more, and that he did not think he had reached a point where he had all the knowledge he needed. He believed that true wisdom comes from constantly questioning and seeking to understand the world around us, rather than simply accepting what we are told or assuming that we already know everything.
