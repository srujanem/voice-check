When you get a shot, the needle is used to inject a small amount of medicine into your body. The needle needs to be big enough to be able to hold the medicine, but it's also small enough to not cause a lot of damage to your skin. When the needle punctures your skin, it can cause a small wound. Your body's natural response to a wound is to send cells to the area to help repair the damage. These cells work to close the wound and create new skin to cover it. As the skin heals, it may form a scar. Scars are made up of collagen, which is a type of protein that helps to repair and strengthen the skin. Scars are usually raised and somewhat visible, and they can be a different color than the surrounding skin. Sometimes, the scar from a shot may be more noticeable because the needle was inserted at an angle or the skin was stretched when the shot was given. In these cases, the scar may be more visible than it would be if the needle had been inserted straight into the skin. Overall, scars are a natural part of the healing process and they help to protect the body from further injury.
