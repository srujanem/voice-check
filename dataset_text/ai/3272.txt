Religious institutions are tax exempt in many countries because they provide certain services to their communities that are considered beneficial to society, such as education, charitable work, and support for the disadvantaged. This tax exempt status is usually granted by governments as a way to support and encourage these activities. It is not because "that's the way it's always been," but rather because governments have determined that it is in the public interest to support the work of these organizations. Some people may disagree with this policy and think that religious institutions should not be tax exempt, but changing it would be a complex and potentially controversial process that would require careful consideration and debate.
