When you eject a USB flash drive, you are telling your computer that you are done using the drive and it is safe to remove it. This is important because it ensures that any data that you are currently accessing or transferring from the drive is saved and stored properly before the drive is removed. 
If you don't properly eject the drive, there is a risk that data could be lost or corrupted. For example, if you were in the middle of transferring a file from the drive to your computer and you just unplugged the drive, the file might not be saved completely. This could cause errors or make the file unusable. 
By properly ejecting the drive, you are telling the computer to finish any ongoing tasks and make sure all the data is stored correctly before you remove the drive. This helps to protect your data and prevent problems with the drive or your computer.
