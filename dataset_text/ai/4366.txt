When you hold down a key on a piano, it causes a hammer to hit the strings inside the piano and produce a sound. The sound waves created by the vibrating strings travel through the air and reach your ears, allowing you to hear the note.\n\nHowever, the strings on a piano are not like the strings on a guitar or other stringed instrument, which stop vibrating as soon as you stop plucking them. Instead, the strings on a piano are held under tension and are able to vibrate for a longer period of time after they are struck by the hammer. This is why the sound of a piano note continues to be heard even after you stop pressing the key.\n\nThe length of time that a piano note will continue to be heard depends on a number of factors, including the type of piano, the quality of the strings, and the way the piano is played. In general, the sound of a piano note will gradually become softer and less distinct as the strings stop vibrating, until it can no longer be heard.
