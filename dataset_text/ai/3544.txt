Sheep are not extinct because they are regularly sheared, which means that their wool is trimmed off by people. This helps to keep the sheep comfortable and prevents their wool from getting too long and causing them problems. When a sheep's wool grows too long, it can start to weigh them down and make it difficult for them to move around. This can be especially dangerous for the sheep if it starts to roll over onto its back, because then it might not be able to get back up again. When a sheep is unable to move, it becomes very vulnerable to predators and could potentially starve to death if it can't get to food. To prevent this from happening, farmers and shearers (people who cut the wool off of sheep) will regularly trim the wool off of the sheep's body. This helps to keep the sheep healthy and comfortable, and allows them to move around freely. So even though sheep's wool never stops growing, they are not in danger of going extinct because people help to take care of them and make sure their wool doesn't get too long.
