In quantum physics, the act of observing something can change the outcome because the very act of observing can disturb the system being observed. This is known as the observer effect. The many worlds theory is a way of explaining this observer effect within the framework of quantum physics. According to the many worlds theory, every time there is a quantum event that has multiple possible outcomes, all possible outcomes actually happen, but in different parallel universes. So, when you observe something, you are essentially choosing which of the possible outcomes you want to see, and the outcome you see becomes reality in your own universe. Probability is related to the many worlds theory because it is used to describe the likelihood of different outcomes occurring. In quantum physics, probability is used to predict the likelihood of different outcomes occurring in a quantum event, based on the various parallel universes that exist.
