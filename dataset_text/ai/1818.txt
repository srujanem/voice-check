When you use a credit card to pay for something, the merchant has to pay a fee to the credit card company for processing the payment. This fee is called the "merchant discount fee" or the "interchange fee." It's a percentage of the total amount of the purchase, and it can range from about 1% to 3%. So, if you buy something for $100 and the merchant discount fee is 2%, the merchant has to pay $2 to the credit card company. In order to cover this cost, the merchant might choose to charge a higher price for the item if you pay with a credit card, so they can still make the same amount of profit they would have made if you had paid with cash. This is called a "surcharge" or a "credit card surcharge." It's an extra fee that merchants can charge if you use a credit card to pay for something. Some merchants choose to do this because they want to offset the cost of the merchant discount fee. There are some states in the US where surcharges are not allowed, so merchants in those states are not allowed to charge more if you use a credit card. In other states, merchants are allowed to charge surcharges, but they have to follow certain rules. For example, they might have to disclose the surcharge to customers before the sale, or they might have to limit the surcharge to a certain percentage of the total sale. Overall, it's important to know that you have the option to pay with cash or a credit card whenever you make a purchase. If you don't want to pay a surcharge, you can choose to pay with cash instead.
