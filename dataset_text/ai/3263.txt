Nikola Tesla was a scientist and inventor who lived in the late 1800s and early 1900s. He was interested in finding new ways to produce and use electricity, and he had an idea for a system that could transmit electrical power wirelessly over long distances. This idea was based on the concept of electromagnetic waves, which are a type of energy that can travel through the air without the need for a physical connection (like a wire). Tesla believed that it would be possible to use electromagnetic waves to transmit electricity from power plants (where electricity is produced) to homes and businesses (where it is used), without the need for wires. This would mean that people could use electricity without having to pay for the cost of building and maintaining power lines. In other words, Tesla's plan was to make electricity "free" in the sense that people wouldn't have to pay for the infrastructure needed to deliver it. Today, we do use electricity without having to pay for the infrastructure (like power lines) that delivers it to our homes and businesses. However, we still have to pay for the electricity itself, because it takes energy to produce electricity and that energy has a cost. So even if Tesla's idea had been successful, it would not have meant that electricity would be completely free. Instead, it would have just made it easier and cheaper for people to access electricity.
