One of the fundamental concepts in biology is that Cell division is the process by which a parent cell divides into two or more daughter cells. In eukaryotes, there are two distinct types of cell division: a vegetative division called mitosis, and a reproductive division called meiosis. Mitosis results in two genetically identical daughter cells, maintaining the diploid chromosome number. Meiosis involves two rounds of division, producing four haploid gametes with genetic variation due to crossing over and independent assortment. Proper regulation of the cell cycle is crucial to prevent uncontrolled proliferation, which characterizes cancer. These processes highlight the intricate complexity and efficiency of biological systems.
