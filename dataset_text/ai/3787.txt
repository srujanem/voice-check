A 401(k) is a type of retirement savings plan offered by many employers to their employees. It allows you to save and invest a portion of your pay before taxes are taken out. This means that you can reduce your taxable income and potentially pay less in taxes each year. The money that you contribute to your 401(k) is deducted from your paycheck and automatically invested in a variety of different financial assets, such as stocks, bonds, and mutual funds. Over time, the value of these investments can grow, which can help you build a larger nest egg for retirement. Your employer may offer a 401(k) plan as a benefit to help you save for retirement. Some employers may also match a portion of the contributions you make to your 401(k), which can be a great way to boost your savings. It's important to note that there are limits on how much you can contribute to a 401(k) each year, and you may have to pay taxes and other penalties if you withdraw money from your 401(k) before you reach a certain age (typically 59 1/2). However, a 401(k) can be a valuable tool for helping you save for the future and plan for your retirement.
