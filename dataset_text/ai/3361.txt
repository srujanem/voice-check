Speed limits are enforced by aircraft by using specialized cameras and radar equipment mounted on planes that fly over the roads. The aircraft use these cameras and radar to measure the speed of the vehicles on the road. If a vehicle is going faster than the speed limit, the aircraft can take a picture of the vehicle and its license plate. The picture is then sent to the police, who can use it to issue a ticket to the driver of the vehicle. So, if you see a sign that says "Speed limit enforced by aircraft," it means that there are planes flying overhead that are watching your speed and making sure you don't go too fast. If you do go too fast, you might get a ticket in the mail. It's important to always follow the speed limit to keep yourself and others safe on the road.
