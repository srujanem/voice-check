Birthmarks are patches of skin that are different in color or texture from the skin around them. They can be any shape or size, and can appear anywhere on the body. There are several different types of birthmarks, but most are caused by a mixture of genetics and environmental factors. Some birthmarks are present at birth, while others may appear later in life. There are two main types of birthmarks: vascular birthmarks and pigmented birthmarks. Vascular birthmarks are caused by abnormal blood vessels in the skin. They can be red, pink, or purple in color. Examples of vascular birthmarks include hemangiomas (bright red birthmarks) and port-wine stains (dark red or purple birthmarks). Pigmented birthmarks are caused by an excess of pigment in the skin. They can be any color, including brown, black, or tan. Examples of pigmented birthmarks include moles and café-au-lait spots (light brown birthmarks). There is no way to prevent or treat most birthmarks, but some can be treated with medications or surgery if they are causing problems or are cosmetically undesirable.
