Scars are a natural part of the healing process after your skin has been injured. When your skin is injured, your body starts to repair the damage by creating new collagen fibers. These collagen fibers help to rebuild the damaged tissue, but they don't always align in the same direction as the surrounding tissue. This can cause the scar to look different from the surrounding skin. 
Scars also don't go away because the skin cells at the bottom of the scar (called the dermis) are different from the skin cells at the top (called the epidermis). The dermis is made up of thicker, stronger cells that are more difficult to replace. The epidermis is made up of thinner, more delicate cells that are constantly being replaced as they die off. When you have a scar, the dermis remains unchanged, while the epidermis cells continue to regenerate on top of it. This is why scars don't disappear, even though the top layer of skin cells is constantly being replaced.
