Sure! Doppler shift is a change in the frequency of sound or light waves as the source of the waves and the observer are moving relative to each other. It's named after Austrian physicist Christian Doppler, who first described the effect in 1842. Imagine you're standing on the side of a road and a car is driving past you, honking its horn. As the car approaches, the sound waves it produces are bunched together, so the pitch (highness or lowness) of the horn sounds higher to you. As the car passes by and moves away from you, the sound waves spread out, so the pitch of the horn sounds lower. This is an example of the Doppler shift. The same thing happens with light waves. If a star is moving towards us, the light waves it emits are bunched together, so the star appears bluer (shorter wavelengths). If a star is moving away from us, the light waves it emits are spread out, so the star appears redder (longer wavelengths). Astronomers use the Doppler shift to measure the speed and direction of stars and galaxies. I hope that helps! Let me know if you have any more questions.
