When it's cold outside, your body tries to keep warm by constricting your blood vessels and reducing the flow of blood to your skin. This helps to conserve heat and keep your body temperature at a healthy level. However, when you're outside in the cold for a long time, your body can lose more heat than it can produce, and your body temperature can drop. When this happens, your muscles may start to shiver or shake in order to try to generate more heat. This is what causes your teeth to chatter. It's your body's way of trying to warm up and get back to a healthy temperature.
