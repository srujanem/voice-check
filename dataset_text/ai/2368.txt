The purpose of the list is to encourage people to work on these problems and to find solutions to them. One of the Millennium Prize Problems that has been solved is called the Poincaré Conjecture. This problem is about shapes called "manifolds." Manifolds are a kind of mathematical object that can be thought of as shapes that can be bent and twisted in different ways, kind of like how a piece of paper can be folded and unfolded. The Poincaré Conjecture is a problem about manifolds that are shaped like a ball. It asks whether any ball-shaped manifold is the same as a sphere. To understand this problem better, imagine you have a rubber ball. A sphere is a ball-shaped object that is perfectly smooth and has no bumps or dimples. Now, imagine you have a bunch of rubber bands wrapped around the ball. These rubber bands are like the folds and twists in a manifold. The Poincaré Conjecture asks whether it is possible to stretch and bend the rubber bands in such a way that the ball becomes a sphere again. If it is possible, then the ball-shaped manifold is the same as a sphere. If it is not possible, then the ball-shaped manifold is not the same as a sphere. In 2002, a mathematician named Grigori Perelman proved that the Poincaré Conjecture is true. This means that any ball-shaped manifold can be transformed into a sphere by stretching and bending it in the right way.
