Printing more money can't fix problems because it doesn't necessarily create more wealth or resources. When a government or central bank prints more money, it increases the supply of money in the economy. However, if the amount of goods and services produced in the economy does not increase at the same rate, then the value of each unit of currency, like a dollar, will go down. This is because there will be more dollars chasing the same amount of goods and services, which means that each dollar will be worth less because it can buy fewer goods and services. Think about it this way: imagine that there is a pie that represents all the goods and services produced in an economy. If there are more dollars in circulation but the size of the pie stays the same, then each person will only get a smaller slice of the pie, even if they have more dollars. On the other hand, if the size of the pie increases (i.e., if there is more production of goods and services), then each person can have a bigger slice of the pie, even if the number of dollars stays the same. It's also important to note that printing more money can lead to inflation, which is when the overall price level in an economy increases over time. This can be caused by an increase in the money supply, among other things. When prices go up, it means that the same amount of money will buy fewer goods and services, which can be a problem for people and businesses that rely on a fixed income or have fixed prices for their goods and services.
