There are a few reasons why the U.S government spends more on healthcare per person than other countries: Higher prices: In the U.S, the prices for healthcare services, such as doctor visits and prescription drugs, are generally higher than in other countries. This means that it costs more for people to access healthcare in the U.S than in other countries. More use of healthcare services: People in the U.S tend to use more healthcare services than people in other countries. For example, they may see a doctor more often or have more tests and procedures done. This also contributes to the higher spending on healthcare in the U.S. Lack of universal coverage: In the U.S, not everyone has healthcare coverage, either through an employer or through a government program like Medicaid. This means that some people have to pay for their healthcare out of pocket, which can be very expensive. In other countries with universal healthcare, everyone has coverage, which helps to spread the cost of healthcare across the entire population. Overall, the U.S spends more on healthcare per person because it has higher healthcare prices, people use more healthcare services, and not everyone has coverage to help pay for those services.
