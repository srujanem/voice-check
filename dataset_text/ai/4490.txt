When you open the door of a car on a hot day, the hot air coming out of the car looks "squiggly" or "wavy" because it is rising up off the pavement. This is because hot air is lighter than cool air, so it rises.\n\nImagine that you have a bowl of hot soup and a bowl of cold soup. If you try to blow on the hot soup, the steam from the soup will rise up into the air. This is because the steam is made of tiny droplets of hot water, which are lighter than the air around them. The steam will rise up into the air and make the air around it look "squiggly" or "wavy."\n\nThe same thing happens with the hot air coming out of your car. The hot air is lighter than the cool air around it, so it rises up off the pavement. This makes the hot air look "squiggly" or "wavy" as it rises up into the air.\n\nThis is something that only happens with hot air because hot air is lighter than cool air. Cool air is heavier than hot air, so it stays closer to the ground. This is why you don't see the same "squiggly" or "wavy" effect when you open the door of a car on a cool day.
