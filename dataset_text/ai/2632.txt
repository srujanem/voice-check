Cadavers, also known as human bodies that have been donated to science for research and education, are prepared in a specific way before they are used for dissection. First, the cadaver is carefully cleaned and disinfected to prevent the spread of any diseases. Next, the blood is drained from the body using a process called embalming. Embalming involves injecting a chemical solution into the veins of the cadaver to preserve the body and prevent decomposition. This solution helps to harden the tissues and prevent the body from breaking down. After the cadaver has been embalmed, it is typically refrigerated until it is needed for dissection. During dissection, the cadaver is carefully examined and studied by medical students or researchers. It is illegal to steal dead bodies and sell them for research or any other purpose. In most countries, the donation of a body to science must be done through a legal process, and the body must be treated with respect and care.
