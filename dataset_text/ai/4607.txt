Windchill makes you feel colder because it increases the rate at which heat is lost from your body. When the air is moving, it can more easily carry away the warm air that is close to your skin, which makes it easier for your body to lose heat. This is why you might feel colder on a windy day, even if the temperature is the same as it is on a calm day.\n\nImagine that you are standing outside on a cold day, and you are wearing a coat to keep you warm. If there is no wind, your coat will help to keep the warm air close to your body, and you will feel warm. But if there is a strong wind blowing, the wind can blow the warm air away from your body and make it easier for the cold air to reach your skin. This will make you feel colder, even if the temperature of the air is the same.\n\nSo, even though the temperature of the air might not change, the wind can still make you feel colder by increasing the rate at which heat is lost from your body.
