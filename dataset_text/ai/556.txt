Milk is more expensive than gas for a number of reasons. One reason is that the production of milk requires more resources and labor than the production of gasoline. For example, milk is produced on farms where cows must be raised, fed, and cared for, while gasoline is produced in refineries using crude oil as a raw material. 
Another reason is that the demand for milk is generally higher than the demand for gasoline. Milk is a staple food that is consumed by people all around the world, while gasoline is mostly used for transportation and is not as essential for daily life. As a result, the price of milk is able to be higher than the price of gasoline. 
Milk prices in the United States are regulated by the government in order to help ensure that farmers are able to make a living and that the supply of milk remains stable. The government sets the minimum price that milk must be sold for, which helps to protect farmers from being underpaid for their product. This regulation also helps to ensure that there is a consistent supply of milk available for consumers.
