Contactless cards use a technology called radio-frequency identification, or RFID, to communicate with the payment terminal. RFID uses radio waves to transmit information wirelessly between the card and the terminal.\n\nThe RFID chip in the card contains a small antenna that is used to send and receive the radio signals. When the card is placed near a payment terminal, the terminal sends a radio signal to the card, which activates the RFID chip. The chip then sends a unique code back to the terminal to identify the card and initiate the payment process.\n\nThe RFID chip in the card does not require a power source or battery because it is able to generate its own electricity using the radio waves it receives from the terminal. This is known as "passive" RFID technology, as the card is not actively sending or receiving signals, but rather responding to signals from the terminal.\n\nI hope this helps to explain how contactless cards work!
