The deep-sea anglerfish is one of the ocean's most peculiar and adapted predators, surviving in total darkness miles below the surface. Female anglerfish feature a specialized bioluminescent lure protruding from their heads, powered by light-emitting bacteria. Unsuspecting prey swim toward the glowing light directly into the fish's massive, needle-lined jaws. To conserve energy in the sparse abyssal environment, male anglerfish are significantly smaller and attach themselves permanently to females, fusing their circulatory systems together in an extreme example of evolutionary adaptation for deep-ocean survival.
