Radioactive decay is a process that occurs when an atomic nucleus becomes unstable and spontaneously changes into a different type of nucleus. This process is completely random, meaning that it is not possible to predict exactly when a specific atom will decay.\n\nImagine that you have a box full of marbles, and each marble represents an atom. Some of the marbles are painted red and some are painted blue. The red marbles represent atoms that are radioactive, and the blue marbles represent atoms that are not radioactive.\n\nNow, imagine that the box is shaken. The marbles will bounce around randomly and eventually come to rest. If you looked inside the box after it was shaken, you would see a random arrangement of red and blue marbles. You would not be able to predict exactly which marbles would be red and which ones would be blue.\n\nIt is the same with radioactive decay. The exact time when an atom will decay is completely random, just like the arrangement of the marbles in the box after it is shaken. Scientists can only make statistical predictions about the likelihood of an atom decaying over a certain period of time, based on the rate at which other atoms of the same type have decayed in the past.
