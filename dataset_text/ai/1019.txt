Occupying a territory means that a group of people or a country has control over a specific area of land. In a domestic war, occupation usually refers to one group of people gaining control over a piece of land that was previously controlled by another group of people. 
There are different ways that a group of people can occupy a territory. For example, they might use military force to take control of the land, or they might negotiate a peace agreement with the people who previously controlled the land and agree to take over control. 
There are no specific rules or guidelines for when a piece of land becomes the property of another group of people. Sometimes, the change in control is recognized by other countries and becomes official, while other times it is not recognized and there is still disagreement over who has the right to control the land. 
In general, it is important to respect the sovereignty and territorial integrity of other countries and not to try to take control of their land without their consent. This can help to prevent conflict and promote peace between different groups of people.
