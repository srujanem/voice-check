Wikipedia has several measures in place to protect its pages from being tampered with or vandalized by malicious users. Here are a few of the ways that Wikipedia protects itself: Registration: In order to make edits to Wikipedia, users must first create an account and log in. This helps to deter casual vandals, who may not want to take the time to register an account. Edit filters: Wikipedia has a system called "edit filters" that automatically checks for certain types of edits that are commonly made by vandals. If an edit is flagged by an edit filter, it will be automatically reviewed by a Wikipedia administrator before it is published. Page protection: If a page is frequently vandalized, Wikipedia administrators can "protect" the page, which means that only users with certain privileges (such as administrators) can make edits to it. This helps to prevent malicious edits from being made to important or high-traffic pages. Reverting: If a vandal does manage to make an edit to a Wikipedia page, other users can "revert" the edit, which means that they can undo the changes and restore the page to its previous version. This helps to keep Wikipedia accurate and reliable. Overall, Wikipedia has a number of tools and processes in place to help protect its pages from being vandalized, and the vast majority of edits made to Wikipedia are made by well-intentioned users who are trying to improve the encyclopedia.
