DRM stands for "Digital Rights Management." It is a way for companies to protect their digital content, like music, movies, and ebooks, from being copied or shared without permission. Imagine you bought a book from a store. You can read the book as many times as you want, but you can't make copies of the book and give them to your friends. DRM works in a similar way, but for digital content. So, if you buy a movie online and it has DRM, you can watch the movie as many times as you want, but you might not be able to download it or copy it and share it with your friends. This is to make sure that the people who made the movie get paid for their work. Some people don't like DRM because they feel like it limits what they can do with the content they paid for. For example, they might not be able to watch a movie they bought on a device that the company doesn't support. Other people think DRM is important because it helps protect the rights of the people who made the content.
