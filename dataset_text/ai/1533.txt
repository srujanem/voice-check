Intelligence quotient, or IQ, tests are designed to measure a person's cognitive abilities, or how well they think and reason. These tests are usually administered by psychologists or other trained professionals, and they typically involve tasks that measure a range of cognitive skills, such as verbal and mathematical reasoning, problem-solving, and spatial ability. 
IQ tests are generally considered to be reliable measures of cognitive ability, although they are not perfect. Like any test, they may have some limitations and may not accurately reflect a person's full range of abilities. Additionally, IQ scores can vary slightly from one testing session to another, so a person's IQ score may not be exactly the same each time they take the test. 
As far as their usefulness, IQ tests can be helpful in certain contexts, such as identifying students who may need extra help in school or identifying individuals who may be candidates for certain jobs that require high levels of cognitive ability. However, it's important to remember that IQ tests are just one tool among many, and they are not the sole determinant of a person's success or potential. Factors such as motivation, hard work, and social skills can also be important in predicting success, and they are not necessarily measured by IQ tests. 
It's also important to note that IQ tests are not designed to measure traits like violence or crime, and they should not be used to make judgments about a person's character or behavior. In fact, using IQ scores to make predictions or decisions about people can be problematic and may perpetuate harmful stereotypes or biases. It's important to approach IQ tests with caution and to use them responsibly, keeping in mind that they are just one tool among many for measuring cognitive ability.
