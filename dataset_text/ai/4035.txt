The National Security Agency (NSA) is a government agency, and like all government agencies, it is subject to laws and regulations. While the NSA is allowed to engage in certain activities that might be considered "breaking the law" in certain circumstances, it is also subject to oversight and accountability to ensure that it is operating within the bounds of the law. For example, the NSA is required to follow the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act (FISA), which sets out the rules for how it can gather intelligence on foreign individuals and organizations. If the NSA were to break the law or violate the Constitution, it could be held accountable through the legal system or through other means of oversight.
